## Split by the requested cycle length {steps: s2}

Let $G(A)$ have the elements of $A$ as vertices, with edges between distinct coprime elements. The requested length $\ell$ is odd and at least $3$, so writing it as $2k+1$ gives two cases, $\ell=3$ and $\ell\ge 5$.

## A dense set contains a triangle {steps: s3}

In the first case the upper bound $3\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1$ gives $n\ge 6$. Assume $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ has no three distinct pairwise coprime elements. The proof bounds its size by $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\lfloor n/3\rfloor-\lfloor n/6\rfloor$.

If $1\in A$, any coprime pair in $A\setminus\{1\}$ would form a triangle with $1$. Partition $\{2,\ldots,n\}$ into consecutive pairs $(2,3),(4,5),\ldots$, with a possible last singleton. Consecutive integers are coprime, so at most one member of each pair can be selected. Including $1$ gives $|A|\le 1+\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, which is within the required bound for $n\ge 6$.

If $1\notin A$, put $q=\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$. Each full block $\{6j+2,\ldots,6j+7\}$ for $0\le j<q$ contains at most four selected elements. To see this, any selection of at least five elements contains one of the triples with offsets $(3,4,5)$, $(3,4,7)$ or $(5,6,7)$ from $6j$. Each triple is pairwise coprime. A common divisor of two entries divides their difference. The differences are at most four, and the entries at distance two or four are odd. For the distance-three pair, $6j+4$ is not divisible by three. Induction over the full blocks now bounds their total by $4q$.

The remaining interval runs from $6q+2$ through $n$. Its length is enough to bound its contribution except when it has four or five elements. With four, choosing all of them includes $6q+3,6q+4,6q+5$, leaving a cap of three. With five, the full-block argument caps the selection at four. Adding the tail bound to $4q$ gives the stated floor bound in all six residue cases.

This contradicts the strict size hypothesis. The resulting three distinct pairwise coprime vertices form a simple cycle by traversing their three edges and returning to the first vertex.

## The remaining odd lengths {steps: h_long s4}

For odd $\ell$ with $5\le\ell\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor+1$, the assembly applies the separate statement with that same interval of lengths and the same density hypothesis. That statement remains open in node erdos-883--h1. The argument above supplies the triangle case.
