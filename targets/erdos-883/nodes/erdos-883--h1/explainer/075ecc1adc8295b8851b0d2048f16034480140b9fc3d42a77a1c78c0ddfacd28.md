## The ordering that remains to be constructed {steps: h_seq}

Take a set $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $|A|>\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\lfloor n/3\rfloor-\lfloor n/6\rfloor$ and an odd length $5\le \ell\le \lfloor n/3\rfloor+1$. The remaining obligation is to find distinct $a_0,\ldots,a_{\ell-1}$ in $A$ with $\gcd(a_i,a_{(i+1)\bmod\ell})=1$ for each $i$. In Lean, the ordering is a function from the vertices of the standard cycle graph. Injectivity gives distinct entries, and the coprimality condition applies to every edge, including the edge joining the last position to the first.

## An ordering gives a cycle in the coprime graph {steps: bridge}

For a given ordering $f$, send position $i$ to the vertex $f(i)$ of the graph on $A$. Membership of each $f(i)$ in $A$ makes the map well-defined. Adjacent positions are distinct and have distinct images because $f$ is injective. The images are coprime by hypothesis, so the map preserves edges. It is also injective as a map to vertices of $A$: equality there implies equality of the underlying integers.

This gives a copy of the standard cycle graph inside the coprime graph. Only the cycle edges have to be preserved, so extra edges between the selected vertices are allowed. The cycle containment theorem identifies such a copy, for $\ell>2$, with a simple closed walk of length $\ell$.

## Apply the bridge at the requested length {steps: s3 close}

Apply the ordering statement with the given $n$, $A$, and $\ell$. Since $\ell\ge5$, the bridge applies and yields a cycle of length $\ell$. Combining that existence statement with the assumed oddness places $\ell$ among the odd cycle lengths of the graph.
