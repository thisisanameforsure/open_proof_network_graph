## What the assembly shows

The sum $\sum_{p\text{ prime}}\frac{1}{2^p-1}$ is irrational, following Section 5 of Tao and Teräväinen (arXiv:2512.01739v2), provided their Theorem 5.1 holds in the form stated as the hole. The partial has one hole. As of 12 November 2026 it is open on the graph (it carries a partial of its own, with one open hole), so this node is not proved.

What is proved in the file, in private lemmas before the theorem: the Lambert identity $\sum_p\frac{1}{2^p-1}=\sum_n\omega(n)/2^n$; equation (5.1), that if the sum is $a/q$ then $q\sum_{h\ge1}\omega(N+h)/2^h$ is an integer for every $N$; equation (5.8), obtained from (5.1) by an alternating sum over a cube of primes $p_\varepsilon=p_0+\sum_k\varepsilon_kv_k$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^K$; the variance bound (5.20); and the comparison of $\mathbb{E}\,e(\sum_pX_p)$ with $\prod_p\mathbb{E}\,e(X_p)$ by Taylor expansion. Here $e(t)=e^{2\pi it}$ and $\mathbb{E}$ is the average over $n$ in a finite set.

## The hole: Theorem 5.1 {steps: h1}

For every $q\ge1$ there is $c_0>0$ such that for every $\delta>0$ there are: a cube of primes $p_\varepsilon$; shifts $r_{\varepsilon,h}$ as in (5.7); a finite nonempty set of integers $n$ obeying (5.5)–(5.6), with all $n+r_{\varepsilon,h+K}$, $1\le h\le H$, in $[1,B]$; and disjoint sets $S_1,S_2$ of primes up to $B$, such that the phases

$$X_p(n)=q\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_\varepsilon(-1)^{|\varepsilon|}\,\frac{\mathbf 1[\,p\mid n+r_{\varepsilon,h+K}\,]}{2^{h+K}}$$

are constant in $n$ for $p$ outside $S_1\cup S_2$, satisfy $|X_p|\le\delta$ on $S_1$, the five error quantities $\kappa_1,\dots,\kappa_5$ of (5.10), (5.11), (5.14), (5.16), (5.17) are at most $\delta$ for some even $M$, and $\sum_{p\in S_1}\operatorname{Var}X_p\ge c_0$.

## Setting up {steps: htay hq hsum s5 hex h_x3b4 h_x3b41 h_x3b42 s10 hq1 hd1 hd2 hsgn' hr' s16}

Suppose the sum is $a/q$ in lowest terms. Take $c_0$ from the hole for this $q$; then $e^{-8c_0}<1$. Put $\delta_0=\min\bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac{1-e^{-8c_0}}{64}\bigr)$ and apply the hole with $\delta=\delta_0/q$. Fix one $n_0$ in the set.

## The contradiction {steps: key s18}

A private lemma combines the three ingredients: if for every $n$ the number $\sum_{p\in S_1}X_p(n)+C_0+W(n)$ is an integer, $\mathbb{E}|W|\le\delta'$, $|\mathbb{E}\,e(\sum_{S_1}X_p)-\prod_{S_1}\mathbb{E}\,e(X_p)|\le\delta'$, $|X_p|\le\frac14$ on $S_1$ and $\sum_{S_1}\operatorname{Var}X_p\ge c_0$, then $1-8\delta'\le e^{-8c_0}$. It is applied with $\delta'=4\delta_0$; the third condition holds because the difference is at most $2\kappa_4+2\kappa_5$. But $1-32\delta_0\ge\frac{1+e^{-8c_0}}{2}>e^{-8c_0}$, a contradiction.

## The integer {steps: s19}

By (5.8), $q\sum_{h\ge1}\sum_\varepsilon(-1)^{|\varepsilon|}\omega(n+r_{\varepsilon,h+K})/2^{h+K}$ plus $q\sum_\varepsilon(-1)^{|\varepsilon|}\sum_{h\ge1}\mathbf 1[\,p_\varepsilon^2\mid n+r_{\varepsilon,h}\,]/2^h$ is an integer. Cut the sum at $H$: since each $n+r_{\varepsilon,h+K}$ lies in $[1,B]$, the truncated sum is $\sum_{p\le B}X_p(n)$. Split the primes into $S_1$, $S_2$ and the rest; on the rest $X_p(n)=X_p(n_0)$, and these give the constant $C_0$. Then $W(n)$ is the sum of the $p_\varepsilon^2$ terms, plus the part beyond $H$, plus $\sum_{S_2}X_p(n)$.

## The error is small on average {steps: s20}

The three parts of $W$ have average absolute value at most $q\kappa_1$, $q\kappa_2$ and (by Cauchy–Schwarz) $\kappa_3$, each at most $\delta_0$. So $\mathbb{E}|W|\le3\delta_0\le4\delta_0$.
