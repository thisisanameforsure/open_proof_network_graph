# Primes case (Erdős 69 = Erdős 257 for A = primes): a route map of Tao–Teräväinen's proof for a Lean partial

Source: T. Tao and J. Teräväinen, *Quantitative correlations and some problems on prime factors of consecutive integers*, arXiv:2512.01739v2, Theorem 1.3 (§1.3, p. 4), proved in §5 (pp. 44–56), using Theorem 3.1 (p. 24, proved in §3). Equation and page numbers below are the paper's. Written 2026-10-22 from the paper itself, read in full for §1.3, §3's statement and §5. Nothing here is new mathematics. The point is to say which steps are elementary, which are standard analytic number theory, and which are new, and for each, whether current Mathlib has its inputs. Mathlib names were checked with the network's fast check (`#check`, Lean 4.33.1 and the Mathlib nearest the erdos-257 pin), not from memory.

Notation (paper, §1.6 and (1.1)): ω(n) = Σ_p 1_{p|n}; log_2 x = log log x, log_3 x = log log log x, log_4 x = log log log log x; e(t) = exp(2πit). In Lean, ω is `ArithmeticFunction.cardDistinctFactors`.

What §5 uses: Theorem 3.1, Lemma 5.2, the definitions (1.1), (1.19) and (1.21), Mertens' theorem, the prime number theorem (only in Chebyshev strength), the Chinese remainder theorem and Selberg's sieve. Sections 2 and 4 (Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.5–1.7) are *not* used. §5 cites Section 2 only for the names "near / far / very far shifts" and "small / medium / large primes".

## The chain, in the paper's order

**S1. Lambert identity, (1.7).** Σ_p 1/(2^p − 1) = Σ_{n≥1} ω(n)/2^n. It follows by expanding each term as a geometric series and swapping the sums. *Elementary.* **Proved in Lean** (a helper, not a node): `lambert_identity` (any A ⊆ ℕ: Σ_{a∈A} 1/(2^a−1) = Σ_m #{d | m : d ∈ A}/2^m) and `lambert_primes`, in the run's evidence file `Q/tt_steps_S1_S4.lean` (network repository, `engineering/evidence/testers-2026-10-22-e257/`), which also holds S2, S3 and S4's cancellation. Fast check okay, axioms propext/choice/Quot.sound only. Mathlib inputs used: `summable_prod_of_nonneg`, `Summable.tsum_comm`, `Function.Injective.tsum_eq`, `tsum_geometric_of_lt_one`, `summable_pow_mul_geometric_of_norm_lt_one`, `Nat.card_divisors_le_self`. (Mathlib's `tsum_pow_div_one_sub_eq_tsum_sigma` covers only A = ℕ.)

**S2. Tails, (5.1).** If the sum is a/q, then q Σ_{h≥1} ω(n+h)/2^h ≡ 0 (mod 1) for every n ∈ ℕ: multiply by 2^n and subtract the integer Σ_{m≤n} ω(m) 2^{n−m}. *Elementary.* **Proved in Lean**: `eq_5_1` in the same file, built on agent L's `tail_mul_den_int`.

**S3. Dilation, (5.2)–(5.3).** Fix a prime p. Since ω(pm) = ω(m) + 1 − 1_{p|m}, we have, for p | n,
q Σ_{h≥1} ω(n+ph)/2^h ≡ −q δ_p(n) (mod 1), where δ_p(n) := Σ_{h≥1} 1_{p²|n+ph}/2^h.
Depends on S2 (applied at n/p) and Σ_h 2^{−h} = 1. *Elementary.* **Proved in Lean**: `omega_mul_prime` (ω(pk) + 1_{p|k} = ω(k) + 1) and `eq_5_2`. Inputs: `Nat.primeFactors_mul`, `Nat.Prime.primeFactors`, `ArithmeticFunction.cardDistinctFactors_apply`.

**S4. Cube and alternating sum, (5.4)–(5.8).** Take p_0 ∈ ℕ and v_1, …, v_K ∈ ℤ such that the numbers p_ε := p_0 + ε_1 v_1 + … + ε_K v_K are distinct primes for all ε ∈ {0,1}^K (5.4). Take n with n ≡ Σ_k k ε_k v_k (mod p_ε) for every ε (5.5), and with n > Σ_k k|v_k| (5.6). Apply S3 with p = p_ε at n − Σ_k k ε_k v_k. The shifts are
r_{ε,h} := p_ε h − Σ_k k ε_k v_k = p_0 h + Σ_k (h−k) ε_k v_k (5.7).
For 1 ≤ h ≤ K, r_{ε,h} does not depend on ε_h. So the alternating sum Σ_ε (−1)^{|ε|} ω(n + r_{ε,h}) vanishes for those h, and the result is
(5.8): q Σ_{h≥1} Σ_ε (−1)^{|ε|} ω(n + r_{ε,h+K}) / 2^{h+K} ≡ −q Σ_ε (−1)^{|ε|} δ_{p_ε}(n − Σ_k k ε_k v_k) (mod 1).
*Elementary* (CRT plus finite combinatorics). **The cancellation is proved in Lean**: `cubeShift_eq` is (5.7), and `cube_cancel` says that for 1 ≤ h ≤ K, Σ_ε (−1)^{|ε|} g(r_{ε,h}) = 0 for every g. Finding n is CRT: `Nat.chineseRemainderOfFinset` and `Ideal.quotientInfRingEquivPiQuotient` are present.

**S5. Exponentiate, average and truncate, (5.9)–(5.11).** Apply t ↦ e(t) and average over a random n obeying (5.5)–(5.6). This gives E e(q Σ_{h≥1} …) = 1 + O(κ_1), with κ_1 = Σ_ε Σ_h P(p_ε² | n + r_{ε,h}) / 2^h (5.10). Cutting at h ≤ H costs O(κ_2), with κ_2 = Σ_{h>H} Σ_ε E ω(n + r_{ε,h+K}) / 2^{h+K} (5.11). *Elementary* (e is 2π-Lipschitz). Mathlib: `Complex.exp`, `Complex.norm_exp_sub_one_le`.

**S6. Split the primes, (5.12)–(5.15).** By (1.1), the phase is Σ_p X_p, where X_p := q Σ_{1≤h≤H} Σ_ε (−1)^{|ε|} 1_{p | n + r_{ε,h+K}} / 2^{h+K} (5.13). Partition the primes as S_0 ⊎ S_1 ⊎ S_2, with X_p deterministic for p ∈ S_0. Taylor expansion and Cauchy–Schwarz then give E e(Σ_{p∈S_1} X_p) = 1 + O(κ_1 + κ_2 + κ_3), with κ_3 := (E (Σ_{p∈S_2} X_p)²)^{1/2} (5.14), (5.15). *Elementary.*

**S7. Variance bound, (5.16)–(5.20).** Compare with jointly independent copies X'_p, by Taylor expansion to an even order M: κ_4 (5.16). The bound e^t + e^{−t} ≥ t^M/M! gives κ_5 (5.17). This yields Π_{p∈S_1} E e(X_p) = 1 + O(Σ_{j≤5} κ_j). If X_p = o(1) on S_1 (5.19), then 1 − cos t ≍ t² gives Σ_{p∈S_1} Var X_p ≪ Σ_j κ_j (5.20). *Elementary probability on a finite uniform space.* Mathlib: `ProbabilityTheory.variance`, `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun`, `ProbabilityTheory.IndepFun.variance_add`, `Real.cos_le_one_sub_mul_cos_sq` (for |x| ≤ π), `Real.cos_bound`, `Real.add_one_le_exp`.

**S8. Theorem 5.1 (technical reduction, p. 49).** Along a sequence x → ∞, there exist K, H, M (M even), p_0, v_1, …, v_K with the p_ε distinct primes, a partition S_0, S_1, S_2 of the primes, and a random n obeying (5.5) and (5.6), such that:
- κ_1, …, κ_5 = o(1) (5.18);
- X_p is deterministic on S_0;
- Σ_{p∈S_1} Var X_p ≫ 1 (5.21).

S1–S7 together with Theorem 5.1 contradict rationality. Its proof is §5.5–§5.14:
- **S8a, parameters (5.22)–(5.29).** All are explicit iterated-log functions of x. As printed: K ≈ 2 log_4 x / log 2 (so 2^K ≈ (log_3 x)^2), H ≈ (log_3 x)^2, M = 2⌊log_2 x⌋, H_+ ≈ (log_2 x)^2, P = exp((log_3 x)^3), R = x^{1/(log_2 x)^2}, R_+ = x^{1/100}, with L in between. Read the floors and exponents from the PDF itself; text extraction drops the super- and subscripts. Every estimate below uses only the hierarchy (5.30).
- **S8b, Lemma 5.2 (p. 50).** There exist p_0 ≥ 1 and v_1, …, v_K such that all p_ε are distinct primes in [P/2, P], and all r_{ε,h+K} (ε ∈ {0,1}^K, 1 ≤ h ≤ H) are distinct. Proof: a Hilbert cube lemma via monotonicity of Gowers norms, ‖f‖_{U^K} ≥ ‖f‖_{U^1} ≫ 1/log P on ℤ/5Pℤ for the indicator of the primes in [P/2, P], plus a union bound. *Standard additive combinatorics.* Needs #{p ∈ [P/2, P]} ≫ P/log P, which is PNT or Chebyshev with constants sharper than log 4.
- **S8c, set-up (5.32)–(5.36).** Define the discriminant W, the prime classes (small: p | W or p = p_ε; medium: up to R; large: R < p ≤ R_+; very large) and S_0 = small, S_1 = medium, S_2 = the rest. Take n uniform on [x/2, x] subject to (5.5) and n ≡ 0 (mod W^♭) (5.33). The progression has spacing Q ≪ P^{2^K} W^♭ (5.34), by CRT and θ(P) ≪ P. Equidistribution (5.35) holds for every u-periodic f with u coprime to Q. Also X_p = O(2^{−K}) off S_0 (5.36). *Elementary, plus Chebyshev's upper bound.*
- **S8d, κ_1 (§5.7).** Elementary from (5.35).
- **S8e, κ_2 (§5.8).** Uses ω(n) ≪ log n, at most O(1) prime factors above R_+, (5.35) and Mertens' theorem Σ_{p≤y} 1/p = log log y + O(1). *Standard.*
- **S8f, κ_4 (§5.9).** Elementary from (5.35) and (5.36).
- **S8g, κ_5 (§5.10).** Taylor expansion, (5.37), (5.38) and Mertens. *Standard.*
- **S8h, variance (§5.11).** Var X_p ≍ 1/(2^K p), so by Mertens Σ_{S_1} Var X_p ≫ (log_2 R − log_2 W)/2^K, which gives (5.21). *Standard.*
- **S8i, κ_3 on large primes, (5.39) (§5.13).** Near-orthogonality of X_p X_{p'} from (5.35), with no collisions of residue classes, plus Mertens. *Standard.*
- **S8j, κ_3 on very large primes, (5.40) (§5.14).** This is where the paper's new input enters.
  - Reduce to (5.41), which is trivial, and to the decorrelation (5.42).
  - Write Σ_{R_+<p<2x} 1_{p|n} = Σ_ℓ (1 − g_ℓ(n) + O(1_{n∈E_ℓ})) (5.43). Here g_ℓ is completely multiplicative with g_ℓ(p) = 1_{p∉J_ℓ}, and the J_ℓ are L blocks of (R_+, 2x] of equal log log length. The exceptional sets E_ℓ are sparse by Mertens (5.44).
  - Then apply **Theorem 3.1(i)** with g_1 = g_ℓ and g_2 = g_ℓ' or 1. This requires the equidistribution hypothesis (3.1) for g_ℓ in progressions of modulus O(log^c x). That is proved by inclusion–exclusion to depth 101, plus Selberg's sieve: O(N/log N) integers ≤ 2N have no prime factor ≤ R_+. Condition (3.2) holds because J_ℓ ⊂ (R_+, 2x]. The exceptional set of Theorem 3.1 is why x runs along a subsequence.
  - *New input: Theorem 3.1. The rest is standard.*

**S9. Theorem 3.1 (quantitative correlation estimate, p. 24; proof in §3).** Let g_1, g_2 be 1-bounded multiplicative functions, 1 ≤ L ≤ log X, and in the equidistributed case (i) assume g_1 is real with Σ_{N<n≤2N, n≡a (q)} g_1(n) = (N/q) δ_N + O(N L^{−1}) (3.1), and (3.2). Then there is a set E ⊂ [√X, X] of logarithmic density ≪ L^{−c} such that (W/N) Σ_{N<n≤2N} (g_1(n+h_1) − δ_N) g_2(n+h_2) 1_{n≡b (W)} ≪ L^{−c} for all N ∈ [√X, X] \ E, all W ≤ L^c, and all distinct h_1, h_2 and b of size O(L^c) (3.4). This extends Pilatte's quantitative Chowla bound (arXiv ref. [42] of the paper) and uses Pilatte's decoupling inequality (Theorem 3.3). *This is the paper's new analytic content.*

## Which steps could be holes of an honest partial

A hole must be honestly smaller than the node and true. On that bar:

- **Good holes: self-contained published theorems, each strictly weaker than the node and reusable.**
  - (a) Lemma 5.2: a Hilbert cube of distinct primes in [P/2, P] with distinct shifts r_{ε,h+K}, for K, H as functions of P. A qualitative form also works: for all K, H there exist p_0, v with the p_ε distinct primes and the r_{ε,h+K} distinct.
  - (b) Mertens' second theorem.
  - (c) #{p ∈ [P/2, P]} ≫ P/log P.
  - (d) Theorem 3.1(i), stated with logarithmic density written out as an integral.
  - (e) The Selberg upper bound for integers ≤ 2N free of prime factors ≤ R_+.
- **The catch is the assembly.** A partial whose holes are only (a)–(e) must carry all of S3–S8 in Lean: the averaging over a CRT progression, every κ_j bound, and o(1) bookkeeping along x → ∞ under the hierarchy (5.30). That is roughly ten pages of estimates, far beyond one session.
- **A smaller honest partial.** Prove S1–S7 and leave Theorem 5.1 as the single hole, stated with explicit quantifiers: there is c > 0 such that for every q ≥ 1 and ε > 0 there are parameters and a finite set of n (the random variable is uniform on it) with κ_1 + … + κ_5 ≤ ε, |X_p| ≤ ε on S_1, X_p constant on S_0, and Σ_{S_1} Var X_p ≥ c. The hole no longer mentions the sum, rationality or tails. It is a statement about ω on consecutive shifts, and it is exactly the paper's analytic content, so it is smaller in the sense of D-12, not easier. The assembly (S3–S7 with explicit constants) is perhaps 400–800 lines of Lean. The constant in (5.20) must be made explicit to state the hole honestly, so the hole has to be written together with the assembly, not before it.
- **Not a hole.** "For every b there is N with b·τ_N ∉ ℤ", where τ_N is the binary tail of ω. It is the node restated after S1–S2, and the gate's offload rule would rightly treat it as no progress (see postmortem #894).

4. Then the S1–S7 partial with Theorem 5.1 as its hole.
