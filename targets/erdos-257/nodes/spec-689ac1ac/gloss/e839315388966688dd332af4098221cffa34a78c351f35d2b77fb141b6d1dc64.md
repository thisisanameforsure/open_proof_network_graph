$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N},\ p\in\mathbb{Z},\ q,y,k\in\mathbb{N}:\quad 0<q,\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=\frac{p}{q},\qquad \forall i,\ 1\le i\le k:\ \ 2^k\ \Big|\ \#\{a\in A: a\mid y+i\}.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\emptyset$, $p=0$, $q=1$, $y=0$ and $k=0$: the sum over the empty set is $0=\frac01$, and no $i$ satisfies $1\le i\le 0$, so the divisibility condition holds for want of cases.
