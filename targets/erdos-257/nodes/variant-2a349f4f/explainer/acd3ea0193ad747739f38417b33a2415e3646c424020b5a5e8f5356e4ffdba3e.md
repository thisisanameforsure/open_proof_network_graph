## Overview

Write $t_n = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ ($t_0 = 0$ in Lean), $T_N = \sum_{k\ge 0} t_{k+N}$, $s_F = \sum_{n\in F} t_n$ for a finite set $F$, and $C$ for the set of subset sums. The proof reuses the helper lemmas of the measure-one proof of node variant-f9c7b81b, and adds two arguments: no point of $C$ is isolated, and $C$ contains no interval. All of it lives in `private` helper lemmas; the main theorem's body is the one outline step `close`.

**Bounds, the gap lemma and the intervals (shared with variant-f9c7b81b).** Helper lemmas `tt_eq` to `gap`, `gg_term_le` to `sF_mem`, `CN`, `gg_mem_CN`, `TT_succ_le`, `sub_Ico`, `erase_Ico`, `sF_erase`, `mixed`, `sep` (and the measure lemmas, which this theorem does not use). As in the measure proof: $2^{-M} \le T_{M+1} \le 2^{-M} + \frac23 4^{-M}$; the gap lemma $T_{N+1} < t_N$ for $N \ge 1$; $C = g(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}})$ with $g(b) = \sum_n b_n t_n$ continuous, so $C$ is compact and closed; every point $g(b)$ lies in $C_M = \bigcup_{F\subseteq\{1..M\}} [s_F, s_F + T_{M+1}]$; and for distinct $F, G \subseteq \{1,\dots,M\}$, $|s_F - s_G| > T_{M+1}$.

**The gaps contain no point of the set.** Helper lemma `gap_not_mem`. Fix $F \subseteq \{1,\dots,M\}$ and $y$ with $s_F + T_{M+2} < y < s_F + t_{M+1}$, the gap left in $[s_F, s_F + T_{M+1}]$ between its two children. Suppose $y \in C_{M+1}$, so $s_G \le y \le s_G + T_{M+2}$ for some $G \subseteq \{1,\dots,M+1\}$. If $M+1 \in G$, write $G' = G \setminus \{M+1\}$, so $s_G = s_{G'} + t_{M+1}$: when $G' = F$ this gives $y \ge s_F + t_{M+1}$, a contradiction; otherwise $|s_F - s_{G'}| > T_{M+1} = t_{M+1} + T_{M+2}$, while both $s_F$ and $s_{G'} + t_{M+1}$ are within $T_{M+1}$ of $y$ on the appropriate side, a contradiction. If $M+1 \notin G$: when $G = F$, $y \le s_F + T_{M+2}$, a contradiction; otherwise $|s_F - s_G| > T_{M+1}$, again incompatible with both being within $T_{M+1}$ below $y$. So $y \notin C_{M+1} \supseteq C$.

**Empty interior.** Helper lemma `interior_gg_empty`. If a ball of radius $\varepsilon$ about $x$ lay in $C$, then $x = g(b) \in C$; pick $M$ with $2^{-M} < \varepsilon/2$ and the interval $[s_F, s_F + T_{M+1}] \ni x$ of $C_M$. The midpoint $y$ of its gap $(s_F + T_{M+2}, s_F + t_{M+1})$ (non-empty by the gap lemma at $M+1$) is within $T_{M+1} \le 2^{-M} + \frac23 4^{-M} < \varepsilon$ of $x$, so $y \in C$; but $y \notin C$ by the previous paragraph.

**No isolated points.** Helper lemma `preperfect`. Given $x = g(b)$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, choose $M$ with $2^{-M} < \varepsilon/2$ and put $n = M+1$, so $0 < t_n \le 2\cdot 2^{-n} = 2^{-M} < \varepsilon$. Flip the $n$-th digit: $b' = b$ except $b'_n = 1 - b_n$. The two series differ in one term, so $g(b') - g(b) = \pm t_n$, and $g(b')$ is a point of $C$ at distance exactly $t_n \in (0, \varepsilon)$ from $x$.

## Conclusion {steps: close}

The theorem: $C$ is the image of the compact Cantor space under the continuous $g$, hence compact; it is closed with no isolated points, hence perfect; and its interior is empty.
