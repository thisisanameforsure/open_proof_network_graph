## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $A=\{a^2+b^2:a,b\in\mathbb N\}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is irrational (in Lean the term at $n=0$ is $\frac10=0$). All the work is in private lemmas.

**The Lambert series** (`series_eq`). Let $\chi(d)=1$ if $d\ge 1$ is a sum of two squares and $\chi(d)=0$ otherwise, and let $f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\chi(d)$ (`chi`, `ff`, `ff_eq`). Expanding $\frac{1}{2^n-1}=\sum_{k\ge 1}2^{-nk}$ and collecting the terms with $nk=N$ gives $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=\sum_{N}f(N)/2^N$.

**Powers of two dividing $f$.** By `SS_iff` (the two-squares theorem, from Mathlib), $n$ is a sum of two squares exactly when every prime $q\equiv 3\pmod 4$ divides it to an even power; so $\chi$ and $f$ are multiplicative (`SS_mul`, `chi_mult`, `ff_mult`). Put $e(p)=2$ if $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$ and $e(p)=1$ otherwise (`ex`). Then $f(p^{e(p)})=2$ (`ff_prime_pow`): the sum is $1+0+1$ or $1+1$. Hence if each prime of a finite set $S$ divides $n\ne 0$ exactly to the power $e(p)$, then $2^{|S|}\mid f(n)$ (`pow_two_dvd_ff`).

**Integer tails.** Suppose $\sum_n f(n)/2^n=a/b$ and put $T(j)=b\sum_{i\ge 1}f(j+i)/2^i$ (`Tail`). Then $T(j)=a2^j-b\sum_{n\le j}f(n)2^{j-n}$ is an integer (`tail_eq_Zint`) and is positive (`tail_pos`). If $2^m\mid f(n)$ for $s<n\le s+m$, then $2^m\mid T(s+m)$ (`Zint_dvd`), so $T(s+m)\ge 2^m$.

**Divisors in progressions.** Write $d(n)$ for the number of divisors; $f(n)\le d(n)\le n$ (`fk_le_dd`, `fk_le`). If $\gcd(r,Q)=1$ and $r+tQ\le X^2$ for all $t<X$, then $\sum_{t<X}d(r+tQ)\le 2X(2+\log X)$ (`sum_dd_AP_le`): a number $n$ has at most twice as many divisors as it has divisors $e$ with $e^2\le n$ (`dd_le_two_mul_small`), and each $e$ divides at most $X/e+1$ of the terms (`card_AP_dvd_le`).

**The contradiction** (`irrational_ff_series`). Choose $m\ge b$ with $15m^6<2^m$ (`exists_param`) and set $q=3(m+m^2)m^2$. By Bertrand's postulate there are distinct primes $p_{ij}$, $i,j<m$, with $2^m<p_{ij}\le 2^{m+m^2}$. Let $Q=\prod_{i,j}p_{ij}^{e(p_{ij})+1}\le 2^q$. By the Chinese remainder theorem there is $c<Q$ with

$$c+tQ+(i+1)\equiv p_{ij}^{e(p_{ij})}\pmod{p_{ij}^{e(p_{ij})+1}}$$

for all $t$ and all $i,j<m$ (`hcong`). So for $i<m$ the number $c+tQ+i+1$ is divisible exactly to the power $e(p)$ by each of the $m$ primes $p=p_{i0},\dots,p_{i,m-1}$, and $2^m\mid f(c+tQ+i+1)$ (`hforced`). Therefore $T(c+tQ+m)\ge 2^m$ for every $t$ (`hlow`). Also $c+m+i+1$ is coprime to $Q$ for $i<3q$ (`hcop`): a prime $p_{ij}$ dividing it would divide a positive integer below $m+3q<2^m<p_{ij}$.

`averaging_contra` finishes. Put $L=3q$ and $X=Q+m+L$. Since $f(n)\le n$, $T(j)\le b\sum_{i<L}f(j+i+1)/2^{i+1}+b(j+L+2)/2^L$ (`tail_upper`). Sum this over $t<X$ at $j=c+tQ+m$ and apply the progression bound to each $i<L$; the result is $X2^m\le 2bX(2+\log X)+X$. Since $\log X\le q+1$, this gives $2^m\le 2b(3+q)+1$, which `param_facts` excludes.

## The theorem {steps: close}

Rewrite the sum over $A$ by `series_eq` and apply `irrational_ff_series`.
