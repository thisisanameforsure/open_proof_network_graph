$$\sum_{m \,\in\, \{a^2+b^2 \,:\, a,\, b \in \mathbb{N}\}} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \ \text{ is irrational}.$$

The sum runs over the set of sums of two squares, $\{0, 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 13, \dots\}$, so each such $m$ is counted once however many representations $m = a^2 + b^2$ it has. Here $\mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, so every square, $0$ included, is a sum of two squares. The sum is Lean's `tsum` over the set, the ordinary sum of a convergent nonnegative series; the term at $m = 0$ is Lean's $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$.
