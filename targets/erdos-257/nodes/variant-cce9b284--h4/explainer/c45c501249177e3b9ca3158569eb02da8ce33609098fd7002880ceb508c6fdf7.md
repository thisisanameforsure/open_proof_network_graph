## Statement

Let $r$ be the greedy walk of $2^{-20}$: $r_0 = 2^{-20}$, and $r_{k+1} = r_k - \frac{1}{2^k-1}$ when $k \ge 1$ and $\frac{1}{2^k-1} \le r_k$ (index $k$ is *taken*), otherwise $r_{k+1} = r_k$. For $n < m$, the walk's weighted count $W_n(m) = \sum_{i\ge0} 2^{-(i+1)}\#\{a \le n \text{ taken} : a \mid m+i+1\}$ is at most the walk-free $G_{20}(m) = \sum_{i \ge 0} 2^{-(i+1)}\#\{a : 20 < a < m,\ a \mid m+i+1\}$.

## The walk never takes an index at most 20

Each term $\frac{1}{2^k-1}$ is nonnegative (for $k = 0$ Lean's $1/0 = 0$), so the walk never increases: $r_k \le r_0 = 2^{-20}$ (`opn_walk_le`). For $1 \le a \le 20$, $\frac{1}{2^a-1} \ge \frac{1}{2^{20}-1} > 2^{-20} \ge r_a$, so $a$ is not taken (`opn_small_not_taken`).

## Termwise comparison

A taken $a \le n$ with $a \mid m+i+1$ has $a > 20$ by the previous step and $a \le n < m$, so the set counted on the left is a subset of the set counted on the right, for each $i$. The right-hand sets lie in $[0, m)$, so they are finite and have at most $m$ elements; hence each left term is at most the right term.

## Summing

Both series are dominated by $\sum_i 2^{-(i+1)} m$, a convergent geometric series, so both are summable, and `Summable.tsum_le_tsum` turns the termwise bound into $W_n(m) \le G_{20}(m)$. $\blacksquare$
