## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $x,y:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ satisfy $x_0>0$, $y_0>0$ and, for every $j$, $x_j\mid x_{j+1}$, $y_j\mid y_{j+1}$, $3x_j\le x_{j+1}$, $3y_j\le y_{j+1}$. Let $A=\{x_j\}\cup\{y_j\}$ and $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

The proof applies a Liouville-type criterion, proved in the file as the private lemma `r2_liou`: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$ and $2^CL\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* Its proof: suppose the sum is a rational $r$ with denominator $q$ and take $m$, $L$ for $C=4q$. Write $r=\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}t_a+T$ with $T$ the tail over $n\ge m$. Then $T>0$, and $T\le 4/2^m$ by comparison with a geometric series (`lac_f_le`, `lac_tail_le`). By `liou_int`, $L$ times the finite part is an integer, so $qLT$ is an integer; it is positive, so $qLT\ge 1$. But $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$ and $4q<2^{4q}$ give $qLT\le 4qL/2^m<1$, a contradiction.

Four lemmas about one chain $x$ with $x_0>0$ follow. `ch_gt`: $j<x_j$ for all $j$, by induction from $3x_j\le x_{j+1}$. `ch_mono`: $x$ is strictly increasing. `ch_dvd`: $x_i\mid x_j$ for $i\le j$, by induction from $x_j\mid x_{j+1}$.

`ch_good` chooses the indices: for every $C$ there are $J,K$ with

$$C+x_J+y_K\le x_{J+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad C+x_J+y_K\le y_{K+1}.$$

Put $K_0=3C+x_0$, $Y=y_{K_0}$ and $Z=y_{K_0+1}$, so $Y>K_0$ and $Z\ge 3Y$. Let $J+1$ be the least index with $Z<x_{J+1}$; it exists because $x_j>j$, and it is not $0$ because $Z>x_0$. So $x_J\le Z<x_{J+1}$. If $C+x_J+Y\le Z$, take $K=K_0$: both inequalities hold since $Z<x_{J+1}$. Otherwise $x_J>Z-Y-C$, and $K=K_0+1$ works: from $x_{J+1}\ge 3x_J$, $Z\ge 3Y$ and $Y>3C$ one gets $C+x_J+Z\le x_{J+1}$, and from $x_J\le Z$, $C\le Z$ and $y_{K_0+2}\ge 3Z$ one gets $C+x_J+Z\le y_{K_0+2}$. The Lean closes both cases by linear arithmetic over the listed facts.

## $A$ is infinite and does not contain $0$ {steps: hinf h0}

By `ch_mono` the sequence $x$ is injective, so its range, and hence $A$, is infinite. By `ch_gt`, $x_k>k\ge 0$ and $y_k>k\ge 0$, so $0\notin A$. So `r2_liou` applies once its last hypothesis is checked for an arbitrary $C$.

## Choosing $m$ and $L$ {steps: s3 hxJ hyK}

Fix $C$ and take $J,K$ from `ch_good`. Record $x_J>J$ and $y_K>K$, so both are nonzero. Take

$$m=\min\bigl(x_{J+1},\,y_{K+1}\bigr),\qquad L=\bigl(2^{x_J}-1\bigr)\bigl(2^{y_K}-1\bigr).$$

## $m\in A$ and $L>0$ {steps: s6 s7}

The minimum is one of $x_{J+1}$, $y_{K+1}$, so $m\in A$. Since $x_J\ne 0$ and $y_K\ne 0$, $2^{x_J}>1$ and $2^{y_K}>1$, so both factors of $L$ are positive.

## Every $2^n-1$ with $n\in A$, $n<m$ divides $L$ {steps: s8}

If $n=x_i<m$ then $x_i<x_{J+1}$, so $i<J+1$ because $x$ is strictly increasing, so $x_i\mid x_J$ by `ch_dvd`. Since $u\mid v$ implies $(2^u-1)\mid(2^v-1)$, $2^n-1$ divides the first factor of $L$. If $n=y_i<m$, the same argument with $y$ and $K$ shows that $2^n-1$ divides the second factor.

## The size condition $2^CL\le 2^m$ {steps: s9}

Dropping the two $-1$'s,

$$2^C L\;\le\;2^C\cdot 2^{x_J}\cdot 2^{y_K}\;=\;2^{\,C+x_J+y_K}\;\le\;2^{\min(x_{J+1},\,y_{K+1})},$$

the last step because $C+x_J+y_K$ is at most both $x_{J+1}$ and $y_{K+1}$, which are the two inequalities from `ch_good`.
