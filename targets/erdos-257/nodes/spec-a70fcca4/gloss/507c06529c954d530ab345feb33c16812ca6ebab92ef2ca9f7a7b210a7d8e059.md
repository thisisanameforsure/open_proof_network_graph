$$\exists\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite}:\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \;=\; \frac12 .$$

The sum is Lean's `tsum` over $A$ (summable for every $A$, so the ordinary sum), and the equation is between real numbers. In Lean the term at $n = 0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so $A$ may contain $0$ at no cost; removing it leaves $A$ infinite and the sum unchanged.

The statement asks for one infinite set whose sum is exactly the rational number $\frac12$. It names no particular set.
