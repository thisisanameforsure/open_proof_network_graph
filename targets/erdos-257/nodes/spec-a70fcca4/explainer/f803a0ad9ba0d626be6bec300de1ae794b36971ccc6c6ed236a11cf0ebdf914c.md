## Overview

The claim is that $\frac12 = \sum_{a\in A} t_a$ for an infinite $A$, where $t_a = \frac{1}{2^a-1}$ (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$). The partial proof runs the greedy walk for $\frac12$: $r_0 = \frac12$, and $r_{m+1} = r_m - t_m$ if $t_m \le r_m$, else $r_{m+1} = r_m$; $A$ is the set of positions the walk takes. Granting four statements (the holes), it shows $r_m \to 0$, hence $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = \frac12$, and that $A$ is infinite because no finite sum of the $t_a$ is $\frac12$. Only the fourth hole, that the walk never stalls, is open; the assembly below is checked by the kernel.

## The four holes {steps: h1, h2, h3, h4}

Each hole is stated for an arbitrary $r$ satisfying the walk's recursion, so each is a statement of its own.

- h1 (spec-a70fcca4--h1, proved): for every $m$, $r_m \ge 0$ and the sum of the taken terms below $m$ is $\frac12 - r_m$.
- h2 (spec-a70fcca4--h2, proved): $\sum_{a\in S} t_a \ne \frac12$ for every finite $S \subseteq \mathbb{N}$.
- h3 (spec-a70fcca4--h3, proved): $(t_n)$ is summable. The assembly below does not refer to h3: the tail-to-zero step (`tendsto_sum_nat_add`) is applied to the sequence with no summability argument.
- h4 (spec-a70fcca4--h4, open): for every $m$, $r_m \le \sum_{k\ge0} t_{k+m}$, the whole tail from $m$ on: the walk never stalls.

## The walk exists {steps: hex, s6}

The sequence $r$ is defined by recursion on $m$ with exactly the recursion above, so it exists; the proof fixes it and specialises h1 and h4 to it.

## The remainder tends to zero {steps: htail, hr}

The tails $\sum_{k\ge0} t_{k+m}$ tend to $0$ as $m \to \infty$. Since $0 \le r_m$ (h1) and $r_m \le \sum_{k\ge0} t_{k+m}$ (h4), the squeeze gives $r_m \to 0$.

## The taken terms sum to one half {steps: hfnn, hpart, hsum}

Let $f(a) = t_a$ if $t_a \le r_a$ (position $a$ is taken) and $f(a) = 0$ otherwise. Every $f(a) \ge 0$. By h1, $\sum_{a<m} f(a) = \frac12 - r_m \to \frac12$. For a nonnegative sequence, convergence of the partial sums is the sum, so $\sum_{a\ge0} f(a) = \frac12$.

## The taken set is infinite {steps: s12}

Take $A = \{a : t_a \le r_a\}$ and suppose it is finite, bounded by $M$. For $a > M$ nothing is taken, so the walk is constant from $M+1$ on: $r_a = r_{M+1}$ for all $a \ge M+1$, by induction on $a$. A constant sequence tends to its value, and $r_m \to 0$, so $r_{M+1} = 0$. Then h1 at $M+1$ says the taken terms below $M+1$ sum to exactly $\frac12$, a finite subsum equal to $\frac12$, which h2 forbids.

## The sum over the taken set is one half {steps: s13}

The sum of $t$ over the set $A$ equals the sum over all $a$ of the indicator of $A$ times $t$, and that indicator function is $f$. So $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = \sum_a f(a) = \frac12$.
