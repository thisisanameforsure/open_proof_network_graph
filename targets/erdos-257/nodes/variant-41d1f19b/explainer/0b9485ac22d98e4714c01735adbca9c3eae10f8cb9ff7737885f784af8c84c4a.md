## Notation and the helper lemmas

Let $A$ be an infinite set of natural numbers with $0\notin A$, and write $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The hypothesis is: for every $C\in\mathbb N$ there are $m\in A$ and a finite set $F$ whose elements are exactly the $a\in A$ with $a<m$, such that $C+\sum_{a\in F}a\le m$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

Six private lemmas come before the theorem. `lac_f_nonneg`: $t_n\ge 0$. `lac_f_le`: $t_n\le 2\cdot(1/2)^n$ (for $n=0$ the left side is $\frac10=0$ in Lean). `lac_summable`: hence $\sum_n t_n$ converges, by comparison with a geometric series. `lac_tail_le`: for any set $A$ and any $m$, $\sum_{n\ge m,\ n\in A}t_n\le 4\cdot(1/2)^m$, by the same comparison. `liou_int`: if $F$ is a finite set of nonzero numbers and $2^a-1$ divides $L$ for every $a\in F$, then $L\sum_{a\in F}t_a$ is an integer, since each $L\,t_a=L/(2^a-1)$ is one.

The last, `r2_liou`, is a Liouville-type criterion and carries the argument: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$ and $2^C L\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* Its proof: suppose the sum is a rational $r$ with denominator $q$, and take $m$ and $L$ for $C=4q$. Then $m\ge 1$. Split $r=\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}t_a+T$ with $T$ the tail over $n\ge m$. The tail is positive, because it contains $t_m>0$, and $T\le 4/2^m$ by `lac_tail_le`. By `liou_int`, $L$ times the finite part is an integer $N$, so $qLT=L\cdot\mathrm{num}(r)-qN$ is an integer; it is positive, hence $qLT\ge 1$. On the other hand $2^{4q}\cdot qL\cdot 4/2^m\le 4q$ because $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$, and $4q<2^{4q}$, so $qLT\le qL\cdot 4/2^m<1$. This is a contradiction.

The theorem applies `r2_liou` with $L$ the product of the numbers $2^a-1$ over the elements of $A$ below $m$.

## Choosing $m$ and $L$ {steps: s1}

Fix $C$. The hypothesis gives $m\in A$ and the finite set $F=\{a\in A: a<m\}$ with $C+\sum_{a\in F}a\le m$. Put

$$L=\prod_{a\in F}\bigl(2^a-1\bigr).$$

Three conditions of the criterion remain.

## $L$ is positive {steps: s2}

Each $a\in F$ lies in $A$, so $a\ne 0$ (step `s2.ha0`), so $2^a>1$ and the factor $2^a-1$ is positive. A product of positive numbers is positive.

## Every $2^a-1$ below $m$ divides $L$ {steps: s3}

If $a\in A$ and $a<m$ then $a\in F$, and a factor of a product divides it.

## The size condition $2^C L\le 2^m$ {steps: s4}

Each factor satisfies $2^a-1\le 2^a$, so (step `s4.hL`)

$$L\;\le\;\prod_{a\in F}2^a\;=\;2^{\sum_{a\in F}a}.$$

Therefore

$$2^C L\;\le\;2^C\cdot 2^{\sum_{a\in F}a}\;=\;2^{\,C+\sum_{a\in F}a}\;\le\;2^m,$$

the last step by the hypothesis $C+\sum_{a\in F}a\le m$. The criterion `r2_liou` now gives the irrationality of $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$.
