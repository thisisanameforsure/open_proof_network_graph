label: a skipped step leaves at most 2^-m + 4^-m/3

$$\forall\, r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}:\quad r_0=\tfrac12,\ \ \forall m:\ r_{m+1}=\begin{cases} r_m-\dfrac{1}{2^m-1} & \text{if } \dfrac{1}{2^m-1}\le r_m,\\[4pt] r_m & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \ \Longrightarrow\ \forall m:\ \ r_m<\frac{1}{2^m-1}\ \Rightarrow\ r_m\le\frac{1}{2^m}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 4^m}.$$

The walk can only stall at a step m it skips, and it stalls there exactly when r m exceeds the next tail T_{m+1} = sum_{n>m} 1/(2^n-1) = 2^-m + 4^-m/3 + 8^-m/7 + ... while lying below 1/(2^m-1) = 2^-m + 4^-m + 8^-m + ...  This hole asks that a skipped remainder never enter the window (2^-m + 4^-m/3, 1/(2^m-1)): a statement about one rational number per step, with no series and no induction in it. It is slightly stronger than never stalling: its window reaches below the stall threshold by T_{m+1} - (2^-m + 4^-m/3) = 8^-m/7 + 16^-m/15 + ..., a little more than 8^-m/7 per step; in the scaled remainder x_m = 2^m r_m it reads: if x_m < 2^m/(2^m-1) then x_m <= 1 + 2^-m/3. Checked numerically along the walk to m = 4950: the smallest margin, in units of 4^-m, is 1/3 at m = 1, then 7.95 at m = 5 and 10.2 at m = 4, growing like 2^m.

(The display restates the Lean in full; the paragraph above is t1022-a's, with one correction by t1022-k from t1022-n's review: the window is a little more, not less, than 8^-m/7. Earlier versions: t1022-a's, then t1022-e's, whose display this keeps.)
