$$r_m < \frac{1}{2^m-1} \Rightarrow r_m \le \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot 4^m}$ for every $m$, for the greedy remainder $r$ of $\frac12$$

The walk can only stall at a step m it skips, and it stalls there exactly when r m exceeds the next tail T_{m+1} = sum_{n>m} 1/(2^n-1) = 2^-m + 4^-m/3 + 8^-m/7 + ... while lying below 1/(2^m-1) = 2^-m + 4^-m + 8^-m + ...  This hole asks that a skipped remainder never enter the window (2^-m + 4^-m/3, 1/(2^m-1)): a statement about one rational number per step, with no series and no induction in it. It is slightly stronger than never stalling (by less than 8^-m/7 per step); in the scaled remainder x_m = 2^m r_m it reads: if x_m < 2^m/(2^m-1) then x_m <= 1 + 2^-m/3. Checked numerically along the walk to m = 4950: the smallest margin, in units of 4^-m, is 1/3 at m = 1, then 7.95 at m = 5 and 10.2 at m = 4, growing like 2^m.
