## Overview

Write $e_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $e_0 = \frac10 = 0$) and let $r$ be the greedy remainder of $\frac12$: $r_0 = \frac12$, and $r_{m+1} = r_m - e_m$ if $e_m \le r_m$ (step $m$ is taken), else $r_{m+1} = r_m$ (step $m$ is skipped). The statement to prove: at every skipped $m$, $r_m \le \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m}$. The proof reduces it to a statement about runs of taken steps: after a skipped $m \ge 1$, the walk skips again within $(m, 3m+2]$. That is the hole. The window is the widest of its kind that works: the terms over $(m, 3m+2]$ sum to at most the bound, while the terms over $(m, 3m+3]$ already sum to more than it.

**The hole.** For every sequence $r$ obeying the recursion from $\frac12$ and every $m \ge 1$ with $r_m < e_m$, there is $n$ with $m < n \le 3m+2$ and $r_n < e_n$. It is left open. It is weaker than the hole of the rival partial on this node, whose window is $(m, 2m]$.

**A termwise bound.** For real $x \ge 4$, $\frac{1}{x-1} \le \frac1x + \frac1{x^2} + \frac43\cdot\frac1{x^3}$: multiplied by $x - 1$, the right side is $1 + \frac{1}{x^3}\left(\frac x3 - \frac43\right) \ge 1$.

**The block bound.** For $m \ge 1$, put $a = 2^{-m} \le \frac12$. Each index $k = m+1+i$ with $0 \le i < 2m+2$ has $2^k \ge 4$, so $e_k \le a\,2^{-(i+1)} + a^2\,4^{-(i+1)} + \frac43 a^3\,8^{-(i+1)}$. Summing the three geometric series over $i < 2m+2$ gives $a + \frac{a^2}{3} - \frac{5}{84}a^3 - \frac{a^6}{48} - \frac{a^9}{336}$, which is at most $a + \frac{a^2}{3} = \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m}$.

**The close.** At $m = 0$ the hypothesis $r_0 < e_0 = 0$ is false. For $m \ge 1$, suppose the bound fails, so $r_m$ exceeds it and hence exceeds every partial sum of the block. Induction along the block shows that every step $m+1, \dots, 3m+2$ is taken, with $r_{m+1+j} = r_m - (e_{m+1} + \dots + e_{m+j})$: at each step the remainder still exceeds the next term, because the partial sum through that term is below $r_m$. But the hole gives a skipped $n$ in $(m, 3m+2]$, which is a contradiction.
