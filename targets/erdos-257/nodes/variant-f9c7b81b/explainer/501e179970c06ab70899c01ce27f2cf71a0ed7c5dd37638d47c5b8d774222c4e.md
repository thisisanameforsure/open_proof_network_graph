## Overview

Write $t_n = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ (so $t_0 = 0$ in Lean) and $T_N = \sum_{k \ge 0} t_{k+N}$, the tail from $N$ on. Let $C = \{\sum_{n\in A} t_n : A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\}$. The proof shows $\lambda(C) \le 1$ by covering $C$ with $2^M$ intervals of length $T_{M+1}$, and $\lambda(C) \ge 1$ because those intervals are pairwise disjoint, decrease with $M$, and their intersection lies in the closed set $C$.

## Bounds on the terms and the tails

Helper lemmas `tt_eq`, `half_pow_le`, `tt_nonneg`, `tt_upper`, `tt_lower`, `tt_le_geom`, `tt_summable`, `tt_summable_shift`, `TT_succ`, `TT_nonneg`, `geom_shift`, `q4`, `geom4_shift`, `TT_upper`, `TT_lower`, `gap`.

With $a = 2^{-m}$ one has $t_m = \frac{a}{1-a}$ for every $m$ (both sides are $0$ at $m=0$). For $m \ge 1$, $0 < a \le \frac12$ gives
$$a + a^2 < t_m \le a + 2a^2,$$
the upper bound because $(a+2a^2)(1-a) - a = a^2(1-2a) \ge 0$ and the lower because $(a+a^2)(1-a) = a - a^3 < a$. Hence $0 \le t_m \le 2\cdot 2^{-m}$, the series converges, and every shifted series converges. Peeling off the first term gives $T_N = t_N + T_{N+1}$. Summing the two geometric series $\sum_k 2^{-(k+N)} = 2\cdot 2^{-N}$ and $\sum_k 4^{-(k+N)} = \frac43 4^{-N}$ against the term bounds gives, for every $M$,
$$2^{-M} \le T_{M+1} \le 2^{-M} + \tfrac23\, 4^{-M}.$$
Comparing with $t_N > 2^{-N} + 4^{-N}$ gives the gap lemma $T_{N+1} < t_N$ for $N \ge 1$: each term exceeds the sum of all later terms.

## The set is the image of Cantor space, hence closed

Helper lemmas `gg_term_le`, `gg_term_nonneg`, `gg_summable`, `gg_continuous`, `CC_eq`, `CC_closed`, `sF_mem`.

For $b \in \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ let $g(b) = \sum_n b_n t_n$. Each summand depends continuously on $b$ (it reads one coordinate) and is bounded by $t_n$, which is summable, so $g$ is continuous by uniform convergence. Writing the sum over $A$ as the sum of the indicator of $A$ times $t$ shows $C = g(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}})$: take $b$ the indicator of $A$, and conversely $A = \{n : b_n = 1\}$. Cantor space is compact, so $C$ is compact and therefore closed. In particular every finite sum $s_F = \sum_{n \in F} t_n$ lies in $C$.

## Upper bound: $\lambda(C) \le 1$

Helper lemmas `CN`, `gg_mem_CN`, `pow_two_half`, `vol_CN_le`, `upper`, `vol_le_one`.

Fix $M$ and let $C_M = \bigcup_{F \subseteq \{1,\dots,M\}} [s_F,\ s_F + T_{M+1}]$. Every $g(b)$ lies in $C_M$: split $g(b)$ into the first $M+1$ terms, which equal $s_F$ for $F = \{1 \le n \le M : b_n = 1\}$ (the $n=0$ term is $0$), and the remaining tail, which lies between $0$ and $T_{M+1}$. Subadditivity gives $\lambda(C) \le \lambda(C_M) \le 2^M T_{M+1}$, and the tail bound gives $2^M T_{M+1} \le 1 + \frac23 2^{-M} \le 1 + 2^{-M}$. Letting $M \to \infty$, $\lambda(C) \le 1$.

## Disjointness of the intervals

Helper lemmas `TT_succ_le`, `sub_Ico`, `erase_Ico`, `sF_erase`, `mixed`, `sep`.

Claim: for distinct $F, G \subseteq \{1,\dots,M\}$, $|s_F - s_G| > T_{M+1}$. Induction on $M$; for $M = 0$ both sets are empty. In the step to $M+1$ write $k = M+1$ and remove $k$ from whichever sets contain it. If both or neither contain $k$, the difference is unchanged and the induction hypothesis gives more than $T_{M+1} \ge T_{M+2}$. If only $G$ contains $k$, with $G' = G \setminus \{k\}$, then either $F = G'$ and $|s_F - s_G| = t_k > T_{k+1}$ by the gap lemma, or $|s_F - s_{G'}| > T_{M+1} = t_k + T_{M+2}$, and subtracting $t_k \ge 0$ from a number of absolute value above $t_k + T_{M+2}$ leaves one of absolute value above $T_{M+2}$. The case where only $F$ contains $k$ is the same with $F$ and $G$ exchanged.

## Lower bound: $\lambda(C) \ge 1$, and the conclusion

Helpers `CN_meas`, `vol_CN_eq`, `vol_CN_ge`, `CN_anti`, `iInter_sub`, `vol_ge_one`, and the theorem's own outline step `close` (rewrite the set as the image of g, then combine the two bounds by antisymmetry).

By disjointness, $\lambda(C_M) = 2^M T_{M+1} \ge 2^M 2^{-M} = 1$. The sets decrease: an interval of $C_{M+1}$ whose set contains $M+1$ is $[s_{F'} + t_{M+1},\ s_{F'} + T_{M+1}]$ inside $[s_{F'}, s_{F'} + T_{M+1}]$ (using $T_{M+1} = t_{M+1} + T_{M+2}$), and one whose set does not is inside its own interval of $C_M$ since $T_{M+2} \le T_{M+1}$. For decreasing measurable sets of finite measure, $\lambda(\bigcap_M C_M) = \inf_M \lambda(C_M) \ge 1$. Finally $\bigcap_M C_M \subseteq C$: a point $x$ of every $C_M$ is within $T_{M+1} \le 2\cdot 2^{-M}$ of a finite sum $s_F \in C$, so $x$ is in the closure of $C$, which is $C$. Hence $\lambda(C) \ge 1$, and with the upper bound $\lambda(C) = 1$.
