## Overview

Write $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$). Let $r \in \mathbb{Q}$, $0 \notin A$, $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = r$, and $n \ge 1$. The proof shows that the remainder $r - \sum_{a\in A,\,a<n} t_a$ equals the tail $\sum_{a\in A,\,a\ge n} t_a$, and splits that tail as $[n\in A]\,t_n + \sum_{a\in A,\,a>n} t_a$. If $n \in A$ the remainder is $t_n$ plus something nonnegative, so $t_n$ fits. If $n \notin A$ the remainder is at most $\sum_{m>n} t_m$, which the gap lemma spec-ddd99827 puts strictly below $t_n$, so $t_n$ does not fit.

Before the theorem the file proves three small lemmas: every $t_m \ge 0$ (since $2^m \ge 1$); $t_m \le 2\,(\frac12)^m$ (both sides are $0$ and $2$ at $m = 0$; for $m \ge 1$, multiplying out, it is $2^m \le 2(2^m-1)$, i.e. $2 \le 2^m$); and hence $(t_m)$ is summable, by comparison with a geometric series.

## Rewriting the sums over the natural numbers {steps: hf, e1, e2}

Let $f = \mathbf 1_A\, t$, the sequence equal to $t_m$ on $A$ and $0$ off it. It is summable, being dominated by the summable $t$. The sum over the set $A$ equals $\sum_{i\ge0} f(i)$, and the sum over $A \cap [0,n)$ equals the finite sum $\sum_{i<n} f(i)$: on $i<n$ the two indicator functions agree (whether or not $i \in A$), and every term with $i \ge n$ vanishes.

## The remainder is the tail from n {steps: e3, e4, htail_nonneg}

Using $r = \sum_{i\ge0} f(i) = \sum_{i<n} f(i) + \sum_{i\ge0} f(i+n)$, the remainder $r - \sum_{a\in A,\,a<n} t_a$ is $\sum_{i\ge0} f(i+n)$. Peeling off its first term, this is $f(n) + \sum_{i\ge0} f(i+n+1)$, and the last sum is nonnegative because every $f(i) \ge 0$.

## If n is in A, its term fits {steps: s7}

Then $f(n) = t_n$, so the remainder is $t_n$ plus a nonnegative tail, hence at least $t_n$.

## If n is not in A, its term does not fit {steps: s8}

Suppose $t_n \le$ remainder but $n \notin A$. Then $f(n) = 0$, so $t_n \le \sum_{i\ge0} f(n+1+i)$. Each $f(n+1+i) \le t_{n+1+i}$ (an indicator of a nonnegative function is at most the function), so the tail is at most $\sum_{k\ge0} t_{n+1+k}$, which is $< t_n$ by the gap lemma at $n \ge 1$. This contradiction gives $n \in A$.
