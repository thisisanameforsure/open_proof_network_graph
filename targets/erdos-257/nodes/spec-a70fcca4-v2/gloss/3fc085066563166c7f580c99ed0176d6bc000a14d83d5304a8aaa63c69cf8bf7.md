label: one half is the sum of an infinite set
---
$$\exists\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite with}\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \;=\; \frac12 .$$

The sum is Lean's `tsum` over $A$ (summable for every $A$, so the ordinary sum). In Lean the term at $n = 0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so $A$ may contain $0$ at no cost; removing it leaves $A$ infinite with the same sum, so the statement says the same of sets of positive integers.
