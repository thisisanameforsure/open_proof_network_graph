## What is proved, and how

Write $S(A)=\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ for a set $A$ of natural numbers, and $A^c=\mathbb N\setminus A$ for its complement. The statement is that for every $A\subseteq\mathbb N$, at least one of $S(A)$ and $S(A^c)$ is irrational. There is no hypothesis on $A$: it may be finite, cofinite, or neither. In Lean the term at $n=0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1}=\frac10=0$, so it does not matter on which side $0$ lies.

The proof has no lemmas of its own. It uses two results from outside the file, both declared dependencies of the node. The first is `spec_a70fcca4__h3` (node `spec-a70fcca4--h3`): the series $\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ converges. The second is `Opn.erdos_257_all_naturals` (node `variant-2f5236aa`): its sum $S(\mathbb N)$ is irrational. Given these, the argument is that $S(A)+S(A^c)=S(\mathbb N)$, and two real numbers whose sum is irrational cannot both be rational.

## The full series converges {steps: hs}

This is the first dependency, quoted as it stands: the function $n\mapsto\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ on $\mathbb N$ is summable. A summable series of real numbers stays summable when restricted to a subset; that is used in the next step for $A$ and for $A^c$.

## The two sums add up to the full sum {steps: key}

Because the restrictions to $A$ and to $A^c$ are both summable, a general fact about sums over a set and its complement (`Summable.tsum_add_tsum_compl`) gives

$$S(A)+S(A^c)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}\frac{1}{2^n-1}.$$

## The full sum is irrational {steps: hirr}

This is the second dependency, quoted as it stands: $\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is irrational.

## Conclusion {steps: close}

Rewrite the irrational number of the last step, using `key`, as $S(A)+S(A^c)$. So $S(A)+S(A^c)$ is irrational. If a sum $u+v$ of two real numbers is irrational, then $u$ is irrational or $v$ is irrational, since the sum of two rationals is rational. Applied to $u=S(A)$ and $v=S(A^c)$ this is the statement.
