label: a set and its complement are not both rational
---
$$\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N}:\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}\notin\mathbb{Q}\quad\text{or}\quad \sum_{n\in \mathbb{N}\setminus A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}\notin\mathbb{Q}.$$

Each sum is Lean's `tsum` over the set (summable for every set, so the ordinary sum). In Lean the term at $n = 0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so whether $0$ lies in $A$ or in its complement changes neither sum.

There is no hypothesis on $A$: it may be finite, have finite complement, or neither. The "or" is inclusive, and "$\notin\mathbb{Q}$" is Lean's `Irrational`: the real number equals no rational. So the statement says that a set and its complement never both have a rational sum.
