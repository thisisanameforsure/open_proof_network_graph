## Overview

Write $d(n)$ for the number of divisors of $n$ (with $d(0) = 0$). The proof is Erdős's 1948 argument: the series is rewritten as $\sum_n d(n)/2^n$, a rational value would make every scaled tail of that series a positive integer, the Chinese remainder theorem makes $m$ consecutive divisor counts divisible by $2^m$, which forces such a tail to be at least $2^m$, and averaging along an arithmetic progression shows the tails are on average far smaller. All the work is in private helper declarations; the theorem itself rewrites with `series_eq` and applies `irrational_dd_series`.

**The Lambert series.** `series_eq`: $\sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} = \sum_{n \ge 0} \frac{d(n)}{2^n}$. This is Mathlib's `tsum_pow_div_one_sub_eq_tsum_sigma` at $r = \frac12$ and $k = 0$, after moving between sums over $\mathbb{N}$ and over the positive integers (both $n = 0$ terms are $0$ in Lean).

**Tails are integers.** Suppose $\sum_n d(n)/2^n = a/b$ with $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $b \ge 1$ (the numerator and denominator of the rational). For each $j$ let $T_j = b \sum_{i \ge 1} d(j+i)/2^i$. Splitting the series after $n = j$ and multiplying by $2^j$ gives (`tail_eq_Zint`)
$$T_j = 2^j a - b \sum_{n=0}^{j} d(n)\, 2^{\,j-n} \in \mathbb{Z},$$
and $T_j > 0$ because $d(j+1) \ge 1$ (`tail_pos`).

**Forced divisibility.** `pow_two_dvd_dd`: if $m$ distinct primes each divide $n \ne 0$ exactly once, then $2^m \mid d(n)$, since $d(n) = \prod_{p \mid n} (v_p(n) + 1)$ and each of those $m$ factors is $2$. `Zint_dvd`: if $2^m \mid d(n)$ for every $n$ with $k < n \le k+m$, then $2^m \mid T_{k+m}$, because in the formula above every term with $n \le k$ carries the factor $2^{k+m-n} \ge 2^m$. So in that case $T_{k+m} \ge 2^m$.

**Choosing the primes.** Take $m \ge b$ with $15 m^5 < 2^m$ (`exists_param`) and $q = 2(m + m^2)m^2$. Bertrand's postulate gives a prime $P_e \in (2^e, 2^{e+1}]$ for each $e$, increasing in $e$. The $m^2$ primes $p_{ij} = P_{m + im + j}$, $0 \le i, j < m$, are distinct, exceed $2^m$, and their squares multiply to $Q = \prod p_{ij}^2 \le 2^q$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives $c < Q$ with $c + i + 1 \equiv p_{ij} \pmod{p_{ij}^2}$ for all $i, j$. Hence for every $t$ and every $i < m$, the number $c + tQ + i + 1$ is divisible by each of $p_{i0}, \dots, p_{i,m-1}$ exactly once, so $2^m \mid d(n)$ for $c + tQ < n \le c + tQ + m$, and therefore
$$T_{c+tQ+m} \ge 2^m \quad \text{for every } t \ge 0.$$
Since every $p_{ij}$ exceeds $m + 3q$, the numbers $c + m + i + 1$ with $i < 3q$ are coprime to $Q$.

**The averaging contradiction.** `averaging_contra`. Put $L = 3q$ and $X = Q + m + L$. Bounding $d(n) \le n$ beyond the first $L$ terms (`tail_upper`) gives
$$T_j \le b \sum_{i < L} \frac{d(j+i+1)}{2^{i+1}} + b\,\frac{j+L+2}{2^L}.$$
For $r$ coprime to $Q$ and $r + tQ \le X^2$ when $t < X$, `sum_dd_AP_le` bounds $\sum_{t<X} d(r + tQ) \le 2X(2 + \log X)$: each divisor pairs with one at most the square root (`dd_le_two_mul_small`), and at most $X/e + 1$ of the $t < X$ have $e \mid r + tQ$ (`card_AP_dvd_le`). Summing the lower bound over $t < X$ and using these estimates yields $X\, 2^m \le b \cdot 2X(2 + \log X) + X$, that is $2^m \le 2b(2 + \log X) + 1$. As $X \le 2^{q+1}$, $\log X \le q + 1$, so $2^m \le 2b(3+q) + 1$, which the choice of $m$ makes false (`param_facts`).
