## Statement

If every interval $(n, 2n-10]$ with $n \ge 1092$ contains a calm point $m$, that is one with $G(m) = \sum_{i\ge0} 2^{-(i+1)} c_m(m+i+1) \le 99/100$ where $c_m(N) = \#\{a : 20 < a < m,\ a \mid N\}$, then every such interval contains $m = 2q-2$ or $m = 4q-2$ with $q$ and $m+1$ both prime. Lean writes $H \to P$ as $\neg H \vee P$ because $H$, the calm-point hole, is open; the proof turns it back into $H \to P$ by `or_iff_not_imp_left`.

## Reduction to the structure lemma

Fix $n \ge 1092$ and take a calm $m$ with $n < m$ and $m + 10 \le 2n$, so $m \ge 1093$. It suffices to show that $m+1$ is prime and $m+2 = 2q$ or $m+2 = 4q$ with $q$ prime: then $2q-1 = m+1$ (or $4q-1 = m+1$) is prime, and the bounds on $2q-2$ (or $4q-2$) are those on $m$.

## Ten terms of G (`L_real`)

Every term of $G$ is nonnegative and $c_m(N) \le m$, so $G$ converges and is at least its first ten terms. Multiplying by $1024$, calm gives $\sum_{k=1}^{10} 2^{10-k} c_m(m+k) \le 1013$.

## Small divisors give large ones (`bcnt_le`)

For $N = m+k$ with $1 \le k \le 10$ we have $480 < N < 2m$. If $d \in \{2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,15,16,18,20,24\}$ divides $N$, then $N/d$ divides $N$ with $20 < N/d < m$, and distinct $d$ give distinct $N/d$. So $c_m(N) \ge b(N)$, the number of those $d$ dividing $N$. As $b(N)$ depends only on $N \bmod 720$ (`bcnt_periodic`), so does $L(m) = \sum_{k=1}^{10} 2^{10-k} b(m+k)$.

## Three residue checks in the kernel (`bA`, `bB`, `bC`)

Over all $r < 720$, by `decide`: (A) for odd $r$, $L \ge 1014$; (B) for even $r$, $L$ with the $k=1$ count raised to at least $1$ is $\ge 1014$; (C) for even $r$, $L$ with the $k=2$ count raised to at least $3$ is $\ge 1014$.

## Conclusions (`calm_structure`)

By (A), $m$ is even. If $m+1$ were composite, $m+1 = ab$ with $2 \le a \le b$ gives $b \ge 34$, a divisor in $(20, m)$ (`cS_one`), and (B) contradicts the ten-term bound; so $m+1$ is prime. If neither $(m+2)/2$ is prime nor $4 \mid m+2$ with $(m+2)/4$ prime, then $m+2$ has three divisors in $(20,m)$ (`cS_three`): with $m+2 = 2u$, $u$ odd composite $= ab$ ($3 \le a \le b$) gives $b, 2b, ab$; with $u = 2v$ and $v = ab$ composite, $b, 2b, 4b$ when $a = 2$ and $2b, ab, 2ab$ when $a \ge 3$. Then (C) contradicts the ten-term bound. $\blacksquare$
