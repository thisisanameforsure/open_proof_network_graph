$$\exists\, x\in\mathbb{Q},\ r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R},\ q:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:\quad x\ge 0,\quad r_0=x,\quad \forall m:\ q_m=\Bigl\lfloor r_m\Big/\tfrac{1}{2^m-1}\Bigr\rfloor,\ \ r_{m+1}=r_m-\frac{q_m}{2^m-1},\quad \sum_{n\in E}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=x-\sum_{m\ge 0}\frac{c_m}{2^m-1},\quad \{m: c_m\ne 0\}\ \text{finite},$$

where $E=\{m : q_m\ne 0\ \text{and}\ r_{m+1}\le\sum_{j\ge 0}\frac{1}{2^{m+1+j}-1}\}$ and $c_m=q_m-1$ for $m\in E$, $c_m=q_m$ otherwise. These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $x=0$ and $r_m=0$, $q_m=0$ for every $m$: then $E$ is empty, every $c_m$ is $0$, and the identity reads $0=0-0$. The floor is the natural-number floor. In Lean the term at $m=0$ is $\frac10=0$.
