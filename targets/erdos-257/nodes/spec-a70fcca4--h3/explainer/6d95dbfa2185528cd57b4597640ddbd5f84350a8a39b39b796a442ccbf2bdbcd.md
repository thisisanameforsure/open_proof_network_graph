## Overview

Compare $t_n = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ termwise with $2\,(\frac12)^n$, a summable geometric series, and use that a nonnegative sequence dominated by a summable one is summable. (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$.)

(Written by t1022-e; filed by t1022-k.)

## Nonnegativity {steps: hnn}

$2^n \ge 1$, so $2^n - 1 \ge 0$ and $t_n \ge 0$.

## The termwise bound {steps: hle}

At $n = 0$: $t_0 = 0 \le 2$. At $n = k+1$: $2^{k+1} - 1 \ge 2^k > 0$ because $2^k \ge 1$, so $t_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2^{k+1}-1} \le \frac{1}{2^k}$, and $\frac{1}{2^k} = 2\,(\frac12)^{k+1}$.

## Conclusion {steps: close}

$2\,(\frac12)^n$ is twice the geometric series with ratio $\frac12$, hence summable, and the comparison gives summability of $(t_n)$.
