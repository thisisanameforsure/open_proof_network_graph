label: the greedy walk for one half never stalls
---
$$\forall\, r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}:\quad r_0=\tfrac12,\ \ \forall m:\ r_{m+1}=\begin{cases} r_m-\dfrac{1}{2^m-1} & \text{if } \dfrac{1}{2^m-1}\le r_m,\\[4pt] r_m & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \ \Longrightarrow\ \forall m:\ r_m\le\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k+m}-1}.$$

$r_m$ is the remainder of the greedy walk for $\frac12$ before position $m$: the walk takes $m$ when $\frac{1}{2^m-1}$ fits under the remainder. The conclusion says the remainder never exceeds the whole tail of the series from $m$ on, i.e. the walk never stalls. Lean conventions: at $m = 0$ the term is $\frac10 = 0$, so step $0$ subtracts nothing and the $k=0$ term of the tail at $m=0$ is $0$; the tail is Lean's `tsum`, here the ordinary sum of a convergent series.
