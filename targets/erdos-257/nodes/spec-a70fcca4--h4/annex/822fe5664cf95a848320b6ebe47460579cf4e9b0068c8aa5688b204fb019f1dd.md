What is specific to 1/2? A Diophantine form of this node, where 1/2's arithmetic is visible, and what the literature says (agent t1022-p, 2026-10-22).

Notation as in the annex by t1022-a on this node: e_n = 1/(2^n - 1) for n >= 1, T_m = sum_{n >= m} e_n, r_m the greedy remainder of 1/2, A the greedy set. Write g_m = e_m - T_{m+1}, the width of the stall window at step m. Since g_m - g_{m+1} = e_m - 2 e_{m+1} = 1/((2^m - 1)(2^{m+1} - 1)) > 0, g is strictly decreasing, and g_m = (2/3) 4^-m (1 + o(1)).

1. A two-sided Diophantine form. For every real x with 0 <= x <= T_1: x is a subsum sum_{n in B} e_n for some B (equivalently, the greedy walk of x never stalls) if and only if no finite nonempty F has 0 < sum_{a in F} e_a - x < g_{max F}.

Proof. If the walk stalls at m (m skipped and r_m > T_{m+1}), take F = (A below m) together with m: then sum_F e - x = e_m - r_m lies strictly between 0 and e_m - T_{m+1} = g_m. Conversely suppose x = sum_{n in B} e_n and F is finite with sum_F e > x. By the gap lemma (node spec-ddd99827: each term exceeds the sum of all later ones) subsums are ordered lexicographically, so the first index n where F and B differ has n in F and n not in B, and n <= max F. Then sum_F e - x >= e_n - T_{n+1} = g_n >= g_{max F}. QED.

So this node says exactly: no finite sum of distinct 1/(2^a - 1) with largest index m exceeds 1/2 by less than about (2/3) 4^-m. It is a one-sided, lacunary-family Diophantine approximation statement about the single number 1/2. Among the 2^(m-1) finite sums with largest index m, spread over an interval of length about 1, the expected number in a window of width g_m is about 2^m 4^-m, summable in m: this is the Borel-Cantelli heuristic of t1022-a's annex, now as a counting statement.

2. Where the denominator 2 of 1/2 helps, and where it stops. sum_F e - 1/2 is a rational whose denominator divides 2 lcm_{a in F}(2^a - 1); if it is not zero it is at least 1/(2 lcm). So a stall at m is impossible as soon as 2 lcm_{a in F}(2^a - 1) <= 1/g_m, with F = (A below m) plus m. For 1/2's own greedy set (2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 20, 21, 26, ...), checked exactly for m <= 60, this rules out a stall at m = 2, ..., 7, 12, 14, 18, 19, 20, and at no m from 21 to 60. The early prefix is divisor-rich (lcm small) because the first remainders telescope: 1/2 - 1/3 = 1/6, 1/6 - 1/7 = 1/42, 1/42 - 1/63 = 1/126, 1/126 - 1/127 = 1/16002, three of these steps (a = 2, 3, 7) being 1/(2^a - 2) - 1/(2^a - 1) = 1/((2^a - 2)(2^a - 1)), so every early remainder is 1 over a product of small Mersenne-type factors; after that the next element is 14 and the pattern ends. This matches the lead's numerics, where the only steps of 1/2's walk that came near a stall were steps 1 to 7 (step 1 is immediate: r_1 = 1/2 < T_2). Past m = 20 the bound never applies again: log2 of the lcm is 36.8, 285.9, 877.7 at m = 20, 40, 60, against the roughly 2m bits the window needs, and the denominators grow like 2^(c m^2), so a Liouville-type argument misses by a factor 2^(Theta(m^2)). Any proof must instead bound |sum_F e - 1/2| from below by about 4^-m for rationals of height 2^(Theta(m^2)), i.e. by height^(-Theta(1/m)), far beyond any known Diophantine lower bound (Liouville gives height^-1). The same holds for every rational x with a small denominator, so nothing here distinguishes 1/2 from j/2^k after the first steps.

3. The carry. The lead's carry into position m is floor(Phi(m)), Phi(m) = sum_{a in A, a < m} 2^(m mod a)/(2^a - 1) (t1022-a's annex). For 1/2, "carry 0 at m implies a skip in (m, m+3]" holds to depth 6000 (68 occurrences of carry 0 up to 3000). It is not structural: among the 512 odd j/2^k with k <= 9 whose walks are alive at depth 300 (the same dyadic structure as 1/2), it fails for 34%, and "skip within 5" fails for 8.6%. Like every finite-window property found today (A, F, J), it holds for 1/2 at the rate of a typical point.

- Kovac and Tao, "On several irrationality problems for Ahmes series", arXiv:2406.17593v4 (14 Jul 2025). Section 2.1.2, p. 4, quotes Erdos [Math. Student 36 (1968), p. 222]: "I know no example ... for which sum 1/(t^{n_i} - 1) is rational, though it seems likely that this can happen", and Erdos-Graham (1980), p. 62. Also p. 4: for each t >= 2 the subsums are distinct and form a Cantor set "in the topological sense", so at most countably many are rational. Remarks 3.1 (p. 11) and 4.1 (p. 13): each term exceeds its tail, hence at most one representation. On p. 5 they write "We do not know how to solve the original question implied by Erdos. In fact, it seems difficult, as the subseries ... already incorporate various delicate series for special cases of the set A". Nothing on 1/2 or on Lebesgue measure.
- erdosproblems.com/257 forum. Kovac, 9 Aug 2025: the subsum set is a fat Cantor set, so it "'almost surely' contains, say, at least one of the dyadic rationals 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, ...", but this cannot be made rigorous (Boes-Darst-Erdos: fat Cantor sets with no rationals). Kovac, 25 Aug 2025: base 2 is special; for t >= 3 the subsum set has measure zero. williamwkcook, 11 Sep 2026, a comment on an AI-assisted note that the author calls unreviewed: with c_x(n) = #{a in A_x : a | n} and the integer defect Q_N = floor(2^N x) - sum_{n <= N} c_x(n) 2^(N-n), "membership in the base-two subsum set is equivalent to infinitely many repairs" (Q_{N+1} <= Q_N), "or, equivalently, a repair in every [K, K+2 floor(sqrt K)+12)", and "members satisfy 0 <= Q_N <= 2 sqrt N + 4". The note studies 1/2 and 1/21 "without establishing the repairs for either target", and says these arguments are "not end-to-end Lean theorems". Q_N equals the integer I_N of t1022-a's annex (x_N = I_N - Phi(N)) minus 1 when N is in A, so this is a reformulation of the carry recursion, not a link to another problem. Its claims are unreviewed; for 1/2 to N = 3000 they hold numerically (Q_N between 1 and 11, mean 3.48, a repair at least every 6 steps; script P/cook_Q.py). They also hold for all 1227 reduced p/q with q <= 50 whose walks are infinite or stall, to depth 300 (alive walks: the bound and the window hold, max Q_N = 9; stalled walks: no repair more than 40 steps after the stall; P/cook_Q_all.py). Like every criterion here, they hold equally for typical points and say nothing specific to 1/2.
- Crmaric and Kovac, arXiv:2504.18712v1: 1/(2^n - 1) "leads to a so-called fat Cantor set" (an observation). Measure exactly 1: Nitecki, arXiv:1106.3779v2, Thm 4(1) (after Hornich 1941), proved on this target as variant-f9c7b81b.
