## Overview

Write $e_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $e_0 = \frac10 = 0$) and $T_m = \sum_{k\ge0} e_{k+m}$ for the tail of the series from $m$ on. The greedy remainder satisfies $r_0 = \frac12$ and $r_{m+1} = r_m - e_m$ if $e_m \le r_m$, else $r_{m+1} = r_m$. The statement to prove is $r_m \le T_m$ for every $m$. The proof is an induction on $m$: a step that takes $e_m$ lowers both sides by $e_m$, so only a skipped step can break the bound, and there the hole supplies $r_m \le \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m}$, which is at most $T_{m+1}$ by a termwise comparison with two geometric series.

**The hole.** Stated for every sequence $r$ obeying the greedy recursion from $\frac12$: if $r_m < e_m$ (step $m$ is skipped) then $r_m \le \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot 4^m}$. It is left open (node spec-a70fcca4--h4--h1).

**Convergence of the series.** Every term is nonnegative, since $2^n \ge 1$. For $n \ge 1$, $2^n \ge 2$ gives $2^n - 1 \ge 2^{n-1}$, so $e_n \le 2\cdot(\tfrac12)^n$; at $n = 0$ the term is $0$. Comparison with $2\sum_n (\tfrac12)^n$ shows $\sum_n e_n$ converges.

**The tail identity.** The shifted series $k \mapsto e_{k+m}$ converges because the series does, and splitting off its first term gives $T_m = e_m + \sum_{k\ge0} e_{k+1+m} = e_m + T_{m+1}$.

**A lower bound for the next tail.** For real $x \ge 2$, $\frac1x + \frac1{x^2} \le \frac{1}{x-1}$, because clearing denominators it reads $x^2 - 1 \le x^2$. With $x = 2^{k+m+1} \ge 2$ this gives $e_{k+m+1} \ge (\tfrac12)^{k+m+1} + (\tfrac14)^{k+m+1}$ for every $k$. Summing the two geometric series, $\sum_{k\ge0}\big((\tfrac12)^{m+1}(\tfrac12)^k + (\tfrac14)^{m+1}(\tfrac14)^k\big) = \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m}$, and the termwise comparison of convergent series gives $\frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m} \le T_{m+1}$.

**The induction.** At $m = 0$: $T_0 = e_0 + T_1 = 0 + T_1 \ge 1 + \frac13$ by the lower bound at $m = 0$, so $r_0 = \frac12 \le T_0$. From $m$ to $m+1$, unfold the recursion. If $e_m \le r_m$, then $r_{m+1} = r_m - e_m \le T_m - e_m = T_{m+1}$ by the induction hypothesis and the tail identity. Otherwise $r_m < e_m$, $r_{m+1} = r_m$, and the hole followed by the lower bound gives $r_m \le \frac{1}{2^m} + \frac{1}{3\cdot4^m} \le T_{m+1}$.
