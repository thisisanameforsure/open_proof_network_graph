## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $A$ be an infinite set of positive integers and suppose there is $N$ such that $2n\le m$ whenever $n<m$ are in $A$ and $n\ge N$. Write $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational. Call a set *lacunary* when $2n\le m$ for all its elements $n<m$.

The private lemma `r2_main` is the case $N=0$. It combines two lemmas. The first is the Liouville-type criterion `r2_liou`: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$ and $2^CL\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* (If the sum were rational with denominator $q$, take $C=4q$: the tail $T$ from $m$ on is positive and at most $4/2^m$ by `lac_tail_le`, and $qLT$ is an integer by `liou_int`, yet $0<qLT<1$.)

The second, `r2_hyp`, supplies $m$ and $L$ for a lacunary $A$. It uses `r2_weighted`: if $F$ is a finite subset of $A$ lying below $m\in A$, then

$$\sum_{a\in F}a+\#\{a\in F:2a\notin A\}\le m,$$

by induction on the largest element $b$ of $F$: the rest contributes at most $b$, and $2b\le m$, with $2b<m$ when $2b\notin A$. Now fix $C$.

*If infinitely many $a\in A$ have $2a\notin A$*, choose $C+1$ of them, let $m$ be the largest, $F=\{a\in A:a<m\}$ and $L=\prod_{a\in F}(2^a-1)$. Then $L\le 2^{\sum_F a}$ and $\sum_F a+C\le m$.

*Otherwise* there is $b$ with $2a\in A$ for every $a\in A$ above $b$ (`hn0`). Let $F_0=\{a\in A:a\le b\}$, pick $n\in A$ with $n>b+1+C+\sum_{F_0}a$, and take $m=2n$, $L=\prod_{a\in F_0}(2^a-1)\cdot(2^n-1)$. An element $a\in A$ with $b<a<2n$ satisfies $a\le n$ by lacunarity; all $a2^j$ are in $A$ (`hdbl`); taking the largest $a2^j\le n$, lacunarity forces $a2^j=n$ (`heq`), so $a\mid n$ and $(2^a-1)\mid(2^n-1)$. Finally $C+\sum_{F_0}a+n\le 2n$.

## The part of $A$ from $N$ on {steps: hBinf hB0 hBr hirr}

Let $B'=\{a\in A:a\ge N\}$. It is $A$ minus the finite set of numbers below $N$, so it is infinite; it does not contain $0$ because $A$ does not; and the hypothesis says exactly that $B'$ is lacunary. By `r2_main`, $\sum_{n\in B'}t_n$ is irrational.

## Splitting the sum {steps: hsA hsB hpt hsplit}

Let $F=\{a\in A:a<N\}$, a finite set. The series $\sum_n t_n$ converges (`lac_summable`), so the sums over $A$ and over $B'$ converge. For every $n$,

$$\mathbf 1_A(n)\,t_n=\mathbf 1_F(n)\,t_n+\mathbf 1_{B'}(n)\,t_n,$$

checked in the four cases $n\in A$ or not, $n<N$ or not (`hpt`). Summing over $n$,

$$\sum_{n\in A}t_n=\sum_{a\in F}t_a+\sum_{n\in B'}t_n .$$

## The finite part is rational, and the conclusion {steps: hq}

The finite sum $\sum_{a\in F}t_a$ is the image in $\mathbb R$ of the rational number $\sum_{a\in F}\frac{1}{2^a-1}$. So if $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ were a rational $r$, then $\sum_{n\in B'}t_n=r-\sum_{a\in F}t_a$ would be rational, contradicting `hirr`.
