label: eventually lacunary of ratio two ⇒ irrational
---
$$\forall A \subseteq \mathbb{N}:\quad A \text{ infinite},\ 0 \notin A,\ \bigl(\exists N\ \forall m, n \in A:\ N \le n < m \Rightarrow 2n \le m\bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_{n \in A} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \notin \mathbb{Q}.$$

The sum is over the elements of $A$ (Lean's `tsum` over the subtype); it converges because $\frac{1}{2^n-1} \le 2^{1-n}$ for $n \ge 1$. The hypothesis $0 \notin A$ excludes the one index where Lean's division by $2^0 - 1 = 0$ would give the junk value $0$. "Irrational" is Mathlib's `Irrational`: the real number is not the image of any rational.

The docstring's examples are to be read with $0$ left out: $\{2^k - 1\}$ means $\{2^k - 1 : k \ge 1\}$, since $k = 0$ gives $0$, which the hypothesis $0 \notin A$ excludes; likewise a finite set joined to an example must not contain $0$.
