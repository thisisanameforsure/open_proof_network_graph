$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite},\ 0\notin A:\quad \exists N\ \ \forall m,n\in A:\ \ N\le n<m\ \Longrightarrow\ 2n\le m.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\{2^k : k\in\mathbb{N}\}$ and $N=0$: the powers of two are infinitely many, none is $0$, and $2^j<2^i$ gives $j<i$, hence $2\cdot 2^j=2^{j+1}\le 2^i$.
