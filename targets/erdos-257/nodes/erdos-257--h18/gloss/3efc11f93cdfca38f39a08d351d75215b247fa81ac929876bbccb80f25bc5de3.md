$$A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite} \Rightarrow A \in \mathcal{C}_1 \cup \cdots \cup \mathcal{C}_{15} \ \lor\ \sum_{n \in A} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \notin \mathbb{Q}$$

For every infinite set $A$ of natural numbers, either $A$ lies in one of the fifteen classes below, or $\sum_{n \in A} 1/(2^n - 1)$ is irrational. Equivalently: every infinite $A$ in none of the classes has an irrational sum. The classes are the special cases of Erdős 257 already on the graph, each a lemma of the root:

1. $A = \mathbb{N}$;
2. $A = \{a + dn : n \ge 0\}$ for some $a \ge 0$, $d \ge 1$;
3. $A$ eventually periodic: for some $p \ge 1$ and $N$, $n \in A \iff n + p \in A$ for all $n \ge N$;
4. $A$ pairwise coprime with $\sum_{a \in A} 1/a < \infty$;
5. $A$ = the squarefree numbers;
6. $A = \{n^k : n \ge 0\}$ for some $k \ge 1$;
7. $A$ = the sums of two squares $\{a^2 + b^2\}$;
8. $A$ = the primes;
9. $0 \notin A$ and, for some $N$, $n < m$ in $A$ with $n \ge N$ implies $m \ge 2n$;
10. $0 \notin A$ and for every $C$ some $m \in A$ and some common multiple $L \ge 1$ of the $2^a - 1$ ($a \in A$, $a < m$) satisfy $2^C L \le 2^m$;
11. $0 \notin A$ and for every $C$ some $m \in A$ satisfies $m \ge C + \sum_{a \in A,\, a < m} a$;
12. $A = \{2^k\} \cup \{3^k\}$;
13. $A = \{a^k\} \cup \{b^k\}$ for some $2 \le a < b$;
14. $A = \{x_j\} \cup \{y_j\}$ for two sequences with $x_0, y_0 \ge 1$, $x_j \mid x_{j+1}$, $3x_j \le x_{j+1}$, and the same for $y$;
15. $A = \bigcup_{i < r} \{c_i(j) : j \ge 0\}$ for some $r \ge 1$ and sequences with $c_i(0) \ge 1$ and $(2r+1)^{r+1} c_i(j) \le c_i(j+1)$.

Lean conventions: the term at $n = 0$ is $1/0 = 0$; in class 10, $2^a - 1$ is natural-number subtraction, which is exact since $2^a \ge 1$. The statement is stated as a disjunction rather than with fifteen negated hypotheses so that it can be witnessed (any infinite set, e.g. $\mathbb{N}$, meets its one hypothesis).
