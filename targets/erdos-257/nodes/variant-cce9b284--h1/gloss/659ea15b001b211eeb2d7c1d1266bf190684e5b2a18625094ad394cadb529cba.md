$$\exists\, x\in\mathbb{Q},\ r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R},\ q:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}:\quad x\ge 0,\quad r_0=x,\quad \forall m:\ \ q_m=\Bigl\lfloor r_m\Big/\tfrac{1}{2^m-1}\Bigr\rfloor\ \ \text{and}\ \ r_{m+1}=r_m-\frac{q_m}{2^m-1}.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $x=0$ and $r_m=0$, $q_m=0$ for every $m$: each quotient is $0$, its floor is $0$, and nothing is subtracted. The floor is the natural-number floor. In Lean the term at $m=0$ is $\frac10=0$, and dividing by it gives $0$.
