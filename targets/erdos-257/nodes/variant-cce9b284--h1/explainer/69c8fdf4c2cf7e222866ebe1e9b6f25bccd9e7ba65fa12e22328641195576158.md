## Notation and the helper lemmas

Write $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$) and $T_{m+1}=\sum_{j\ge0}t_{m+1+j}$. The walk starts from a rational $x\ge 0$: $r_0 = x$, $q_m = \lfloor r_m/t_m\rfloor$ and $r_{m+1} = r_m - q_m t_m$. Let $A=\{m : q_m\ne 0 \text{ and } r_{m+1}\le T_{m+1}\}$ and let $\mathbf 1_A$ be its indicator. The statement is

$$\sum_{m\in A} t_m \;=\; x-\sum_{m}\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)\,t_m .$$

Five private lemmas come before the theorem. Each $t_m$ is nonnegative. For $t\ge0$ and $r\ge 0$, $\lfloor r/t\rfloor\, t\le r$ (when $t = 0$ the left side is $0$). Hence every remainder is nonnegative, by induction on $m$. The terms $t_N$ tend to $0$. Since $r_N/t_N<\lfloor r_N/t_N\rfloor+1$ for $N \ge 1$, the next remainder satisfies $0\le r_{N+1}<t_N$, so $r_N\to0$. Finally the partial sums telescope, $\sum_{i<N}q_i t_i = \sum_{i<N}(r_i-r_{i+1}) = x-r_N$, and the terms are nonnegative, so $\sum_m q_m t_m = x$.

## The walk uses up $x$ {steps: hf hfnn}

By the last helper lemma the series $\sum_m q_m t_m$ converges to $x$. Its terms are nonnegative.

## The chosen terms are summable {steps: hg_le hg_nn hg}

For every $m$, $0\le \mathbf 1_A(m)\,t_m\le q_m t_m$: if $m\in A$ then $q_m\ne0$, so $q_m\ge 1$; if $m\notin A$ the middle term is $0$. By comparison with the convergent series of the previous step, $\sum_m \mathbf 1_A(m)\,t_m$ converges.

## The sum over $A$ as a sum over all $m$ {steps: hL}

A sum over the members of a set is the sum of the indicator times the term:

$$\sum_{m\in A}t_m=\sum_m \mathbf 1_A(m)\,t_m .$$

## The subtracted terms {steps: hR}

Both series $\sum_m q_m t_m$ and $\sum_m\mathbf 1_A(m)t_m$ converge, so their termwise difference does, and

$$\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m=\sum_m q_mt_m-\sum_m\mathbf 1_A(m)t_m = x-\sum_m\mathbf 1_A(m)\,t_m .$$

## Conclusion {steps: close}

Substituting the last two identities, the claim reads $\sum_m\mathbf 1_A(m)t_m = x-\bigl(x-\sum_m\mathbf 1_A(m)t_m\bigr)$, which holds.
