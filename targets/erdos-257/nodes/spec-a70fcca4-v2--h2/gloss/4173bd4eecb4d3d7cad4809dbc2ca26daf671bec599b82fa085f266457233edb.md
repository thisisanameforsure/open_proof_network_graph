$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N},\ p\in\mathbb{Z},\ q\ge 1,\ C\in\mathbb{Z}:\quad \forall j:\ \ 0\ \le\ p\,2^j-q\sum_{m=0}^{j} d_A(m)\,2^{j-m}\ \le\ C\,(j+1),\qquad d_A(m)=\#\{a\in A: m>0,\ a\mid m\}.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\emptyset$, $p=0$, $q=1$ and $C=0$: every count $d_A(m)$ is $0$, so the middle quantity is $0$ for every $j$. In Lean the lower bound and the upper bound are two separate hypotheses, and $C$ belongs to the second.
