## Setting up {steps: s1 hqR}

Fix a set $A$ of natural numbers, an integer $p$ and a natural number $q>0$. Write $D(m)$ for the number of elements of $A$ that divide $m$ (with $D(0)=0$, since the Lean asks $0<m$), and

$$Z_j \;=\; p\,2^j \;-\; q\sum_{m=0}^{j} D(m)\,2^{\,j-m}\qquad(j\ge 0),$$

an integer, the carry at position $j$. The two hypotheses are $Z_j\ge 0$ for every $j$, and $Z_j\le C\,(j+1)$ for every $j$ with one integer $C$, which the proof takes out of the existential. It also records $q>0$ as a real number.

## The partial sums, written with the carry {steps: key}

For every $j$,

$$\sum_{m=0}^{j}\frac{D(m)}{2^m} \;=\; \frac pq \;-\; \frac{Z_j}{q\,2^j}.$$

Indeed, for $m\le j$ we have $2^j = 2^{\,j-m}\,2^m$, so $\sum_{m\le j} D(m)\,2^{\,j-m} = 2^j\sum_{m\le j} D(m)/2^m$ as real numbers; substituting this into the definition gives $Z_j = p\,2^j - q\,2^j\sum_{m\le j}D(m)/2^m$, and dividing by $q\,2^j$ and rearranging gives the identity.

## A linear bound over a power of two tends to zero {steps: hlin}

$$\frac{n+1}{2^n}\;\longrightarrow\;0 .$$

It is the sum of $n/2^n$, which tends to $0$ because a power of $n$ is negligible against $2^n$, and $(1/2)^n$, which tends to $0$.

## The carry term vanishes {steps: hZt}

By the two hypotheses, for every $j$

$$0\;\le\;\frac{Z_j}{q\,2^j}\;\le\;\frac{C}{q}\cdot\frac{j+1}{2^j},$$

and the right-hand side tends to $0$ by the previous step. So $Z_j/(q\,2^j)\to 0$ by squeezing.

## The series converges to $p/q$ {steps: hpart hs close}

By the identity for the partial sums and the last step, $\sum_{m<n} D(m)/2^m \to p/q$ as $n\to\infty$ (the Lean shifts the index by one to match the sum over $m\le j$). The terms $D(m)/2^m$ are nonnegative, so convergence of the partial sums is convergence of the series:

$$\sum_{m\ge 0}\frac{D(m)}{2^m} \;=\; \frac pq .$$
