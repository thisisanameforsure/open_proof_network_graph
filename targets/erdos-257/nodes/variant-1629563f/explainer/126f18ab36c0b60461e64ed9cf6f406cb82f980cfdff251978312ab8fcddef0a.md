## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Write $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. Let $A$ be an infinite set of natural numbers with $0\notin A$, and suppose that for every $C\in\mathbb N$ there are $m\in A$ and an integer $L>0$ such that $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for every $a\in A$ with $a<m$, and $2^C L\le 2^m$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

The idea is Liouville's: if the sum were a rational with denominator $q$, then multiplying the tail of the series from $m$ on by $qL$ would give a positive integer, while the tail is so small that this product is less than $1$.

Five private lemmas come before the theorem. `lac_f_nonneg`: $t_n\ge 0$. `lac_f_le`: $t_n\le 2\cdot(1/2)^n$ (for $n=0$ the left side is $\frac10=0$ in Lean). `lac_summable`: hence $\sum_n t_n$ converges, by comparison with a geometric series. `lac_tail_le`: for any set $A$ and any $m$, $\sum_{n\ge m,\ n\in A}t_n\le 4\cdot(1/2)^m$, by the same comparison and the sum of the geometric series. `liou_int`: if $F$ is a finite set of nonzero numbers and $2^a-1$ divides $L$ for every $a\in F$, then $L\sum_{a\in F}t_a$ is an integer, because each $L\,t_a=L/(2^a-1)$ is one.

## Supposing the sum is rational {steps: hsum hrA s3 hm1}

Suppose $\sum_{n\in A}t_n=r$ with $r$ rational. The series over $A$ is the series over all $n$ of $\mathbf 1_A(n)\,t_n$, which converges by `lac_summable`. Let $q$ be the denominator of $r$, and apply the hypothesis with $C=4q$: there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$, and $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$. Since $0\notin A$, we have $m\ge 1$.

## Splitting off the tail {steps: hsplit hfin hTs hTpos hTle hrT}

Let $F=\{a\in A: a<m\}$ and let

$$T=\sum_{n\ge m}\mathbf 1_A(n)\,t_n$$

be the tail. A convergent series is its first $m$ terms plus the rest, and the first $m$ terms of $\mathbf 1_A(n)t_n$ are the sum over $F$, so

$$r=\sum_{a\in F}t_a+T .$$

The tail is positive: its terms are nonnegative and its first term is $t_m>0$, because $m\in A$ and $m\ge 1$. And $T\le 4\cdot(1/2)^m$ by `lac_tail_le`.

## $qLT$ is a positive integer {steps: s11 hPpos hqr hZ hqpos hpos hge}

Every $a\in F$ is nonzero and has $(2^a-1)\mid L$, so by `liou_int` there is an integer $N$ with $L\sum_{a\in F}t_a=N$. Also $r\,q=\mathrm{num}(r)$, the numerator of $r$. Substituting $T=r-\sum_{a\in F}t_a$,

$$q\,L\,T \;=\; L\cdot\mathrm{num}(r)-q\,N,$$

an integer. Since $q>0$, $L>0$ and $T>0$, it is positive, and a positive integer is at least $1$:

$$q\,L\,T\ge 1 .$$

## $qLT$ is less than one {steps: hLm' hbig h2m hupper}

From $T\le 4\cdot(1/2)^m$ we get $qLT\le qL\cdot 4\,(1/2)^m$ (`hupper.hA1`). Multiplying by $2^{4q}$ and using $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$ and $2^m(1/2)^m=1$,

$$2^{4q}\cdot qL\cdot 4\,(1/2)^m\;\le\;4q\cdot 2^m(1/2)^m\;=\;4q$$

(`hupper.hA2`). Since $4q<2^{4q}$ (`hbig`), this forces $qL\cdot 4\,(1/2)^m<1$: otherwise the left side would be at least $2^{4q}>4q$. Hence $qLT<1$.

This contradicts $qLT\ge 1$ from the previous section, so the sum is not rational.
