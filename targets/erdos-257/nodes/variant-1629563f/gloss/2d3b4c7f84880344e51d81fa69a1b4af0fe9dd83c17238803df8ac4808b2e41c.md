$$\forall A \subseteq \mathbb{N}:\quad A \text{ infinite},\ 0 \notin A,\ \Bigl(\forall C \in \mathbb{N}\ \exists m \in A\ \exists L \in \mathbb{N}:\ L > 0,\ \bigl(\forall a \in A,\ a < m \Rightarrow (2^a - 1) \mid L\bigr),\ 2^C L \le 2^m\Bigr) \ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_{n \in A} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} \notin \mathbb{Q}.$$

In the hypothesis $2^a - 1$ is subtraction on natural numbers, which never truncates here since $2^a \ge 1$. The sum is over the elements of $A$; $0 \notin A$ excludes Lean's junk value at $n = 0$.

The docstring's example $\{2^k - 1\}$ is to be read with $k \ge 1$: at $k = 0$ it contains $0$, which the hypothesis $0 \notin A$ excludes.
