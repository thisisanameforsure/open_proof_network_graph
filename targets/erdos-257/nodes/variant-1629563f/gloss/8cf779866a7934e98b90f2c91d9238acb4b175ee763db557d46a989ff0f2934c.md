$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite},\ 0\notin A:\quad \forall C\ \exists m\in A\ \exists L\ge 1:\ \ (2^a-1)\mid L\ \ \forall a\in A,\ a<m,\quad\text{and}\quad 2^C L\le 2^m.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\{2^k : k\in\mathbb{N}\}$, and for each $C$ it takes $m=2^{C+1}$ and $L=2^{2^C}-1$. The elements of $A$ below $m$ are $2^j$ with $j\le C$, and $2^j\mid 2^C$ gives $(2^{2^j}-1)\mid L$; and $2^C L\le 2^{C+2^C}\le 2^m$.
