label: no rational is an infinite greedy sum (open core)

$$\forall\, r\in\mathbb{Q},\ \forall\, G\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\quad 0\notin G,\ \ \Bigl(\forall n\ge 1:\ n\in G \iff \frac{1}{2^n-1} \le r-\sum_{a\in G,\ a<n}\frac{1}{2^a-1}\Bigr),\ \ \sum_{a\in G}\frac{1}{2^a-1}=r \ \Longrightarrow\ G \text{ is finite}.$$

The middle hypothesis says $G$ is the greedy set for $r$: run through $n = 1, 2, \dots$ and take $n$ exactly when $\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ fits under what remains of $r$ after the terms already taken. Sums are Lean's `tsum`, in $\mathbb{R}$, and every such family is summable. With $\sum_G = r$ assumed, the statement is: no rational number is the sum of an infinite greedy set. Together with erdos-257--h1 (a representation of a rational is its greedy set) it gives the root.
