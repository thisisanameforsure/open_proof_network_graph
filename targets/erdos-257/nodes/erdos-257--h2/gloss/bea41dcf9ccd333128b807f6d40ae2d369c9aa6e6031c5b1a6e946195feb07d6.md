$$0 \notin G,\ G \text{ greedy for } r \in \mathbb{Q},\ \sum_{a\in G} \frac{1}{2^a-1} = r \implies G \text{ finite}$$

The open core of Erdős 257. Expected FALSE: Kovač (erdosproblems.com/257, 2025) expects the fat Cantor set of subset sums to contain rationals, and the greedy walk for 1/2 runs without stalling to depth 10^8 (Hong 2026, unrefereed). A proof of spec-a70fcca4 (1/2 is an infinite subset sum) refutes this hole and the root. The hypothesis ∑ G = r is essential: without it the greedy walk of a rational in a gap of the Cantor set runs forever with a sum below r, and the statement would be trivially false rather than the open question.
