label: some infinite set has a rational sum
---
$$\exists\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite},\ \ \exists\, s \in \mathbb{Q}:\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \;=\; s .$$

The sum is Lean's `tsum` over $A$ (summable for every $A$, so the ordinary sum). In Lean the term at $n = 0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so $A$ may contain $0$ at no cost; removing it leaves $A$ infinite and the sum unchanged.

The rational $s$ is not named: the statement asks only that some infinite set has a rational sum, which is the negation of the problem's statement that every such sum is irrational.
