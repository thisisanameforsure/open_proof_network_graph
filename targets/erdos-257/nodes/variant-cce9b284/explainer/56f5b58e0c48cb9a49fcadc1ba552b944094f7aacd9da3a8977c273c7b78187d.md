## What the assembly shows

Some infinite set $A$ has a rational sum $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$, provided some positive rational $x$ has a repair walk that chooses infinitely many terms and whose subtracted terms add up to a rational number. Write $t_m=\frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $t_0=\frac10=0$). The *walk* of a rational $x\ge0$ is

$$r_0=x,\qquad q_m=\lfloor r_m/t_m\rfloor,\qquad r_{m+1}=r_m-q_m\,t_m ,$$

so $q_m$ is the number of times $t_m$ is subtracted at index $m$. The *chosen set* is

$$A=\Bigl\{m:\ q_m\ne0\ \text{ and }\ r_{m+1}\le\sum_{j\ge0}t_{m+1+j}\Bigr\},$$

the indices where a term is taken and the remainder stays within the whole tail. The *repair sum* is $\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m$: every subtraction that is not the one chosen term.

The partial has two holes. As of 12 November 2026 the first is proved on the graph and the second is open, so this node is not proved. The second hole holds all of the difficulty: the partial exhibits no $x$ and gives no reason one exists. A proof of this node would refute Erdős problem 257.

## The walk accounts for $x$ {steps: hall.s1.h_account}

The first hole, proved on the graph: for the walk of any rational $x\ge0$,

$$\sum_{m\in A}t_m \;=\; x-\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m .$$

## A rational whose repair sum is rational {steps: hall.s2.h_walk}

The second hole, open: there are a rational $x>0$ and its walk $(r,q)$ such that the chosen set $A$ is infinite and

$$\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m=s$$

for some rational $s$. The partial's comment names two ways this could hold: only finitely many repairs, or infinitely many whose sum is rational all the same.

## The assembly {steps: s2 s3 close}

Take $x$, its walk and the rational $s$ from the second hole, and let $A$ be the chosen set, which is infinite. By the first hole and the value of the repair sum,

$$\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=x-s ,$$

a rational number. So $A$ and $x-s$ witness the statement.
