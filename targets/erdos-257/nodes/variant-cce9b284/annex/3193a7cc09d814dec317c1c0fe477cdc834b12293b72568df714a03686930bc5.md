# Existence instead of one number: the dyadic family of level L, what a count proves, and where it stops

Notes from one session (2026-11-12), drafted by a model at the suggestion of the target's owner: do not follow one rational; show that at least one of a family survives. Nothing here proves this node. The statements marked proved have paper proofs given or sketched below and were checked by exact computation where stated; none is formalized.

Notation as in the earlier annex on this node: $t_m=1/(2^m-1)$, $T_m=\sum_{k\ge m}t_k$, $S_F=\sum_{a\in F}t_a$, and a rational *dies at level $k$* when its greedy walk stalls there, that is, when it lies in the gap $(S_F+T_{k+1},\,S_F+t_k)$ below its prefix $F\subseteq[1,k-1]$.

## 1. The reduction

Fix $L$. The fractions $p/2^L$ with $p$ odd in $(0,T_1)$ are finitely many. If for every $N$ one of them has not died by level $N$, one of them never dies, and this node holds. About $2^{L-1}$ per unit length are alive at level $L$, and the heuristic expects about $\tfrac13\cdot 2^{L-k}$ deaths at each later level $k$, a total below 1. So it would be enough to prove, for one $L$, any bound on the number of deaths after level $L$ that is smaller than the number alive. The slack is a factor of about $2^L$.

## 2. Exact structure (proved; checked exhaustively for $L=10,14,18,20$)

Work modulo 1 (since $t_1=1$). Put $v_a=2^{\,L \bmod a}/(2^a-1)$ for $2\le a\le L$. The members alive at level $L$ correspond to the prefixes $F\subseteq[2,L]$, and the whole future of the member with prefix $F$ is decided by one number,

$$u_F \;=\; 1-\operatorname{frac}\Bigl(\sum_{a\in F}v_a\Bigr)\in(0,1):$$

the member dies at level $L+n$ exactly when $u_F$ lies in a window just to the right of a dyadic rational $y$ of exact level $n$, of length about $\tfrac23\,2^{-L}4^{-n}$, at an offset from $y$ that is less than $\tfrac13 2^{-L}(1+2^{-L})$ and is determined by $y$. Call the union of all these windows $U_L$: it is open, dense in $(0,1)$, and has measure about $\tfrac13 2^{-L}$. So

**this node holds if and only if, for some $L$, some subset sum $-\sum_{a\in F}v_a \bmod 1$, $F\subseteq[2,L]$, lies outside $U_L$.**

The map $F\mapsto u_F$ is injective, and $u_{F\cup\{a\}}=u_F-v_a$ exactly: nothing here is chaotic.

## 3. What the count proves (proved)

**Pair lemma.** If two members with prefixes $F\ne F'$ die at levels $L+n$ and $L+n'$, and $q=\operatorname{lcm}(2^a-1: a\in F\,\triangle\,F')$, then $2^{\max(n,n')}>3\cdot2^L/\bigl(q(1+2^{-L})\bigr)$.

Proof. Write $u_F=y+o$, $u_{F'}=y'+o'$ with $y,y'$ dyadic of level at most $N=\max(n,n')$ and offsets $o,o'\in(0,\tfrac13 2^{-L}(1+2^{-L}))$. Then $2^N(u_F-u_{F'})\equiv 2^N(o-o') \pmod 1$. The left side is a nonzero multiple of $1/q$ modulo 1 ($q$ is odd), so it is at least $1/q$ from the integers; the right side is smaller than $\tfrac13 2^{N-L}(1+2^{-L})$ in absolute value. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary.** Two members whose prefixes differ only in the element 2 have $q=3$, so at most one of them dies before level $2L$. Hence at most half of the family dies at levels strictly between $L$ and $2L$, and at least $2^{L-2}$ members per unit length are alive at level $2L-1$, for every $L$.

## 4. Where it stops (proved)

Past level $2L+2$ the pair lemma is empty, and no argument of its kind can replace it:

- every interval of length $2^{-n}$ contains a window of $U_L$ of level at most $L+n$, so knowing $u_F$ to within $2^{-L}/3$, which is all the lemma uses, excludes no death beyond level $2L+2$;
- for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are reals $\tilde v_a$ with $|\tilde v_a-v_a|<\varepsilon$ for which every subset sum $-\sum_F\tilde v_a \bmod 1$ lies in $U_L$. (For each $F$ the set of such $\tilde v$ is the preimage of a dense open set under an open map of tori, so it is dense and open; a finite intersection of dense open sets is dense.)

So a proof that some member survives must use the exact values $v_a$ at a resolution finer than $4^{-L}$. A bound from counting, equidistribution, monotonicity or injectivity survives a small perturbation of the $v_a$ and therefore cannot do it. The exact arithmetic available, that differences of the $u_F$ have denominators dividing $\operatorname{lcm}(2^a-1:a\le L)\approx2^{0.3L^2}$, is too weak by exactly the factor needed.

## 5. Exact tables

Every member of the family for $L\le22$ (3,369,484 members at $L=22$) was walked to level 130 in 318-bit fixed point with no ambiguous comparison. The number alive at level $L$ is $2^{L-1}$ per unit length. Deaths after level $L$, modulo 1: $17891/2^{15}$ at level 16, $4203/2^{15}$ at level 17, $59773/2^{17}$ at level 18, and no others. No member dies after level $2L$ for any $L\le22$. The heuristic expects about $2^{-L}/12$ such deaths for each $L$, so the empty table is what chance predicts and is not evidence of a rule. "Alive at level $2L$ implies alive forever", for one $L$, is this node.

## 6. Grading by level: the sum of the elements

The owner also proposed grading finite sets by the sum of their elements, so that level $n$ is the set of partitions of $n$ into distinct parts. Three remarks.

- The level is a height: $S_F$ has denominator dividing $\prod_{a\in F}(2^a-1)<2^n$. In this form Liouville's argument is node variant-41d1f19b. Its contrapositive: a set $A$ with a rational sum has a constant $C$ with $m<C+\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}a$ for every $m\in A$. That is Brown's criterion for a complete sequence, up to the constant, so the sums of the finite subsets of a counterexample have gaps of bounded length. The greedy set of $\tfrac12$ meets every level up to 3000 except 1 and 4.
- $\sum_{a\in A}t_a$ is the expected number of parts of a random partition with parts in $A$, a partition $\lambda$ having weight $2^{-|\lambda|}$ (the multiplicity of $a$ is geometric with mean $t_a$). For partitions into distinct parts the same expectation is $\sum_{a\in A}1/(2^a+1)$, the neighbouring series for which every rational in range is attained (root annex of this date, §4).
- Grading instead by the sum of the numbers $2^a-1$ gives nothing: each exceeds the sum of the smaller ones, so the grade of $F$ is $m-s_2(m)$ with $m=\sum_{a\in F}2^a$ and $s_2$ the binary digit sum, a relabelling of the set.

Height arguments reach only sparse sets: the prefix of the walk of $\tfrac12$ at step 40 has level 337, against the 80 that Liouville's argument would need.
