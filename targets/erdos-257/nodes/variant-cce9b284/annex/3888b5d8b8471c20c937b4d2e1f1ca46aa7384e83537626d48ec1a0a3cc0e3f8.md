# A walk-free sufficient condition: calm points, and the truncated problems $P_k$

Notes from one session (2026-11-12), drafted by a model with subagents. Nothing here proves this node. It gives a short lemma with proof, two consequences with proofs, and computations. The proofs were written and read in one sitting and are not formalized. The truncation idea of §4 and the remark that certificates of this kind need prime pairs are already in C. M. Hong's unreviewed note (Zenodo 23287959), which reports truncation orders up to 30; what is added here is the lemma in the form below, the reduction of §3 to a statement with no walk in it, and the checks.

Notation: $t_m=1/(2^m-1)$, $T_m=\sum_{k\ge m}t_k$. For a target $x$, $A_n$ is the greedy set of $x$ below $n+1$, $r_{n+1}=x-\sum_{a\in A_n}t_a$, and the walk *stalls at step $n+1$* when $T_{n+2}<r_{n+1}<t_{n+1}$.

## 1. The lemma

For $m>n$ put $W_n(m)=\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-i}\,\#\{a\in A_n: a\mid m+i\}$.

**Lemma.** Let $x=p/2^L$ and $n\ge L$. If the walk of $x$ stalls at step $n+1$, then for every $m$ with $n<m\le 2n$,

$$W_n(m)\;>\;1-\frac{2^{\,m-n}}{2\,(2^{n+1}-1)} .$$

In particular $W_n(m)>0.9997$ for $n<m\le 2n-10$.

Proof. Since $\sum_{a\in A_n}t_a=\sum_M 2^{-M}\#\{a\in A_n:a\mid M\}$ and $2^nx$ is an integer, $2^nr_{n+1}\equiv -Z \pmod 1$ with $Z=\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-i}\#\{a\in A_n:a\mid n+i\}$. A stall puts $2^nr_{n+1}$ in $(2^nT_{n+2},\,2^nt_{n+1})\subset(\tfrac12,\tfrac12+\theta_n)$ with $\theta_n=\frac{1}{2(2^{n+1}-1)}$, so $Z\equiv\tfrac12-\theta$ with $0<\theta<\theta_n$. For $m=n+l$, splitting the sum for $Z$ after $l$ terms gives $2^lZ=\text{integer}+W_n(m)$, hence $W_n(m)\equiv-2^l\theta \pmod 1$ for $l\ge1$. As $W_n(m)\ge0$ and $0<2^l\theta<1$ for $l\le n$, $W_n(m)\ge 1-2^l\theta>1-2^l\theta_n$. $\blacksquare$

So one $m$ in $(n,2n-10]$ with $W_n(m)\le0.99$ certifies that the walk does not stall at step $n+1$.

Checked: on 418 real stalls (all reduced $p/Q$, $Q<260$, stalling at a step $n+1\ge5$, exact fractions) the general form of the lemma has no violation. On the walk of $\tfrac12$ to $2\cdot10^7$ the quantity $\sum_i2^{-i}\#\{a\in A:a<m,\ a\mid m+i\}$, which bounds $W_n(m)$ for every $n<m$, is below $0.87$ at 518 points $m$, never more than a factor $1.6$ apart: every step from 16 to $1.98\cdot10^7$ has a certificate. The same holds for $\tfrac14,\tfrac18,\tfrac1{16},\tfrac1{32}$ (2615, 5499, 11776, 13016 points); for $\tfrac38$ there is one break, after $m=870$.

## 2. What a certificate point looks like

If $m+i=i\,q_i$ with $q_i$ prime for $1\le i\le I$, the divisors of $m+i$ are the divisors $d$ of $i$ and the numbers $d\,q_i$. The first kind contribute $\sum_i2^{-i}\#\{a\in A_n:a\mid i\}\le\sum_{a\in A_n}t_a\le x$ to $W_n(m)$; the second kind at most $\sum_i2^{-i}(d(i)-1)=E-1=0.6067$, where $E=\sum_n t_n$. For $x=\tfrac12$ the total can exceed 1, so a certificate there also needs one of the special primes $q_i$ to be outside $A$, a fact about the walk. For a small target it needs nothing about the walk. That is §3.

## 3. A sufficient condition with no walk in it

Fix $j\ge2$ and the target $x=2^{-j}$. Its greedy set lies in $[j+1,\infty)$, because $t_a\le2^{-j}$ exactly when $a\ge j+1$. Define, from divisors alone,

$$G_j(m)\;=\;\sum_{i\ge1}2^{-i}\,\#\{a:\ a\mid m+i,\ j<a<m\}.$$

Then $W_n(m)\le G_j(m)$ for every $n<m$. Call $m$ *calm* when $G_j(m)\le0.99$.

**Theorem.** Suppose the walk of $2^{-j}$ does not stall at any step up to $n_0+1$, and every $n\ge n_0$ has a calm $m$ with $n<m\le2n-10$. Then $2^{-j}=\sum_{a\in A}t_a$ for an infinite set $A$, and this node, and so the negation of the root, holds.

Proof. By the lemma no step stalls, so the greedy set sums to $2^{-j}$; it is infinite because a finite sum of the $t_a$ has odd denominator. $\blacksquare$

The hypothesis says: in every interval $(n,2n)$ there is an $m$ such that $m+1,m+2,\dots$ have, with weights $\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\dots$, fewer than $0.99$ divisors strictly between $j$ and $m$. It forces $m+1$ to be a prime or a prime times a number at most $j$, and similar shapes at $m+2,m+3,\dots$: a statement about prime constellations in every dyadic interval, with a divisor condition on the next few integers. It is what the prime tuples conjecture predicts with room to spare, and it is not known; even one prime pair $q,(q+1)/2$ in every interval is not known (the barrier in Hong's note). I have not derived the hypothesis from a standard form of the prime tuples conjecture: the tail $\sum_{i>I}2^{-i}d(m+i)$ at a constellation point needs a bound that the standard conjecture does not state.

Computed (divisor sieve to $10^8$, no walk):

| $j$ | calm points below $10^8$ | in $(10^7,10^8)$ | largest ratio of consecutive calm points beyond 3000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 393 | 224 | 1.82 |
| 100 | 1154 | 458 | 1.33 |
| 1000 | 6690 | 848 | 1.10 |

For $j=20$ the chain of calm points with $m'\le2m-10$ is unbroken from $m=1092$ to $m=99{,}229{,}860$; its only break is between 540 and 1092, and the walk of $2^{-20}$ was checked in exact arithmetic to step 4000. So the walk of $2^{-20}$ does not stall before step $9.9\cdot10^7$, by a certificate that uses no property of the walk beyond its first 4000 steps. The number of calm points per decade grows: 12, 24, 82, 224 from $10^4$ to $10^8$.

## 4. The truncated problems

Let $P_k$ ask for an infinite $A$ with $\sum_{a\in A}(2^{-a}+2^{-2a}+\dots+2^{-ka})=\tfrac12$. The truncated terms still exceed their tails, so $P_k$ has the same Cantor structure.

- If $P_k$ is solvable for infinitely many $k$, the root is false: the true sums of the solutions $A_k$ are within $2^{1-k}$ of $\tfrac12$, a subsequence converges to a set $A$, and the sum is continuous.
- The lemma holds for $P_k$ with $\#\{a\in A_n:a\mid m+i,\ (m+i)/a\le k\}$ in place of the count. If $m$ is a multiple of $\Lambda_k=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,k)$, a quotient $j\le k$ dividing $m+i$ divides $i$, so the count is at most $d(i)-1$ and $W\le E-1=0.6067$. Hence **the $P_k$ walk cannot stall at step $n+1$ once $[n+1,2n]$ contains a multiple of $\Lambda_k$**, and $P_k$ is decided by a finite computation.
- A subagent ran that computation in exact integer arithmetic for $2\le k\le16$ (up to step 360,362 with 720,924 bits) and reports no stall and no ambiguous comparison: for each such $k$ an infinite $A_k$ with truncated sum exactly $\tfrac12$. I did not repeat these runs. Hong's note reports orders up to 30.

## 5. What this changes and what it does not

Every earlier form of the open core asks one number to avoid a target of relative size $2^{-n}$ at every scale, and a small perturbation of the terms destroys any counting argument (annex on this node of the same date, §4). The lemma uses the exact arithmetic once and leaves a condition with slack: one point in each interval $(n,2n)$ where a weighted divisor count is below an absolute constant, among many such points. For the target $2^{-j}$ that condition does not mention the walk. It is still an unproved statement at every scale, of prime-constellation type, and sieve methods alone cannot give it.
