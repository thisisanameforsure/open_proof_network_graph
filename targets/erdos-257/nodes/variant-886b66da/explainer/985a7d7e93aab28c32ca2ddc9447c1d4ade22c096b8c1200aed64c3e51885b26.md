## Overview

The proof of Luca–Tachiya's theorem for purely periodic sets, specialised to base $2$, reached through a general form of Erdős's 1948 argument, and then a finite change to pass to eventually periodic sets. An arithmetic progression is eventually periodic. All of it is in private helpers (`irrational_series`, `pp_irr`, `ep_irr`); the theorem applies `ep_irr`.

## A general irrationality criterion

`irrational_series` is Erdős's 1948 argument for $A = \mathbb{N}$ abstracted to a counting function $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$: the series $\sum_n f(n)/2^n$ is irrational provided (i) $f \le d$, the divisor-counting function; (ii) every tail has a positive term, $\forall j\ \exists i:\ f(j+i+1) > 0$; and (iii) for some $p$ and some $A_0 \ge 2$, whenever $S$ is a finite set of primes $q > p$ each dividing $n \ne 0$ exactly to the power $A_0 - 1$, then $2^{|S|} \mid f(n)$. As for $A = \mathbb{N}$: a rational value $a/b$ makes every scaled tail $T_j = b\sum_{i \ge 1} f(j+i)/2^i$ a positive integer (`tail_eq_Zint`, `tail_pos`); Bertrand primes above $2^m$ and the Chinese remainder theorem give $c$ with $m$ such primes dividing each of $c + tQ + 1, \dots, c + tQ + m$ exactly to the power $A_0 - 1$, so $T_{c+tQ+m} \ge 2^m$ for every $t$ (`Zint_dvd`); and since $f \le d$ the divisor-function average along a progression coprime to $Q$ (`sum_dd_AP_le`) and $f(n) \le n$ (`tail_upper`) make the average of these tails smaller than $2^m$ (`averaging_contra`), with $m \ge b$, $m \ge p$ and $15A_0 m^5 < 2^m$ (`exists_param`).

## Purely periodic sets

`pp_irr`: if $B \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is nonempty and $n \in B \iff n + p \in B$ for all $n$ (some $p > 0$), then $\sum_{n \in B} \frac{1}{2^n - 1}$ is irrational. Put $f_B(n) = \#\{e \mid n : e \in B\}$ (`fB` with $u = 1$). Then:

- `lambert_B`: $\sum_{n \in B} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} = \sum_n f_B(n)/2^n$, by expanding each term as a geometric series and regrouping by divisors.
- $f_B \le d$ (`fB_le_dd`), and every tail has a positive term because $B$ has an element $e_0$ and its multiples recur (`fB_pos_tail`).
- `two_pow_dvd_fB`, with $\varphi$ Euler's function and $A_0 = 2\varphi(p)$: if primes $q$ coprime to $p$ each divide $n$ exactly to the power $2\varphi(p) - 1$, then $2^{|S|} \mid f_B(n)$. For one such $q$, write $n = q^{a} r$ with $q \nmid r$, $a = 2\varphi(p) - 1$; the divisors of $n$ are $q^k e$ with $k \le a$, $e \mid r$ (`fB_prime_pow_mul`, for the counting function $f_{B,u}(n) = \#\{e \mid n : ue \in B\}$, which makes the induction over $S$ go through). Since $q^{\varphi(p)} \equiv 1 \pmod p$ and $B$ is $p$-periodic, whether $u q^k e \in B$ depends on $k$ only modulo $\varphi(p)$ (`fB_periodic`); as $k$ runs over $0, \dots, 2\varphi(p) - 1$ each residue occurs exactly twice, so each such prime contributes a factor $2$.

Primes $q > p$ are coprime to $p$, so the criterion applies with $A_0 = 2\varphi(p)$.

## Eventually periodic sets

`ep_irr`: if $A$ is infinite and $n \in A \iff n + p \in A$ for all $n \ge N$ (some $p > 0$), then $\sum_{n \in A} \frac{1}{2^n - 1}$ is irrational. Let $B = \{n : n + pN \in A\}$. Then $B$ is purely $p$-periodic, $A$ and $B$ agree from $N$ on (`hiter`), and $B$ is nonempty because $A$ is infinite. The two sums differ by $\sum_{n < N} \bigl(1_A(n) - 1_B(n)\bigr) \frac{1}{2^n - 1}$, a rational number, so the irrationality of the sum over $B$ (`pp_irr`) gives that of the sum over $A$.

## Arithmetic progressions

The theorem, for $a$ and $d > 0$: the set $\{a + dn : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ is infinite because $n \mapsto a + dn$ is injective, and for $n \ge a$, $n$ is in it exactly when $n + d$ is (if $n + d = a + dk$ then $k \ge 1$, since $n \ge a$). So `ep_irr` applies with period $d$ and threshold $a$.
