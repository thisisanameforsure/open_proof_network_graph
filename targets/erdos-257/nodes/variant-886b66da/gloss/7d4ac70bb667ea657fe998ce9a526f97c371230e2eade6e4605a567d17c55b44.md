$$\Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite}:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr) \;\Longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\forall\, a, d \in \mathbb{N},\ d > 0:\ \sum_{m \,\in\, \{a + dn \,:\, n \in \mathbb{N}\}} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr).$$

The root's statement implies this variant (relation `partial`): for $d > 0$ the map $n \mapsto a + dn$ is injective, so the progression $\{a, a+d, a+2d, \dots\}$ is infinite, and the root's statement applies to it.
