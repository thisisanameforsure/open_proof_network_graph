$$\forall\, a, d \in \mathbb{N},\ d \ge 1:\qquad \sum_{m \in \{a + dn \,:\, n \ge 0\}} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \ \text{ is irrational}.$$

Every infinite arithmetic progression with first term $a \ge 0$ and difference $d \ge 1$. Sums are Lean's `tsum` over the set, the ordinary sum of a convergent nonnegative series; the term at $m = 0$ is Lean's $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$.
