label: a rational's greedy set is finite or co-finite
---
$$r \in \mathbb{Q},\ G = \{n \ge 1 : \tfrac{1}{2^n-1} \le r - \sum_{a \in G,\, a<n} \tfrac{1}{2^a-1}\} \Rightarrow G \text{ finite or } \{n \ge 1\} \setminus G \text{ finite}$$

the open core is a property of the greedy walk alone, with no infinite sum in it. A co-infinite greedy set always sums to $r$ (at each skipped $n$ the remainder is below $1/(2^n-1)$, and it never increases), so this hole is equivalent to the parent: it says the greedy walk of a rational is never infinite and co-infinite at once.
