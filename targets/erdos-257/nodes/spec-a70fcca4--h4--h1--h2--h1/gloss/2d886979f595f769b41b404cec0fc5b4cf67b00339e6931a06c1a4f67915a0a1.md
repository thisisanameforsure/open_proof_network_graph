$$\exists\, r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R},\ m\in\mathbb{N}:\quad r_0=\tfrac12,\qquad r_{m+1}=\begin{cases} r_m-\frac{1}{2^m-1} & \text{if } \frac{1}{2^m-1}\le r_m,\\ r_m & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad(\forall m),\qquad m>100,\qquad r_m<\frac{1}{2^m-1}.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It defines $r$ by the recursion itself and takes $m=101$. It computes $r_1,\dots,r_{101}$ as exact fractions, one step at a time, and compares $r_{101}$ with $\frac{1}{2^{101}-1}$. In Lean the term at $m=0$ is $\frac10=0$, so $r_1=r_0$.
