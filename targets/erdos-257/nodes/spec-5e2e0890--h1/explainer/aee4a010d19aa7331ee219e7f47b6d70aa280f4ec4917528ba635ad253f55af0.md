## Overview

The hypotheses about $A$ and $B$ are introduced and not used: the conclusion, that $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m - 1}$ is summable over $m \in \mathbb{N}$ (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$), is proved by comparison with a geometric series.

## The geometric series {steps: hgeom}

$m \mapsto 2\,(\tfrac12)^m$ is twice a geometric series of ratio $\tfrac12 < 1$, hence summable.

## Nonnegativity {steps: s2}

$2^m \ge 1$, so $2^m - 1 \ge 0$ and $t_m \ge 0$.

## The termwise bound {steps: s3}

At $m = 0$, $t_0 = 0 \le 2$. For $m \ge 1$, $2^m \ge 2$, so $2^m - 1 > 0$, and $t_m \le 2\,(\tfrac12)^m = \frac{2}{2^m}$ becomes, after clearing the positive denominators, $2^m \le 2(2^m - 1)$, that is $2 \le 2^m$. A nonnegative sequence bounded termwise by a summable one is summable.
