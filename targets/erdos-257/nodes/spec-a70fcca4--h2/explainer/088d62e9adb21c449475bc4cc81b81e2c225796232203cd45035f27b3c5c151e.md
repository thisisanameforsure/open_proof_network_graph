## Overview

Write $t_a = \frac{1}{2^a-1}$ (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$). Every finite sum $\sum_{a\in S} t_a$ is a fraction $p/q$ of natural numbers with $q$ odd, because each $2^a - 1$ with $a \ge 1$ is odd and a product of odd numbers is odd. If such a sum were $\frac12$, then $q = 2p$ would be even. Contradiction.

## Finite sums have odd denominators {steps: key}

By induction on the finite set $S$. For $S = \varnothing$ the sum is $0 = 0/1$. Adding a new element $a$ to $S$ whose sum is $p/q$ with $q$ odd: if $a = 0$ the new term is Lean's $0$ and nothing changes. If $a \ge 1$, then $d = 2^a - 1$ is odd ($2^a$ is even and $\ge 1$), it is nonzero, and as a real number it equals $2^a - 1$; so
$$\frac{1}{2^a-1} + \frac pq \;=\; \frac{q + p\,d}{q\,d},$$
with $q\,d$ odd as a product of odd numbers.

## One half is not such a fraction {steps: s2, hqne, h2, h3, close}

Take $S$ with $\sum_{a\in S} t_a = \frac12$ and write the sum as $p/q$, $q$ odd (so $q \ne 0$). Clearing denominators in $p/q = \frac12$ gives $q = 2p$, first in $\mathbb{R}$ and then, casting back, in $\mathbb{N}$. So $q$ is even, contradicting that it is odd.
