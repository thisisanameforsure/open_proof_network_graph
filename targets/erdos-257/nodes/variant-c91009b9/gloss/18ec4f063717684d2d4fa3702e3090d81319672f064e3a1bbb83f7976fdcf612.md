$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N}:\quad A\ \text{infinite},\qquad \gcd(a,b)=1\ \ \forall a\ne b\in A,\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac1n\ \text{converges}.$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\{p^2 : p\ \text{prime}\}$: there are infinitely many primes and squaring is injective, squares of distinct primes are coprime, and the sum of $\frac{1}{p^2}$ is compared with the convergent $\sum_m \frac{1}{m^2}$ through the square root.
