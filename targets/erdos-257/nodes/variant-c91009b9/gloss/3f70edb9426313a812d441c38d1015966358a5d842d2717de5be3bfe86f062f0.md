$$\Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite}:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr) \;\Longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite, pairwise coprime, with } \sum_{n\in A}\frac1n < \infty:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr).$$

The root's statement implies this variant (relation `partial`): the variant adds two hypotheses on $A$ (pairwise coprime, and $n \mapsto \frac1n$ summable on $A$), and the proof applies the root's statement to the same $A$ without using them.
