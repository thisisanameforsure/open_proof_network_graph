## Overview

Fix $n \ge 1$ and write $N = 2^{n+1}-1$. The proof bounds each term of the tail by a geometric series,
$$\frac{1}{2^{n+1+k}-1} \;\le\; \frac{1}{N}\Bigl(\frac12\Bigr)^{k} \qquad (k \ge 0),$$
sums it to get $\sum_{k\ge0} \frac{1}{2^{n+1+k}-1} \le \frac{2}{N}$, and then checks $\frac{2}{2^{n+1}-1} < \frac{1}{2^n-1}$, which after clearing denominators is $2^{n+1}-2 < 2^{n+1}-1$.

## The denominators are positive {steps: h2n, hpos1}

From $n \ge 1$, $2 = 2^1 \le 2^n$ (monotonicity of $k \mapsto 2^k$). Hence $2^{n+1} - 1 = 2\cdot 2^n - 1 \ge 3 > 0$, and also $2^n - 1 \ge 1 > 0$, which is used at the end.

## Termwise comparison with a geometric series {steps: hterm, hnonneg}

For each $k$, $2^{n+1+k} = 2^{n+1}\cdot 2^k$ and $2^k \ge 1$, so $2^{n+1+k}-1 > 0$. Both sides of the claimed bound have positive denominators; cross-multiplying, it becomes
$$2^k\,(2^{n+1}-1) \;\le\; 2^{n+1}2^k - 1, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad 1 \le 2^k,$$
which holds. The same positivity gives that every term $\frac{1}{2^{n+1+k}-1}$ is nonnegative.

## Summing the comparison {steps: hsum, hle, hgeom}

The majorant $\frac1N (\frac12)^k$ is a constant multiple of a geometric series with ratio $\frac12$, so it is summable. A nonnegative series bounded termwise by a summable one is summable, and its sum is at most the majorant's: $\sum_k \frac{1}{2^{n+1+k}-1} \le \sum_k \frac1N(\frac12)^k$. Pulling out the constant and using $\sum_{k\ge0} 2^{-k} = 2$, the right side equals $\frac{2}{2^{n+1}-1}$.

## The final inequality {steps: s8, s9}

Chain the two: the tail is at most $\frac{2}{2^{n+1}-1}$, and
$$\frac{2}{2^{n+1}-1} < \frac{1}{2^n-1} \iff 2\,(2^n-1) < 2^{n+1}-1 \iff 2^{n+1}-2 < 2^{n+1}-1,$$
where both denominators are positive by the first section. This is linear arithmetic once $2^{n+1}$ is written as $2\cdot 2^n$.
