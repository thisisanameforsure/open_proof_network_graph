label: 'gap lemma: each term exceeds the sum of all later terms'
---
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{\,n+1+k}-1} \;<\; \frac{1}{2^{n}-1} \qquad \text{for every integer } n \ge 1 .$$

Equivalently, writing $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$: for $n \ge 1$, $\sum_{m>n} t_m < t_n$ — each term of the series $\sum_{m\ge1} t_m$ exceeds the sum of all the terms after it. Division is real division; $n \ge 1$ keeps every denominator positive. The left side is Lean's `tsum`, which here is the ordinary sum of a convergent positive series.
