A structure lemma for calm points, the conditional-on-primes barrier it gives, and the calm points up to 4·10^9. Complements the earlier annex on this node (5c758f8b...), which recorded the structure only as an observation from the data.

Notation as in that annex: c_m(j) = #{a : 20 < a < m, a | j}, G(m) = sum over i ≥ 0 of 2^-(i+1) c_m(m+i+1), calm means G(m) ≤ 99/100.

**Lemma (structure of calm points).** For m ≥ 1092, if G(m) ≤ 99/100 then m ≡ 0 (mod 6), m+1 is prime, and either (m+2)/2 is prime or 4 | m+2 and (m+2)/4 is prime.

Proof. (1) A lower bound that depends only on m mod 27720 = lcm(2..12). For j = m+i+1 with 0 ≤ i < 24 and 2 ≤ d ≤ 12, d | j, the number j/d is a divisor of j with 20 < j/d < m (as j > 240 and j < 2m), and distinct d give distinct j/d. So c_m(j) ≥ c(j) := #{2 ≤ d ≤ 12 : d | j}, and G(m) ≥ L(m) := sum over i < 24 of 2^-(i+1) c(m+i+1), which depends only on r = m mod 27720. Exact rational arithmetic over all 27720 residues gives: min L over odd r = 1.2016 > 99/100, so m is even. (2) Replace the i = 0 term by max(c(m+1), 1): over even r the minimum is 1.1025 > 99/100, so m+1 has no divisor in (20, m). Any composite m+1 > 441 has its largest proper divisor in [sqrt(m+1), m), above 20, so m+1 is prime. (3) Replace the i = 1 term by max(c(m+2), 3) over even r with c(m+1) = 0: the minimum is 16660165/2^24 = 0.99302 > 99/100, so c_m(m+2) ≤ 2. For m+2 = 2u ≥ 1094: if u is composite with least prime s, then u, 2u/s and u/s are three distinct divisors in (20, m) unless s = 2; with u = 2t, t composite with least prime s', the divisors t, 2t, 2t/s' (s' ≥ 3) or t, 2t, t/2 (s' = 2, t ≥ 274) are three. So m+2 = 2q or 4q with q prime. (4) The 1060 residues mod 27720 with L ≤ 99/100 are all ≡ 0 or 6 mod 12, so 6 | m.

Both cases of (3) occur: 1092 = 2·547 − 2, and m = 41919570 is calm (G = 0.98202) with m+2 = 4·10479893. So the earlier observation that every calm point is ≡ 0 mod 12 with m+2 twice a prime is false; 12 of the 2630 calm points below 4·10^9 have m+2 = 4q.

**Barrier.** With the lemma, this hole implies: for every n ≥ 1092 there is a prime q with 2q−1 prime and n < 2q−2, 2q+8 ≤ 2n, or with 4q−1 prime and n < 4q−2, 4q+8 ≤ 2n. In particular there would be infinitely many primes q with 2q−1 or 4q−1 prime. That is a Dickson-type statement about a fixed pair of linear forms (in the 2q−1 case, the Cunningham-chain-of-the-second-kind pair); no unconditional proof of infinitely many such q is known, and sieve methods meet the parity barrier there (Chen-type methods give only 2q−1 with at most two prime factors; Maynard–Tao does not control which forms are prime). So a proof of this hole would prove an open prime-pair conjecture in a quantitative, every-dyadic-interval form; we regard it as out of reach unconditionally. **Is the hole false? No sign of it.** Using the lemma as an exact filter (cross-checked against an unfiltered computation of G for every m from 1092 to 10^8: identical calm sets, 350 points), there are 2630 calm points in [1092, 4·10^9) and no gap: every n with 1092 ≤ n < 3999925776 (the last calm point found) has a calm point in (n, 2n−10]. The tightest interval is still the early one, 3060 → 5580 (ratio 1.8235, slack 530). The largest ratio of consecutive calm points is 1.176 above 10^6, 1.032 above 10^8 and 1.0096 above 10^9, and the count per dyadic block [2^k, 2^(k+1)) grows steadily (13, 26, 35, 29, 56, 75, 119, 177, 267, 396, 614, 779 for k = 20..31), as a Hardy–Littlewood count for a prime constellation would. G was summed over 64 terms in double precision; the closest value to 99/100 found was within 2.5·10^-5 (m = 2323230000), far above the rounding error.

**Weakenings.** Raising the threshold to 1 − 2^-11 (as the earlier annex notes h_stall_forces_count allows) does not help: steps (1) and (2) still go through (bounds 1.20 and 1.10 exceed 1), so m+1 must still be prime, and step (3) fails only narrowly (0.99302 < 0.99951); empirically, all 165 points below 2·10^7 with G ≤ 1 − 2^-11 still have m+1 and (m+2)/2 prime. Any condition that bounds a count over all divisors in (20, m), uniformly over the walk's possible sets, reduces to such a G-type bound and meets the same prime-pair obstruction; a route avoiding it would have to use the specific set the walk of 2^-20 takes, for which no structure is known beyond the first steps. We found no provable replacement for this hole.
