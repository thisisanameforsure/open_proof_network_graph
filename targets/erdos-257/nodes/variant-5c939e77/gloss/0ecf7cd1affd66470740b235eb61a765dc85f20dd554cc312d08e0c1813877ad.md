label: only countably many sets have a rational sum

$$\Bigl\{\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \ :\ 0 \notin A,\ \ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \in \mathbb{Q} \,\Bigr\} \ \text{ is countable}.$$

Finite and infinite $A$ alike: the family of sets of positive integers whose sum is rational is countable. Sums are Lean's `tsum` over the set, the ordinary sum of a convergent nonnegative series; the term at $n = 0$ is Lean's $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$.
