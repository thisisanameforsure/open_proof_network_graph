## Statement

For $m \ge 1092$ with $m+1$ prime, write $c_m(N) = \#\{a : 20 < a < m,\ a \mid N\}$ and $G(m) = \sum_{i \ge 0} 2^{-(i+1)} c_m(m+i+1)$. Then $c_m(m+3) \le 4$ or $G(m) > 99/100$. Assume $c_m(m+3) \ge 5$; we show $G(m) > 99/100$.

## Counting tools (`hfin`, `hcardle`, `hone`, `htwo`)

Each set $\{a : 20 < a < m,\ a \mid N\}$ lies in $[0, m)$, so it is finite and $c_m(N) \le m$. One member gives $c_m(N) \ge 1$, and two distinct members give $c_m(N) \ge 2$ (`Set.ncard_le_ncard` with `Set.ncard_pair`).

## G is at least its first four terms

The terms are nonnegative and at most $m\,2^{-(i+1)}$, so the series converges and $G(m) \ge \sum_{i<4} 2^{-(i+1)} c_m(m+i+1)$. The $i = 2$ term is $c_m(m+3)/8 \ge 5/8$.

## Parity and the two cases

$m+1$ is a prime above $2$, so it is odd and $m$ is even. Write $m = 2k$ and split on the parity of $k$.

- $m = 4j$: $(m+2)/2 = 2j+1$ divides $m+2$, so the $i=1$ term is at least $1/4$. Both $(m+4)/2 = 2j+2$ and $(m+4)/4 = j+1$ divide $m+4$, so the $i=3$ term is at least $2/16$. The total is at least $1/4 + 5/8 + 1/8 = 1$.
- $m = 4j+2$: both $(m+2)/2 = 2j+2$ and $(m+2)/4 = j+1$ divide $m+2$, so the $i=1$ term is at least $2/4$. The total is at least $1/2 + 5/8 = 9/8$.

All divisors used exceed $20$ and are below $m$ because $j \ge 273$. In both cases $G(m) \ge 1 > 99/100$. $\blacksquare$
