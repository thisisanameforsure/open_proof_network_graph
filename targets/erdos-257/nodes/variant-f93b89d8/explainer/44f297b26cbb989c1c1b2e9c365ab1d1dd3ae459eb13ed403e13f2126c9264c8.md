## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $2\le a<b$ be integers, $A=\{a^k:k\ge 0\}\cup\{b^k:k\ge 0\}$ and $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

The proof applies a Liouville-type criterion, proved in the file as the private lemma `r2_liou`: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^x-1)\mid L$ for all $x\in A$ below $m$ and $2^CL\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* Its proof: suppose the sum is a rational $r$ with denominator $q$ and take $m$, $L$ for $C=4q$. Write $r=\sum_{x\in A,\,x<m}t_x+T$ with $T$ the tail over $n\ge m$. Then $T>0$, and $T\le 4/2^m$ by comparison with a geometric series (`lac_f_le`, `lac_tail_le`). By `liou_int`, $L$ times the finite part is an integer, so $qLT$ is an integer; it is positive, so $qLT\ge 1$. But $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$ and $4q<2^{4q}$ give $qLT\le 4qL/2^m<1$, a contradiction.

The arithmetic input is `gen_good`: for every $C$ there are $J,K$ with

$$C+a^J+b^K\le a^{J+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad C+a^J+b^K\le b^{K+1}.$$

If $(a,b)=(2,3)$ this is `u23_good`, which gives $J',k$ with $3^{J'}+C\le 2^k$ and $2^k+C\le 2\cdot 3^{J'}$; take $J=k$, $K=J'$. (`u23_good` rests on `u23_gap`, $2^y\le|2^x-3\cdot 2^y|$, and `u23_bad`.) Otherwise $b\ge 4$. Put $X=b^{C+1}\ge 4C+4$ and $Z=b^{C+2}\ge 4X$, so $b^{C+3}\ge 4Z$, and let $a^J$ be the largest power of $a$ with $a^J\le Z<a^{J+1}$. If $C+a^J+X\le Z$, the pair $(J,C+1)$ works. If not, $a^J>Z-C-X$, and then $(J,C+2)$ works when $a\ge 3$ and $(J+1,C+2)$ works when $a=2$; the inequalities are linear in $C$, $X$, $Z$, $a^J$ and are checked by `omega`.

## $A$ is infinite and does not contain $0$ {steps: hinf h0}

$A$ contains the powers of $a$, the range of the map $k\mapsto a^k$, which is injective because $a\ge 2$; so $A$ is infinite. No power of $a$ or of $b$ is $0$. So `r2_liou` applies once its last hypothesis is checked for an arbitrary $C$.

## Choosing $m$ and $L$ {steps: s3}

Fix $C$ and take $J,K$ from `gen_good`. Put

$$m=\min\bigl(a^{J+1},\,b^{K+1}\bigr),\qquad L=\bigl(2^{a^J}-1\bigr)\bigl(2^{b^K}-1\bigr).$$

## $m\in A$ and $L>0$ {steps: s4 s5}

The minimum is one of the two numbers, a power of $a$ or a power of $b$, so $m\in A$. Both $a^J$ and $b^K$ are nonzero, so $2^{a^J}>1$ and $2^{b^K}>1$, and both factors of $L$ are positive.

## Every $2^x-1$ with $x\in A$, $x<m$ divides $L$ {steps: s6}

If $x=a^i<m$ then $a^i<a^{J+1}$, so $i\le J$ and $a^i\mid a^J$. Since $u\mid v$ implies $(2^u-1)\mid(2^v-1)$, the number $2^x-1$ divides the first factor of $L$. If $x=b^i<m$ then $b^i<b^{K+1}$, so $i\le K$, $b^i\mid b^K$, and $2^x-1$ divides the second factor.

## The size condition $2^CL\le 2^m$ {steps: s7}

Dropping the two $-1$'s,

$$2^C L\;\le\;2^C\cdot 2^{a^J}\cdot 2^{b^K}\;=\;2^{\,C+a^J+b^K}.$$

The exponent is at most $m$ because it is at most $a^{J+1}$ and at most $b^{K+1}$, the two inequalities from `gen_good`.
