$$\exists\, A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite}:\quad \exists\, p,N\in\mathbb{N}:\ \ 0<p\quad\text{and}\quad \forall n\ge N:\ (n\in A\iff n+p\in A).$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=\mathbb{N}$, the whole set of natural numbers, with period $p=1$ and threshold $N=0$: every $n$ and every $n+1$ belong to $A$.
