$$\Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite}:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr) \;\Longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite, eventually periodic}:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr).$$

"Eventually periodic" is the Lean hypothesis $\exists\, p, N \in \mathbb{N}:\ p > 0 \text{ and } \forall n \ge N,\ (n \in A \iff n + p \in A)$. The root's statement implies this variant (relation `partial`): the proof applies the root's statement to the same $A$ without using the periodicity hypothesis.
