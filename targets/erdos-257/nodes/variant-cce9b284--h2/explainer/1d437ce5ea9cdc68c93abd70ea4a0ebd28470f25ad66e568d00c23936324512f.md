## What the assembly shows

A positive rational whose repair walk chooses infinitely many terms and has a rational repair sum exists, provided there is one whose walk in addition repairs infinitely often. Notation as on the parent: $t_m=\frac{1}{2^m-1}$; the walk of $x$ is $r_0=x$, $q_m=\lfloor r_m/t_m\rfloor$, $r_{m+1}=r_m-q_mt_m$; the chosen set is $A=\{m: q_m\ne0,\ r_{m+1}\le\sum_{j\ge0}t_{m+1+j}\}$; and index $m$ is a *repair* when $q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\ne0$.

This is the second of two partials on the node, and it is a case label more than a reduction. Its one hole is the node's statement with one further requirement added, so the hole is at least as strong as what it is used to prove. As of 12 November 2026 the hole is open, so this route does not prove the node. The partial names no $x$ and gives no argument that such a walk exists. Its purpose is to give the case of infinitely many repairs a statement of its own, beside the first partial's case of finitely many.

## Infinitely many repairs with a rational sum {steps: h_infinite}

The hole: there are a rational $x>0$ and its walk $(r,q)$ such that

- the chosen set $A$ is infinite,
- the set of repairs $\{m: q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\ne0\}$ is infinite, and
- $\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m=s$ for some rational $s$.

## The assembly {steps: s2 close}

Take $x$, its walk and $s$ from the hole and drop the second property. What remains is the statement: $x>0$, the walk's recursion, $A$ infinite, and a rational repair sum.
