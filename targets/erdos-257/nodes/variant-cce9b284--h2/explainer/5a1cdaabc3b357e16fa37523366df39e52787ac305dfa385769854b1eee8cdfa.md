## What the assembly shows

A positive rational whose repair walk chooses infinitely many terms and has a rational repair sum exists, provided some positive rational with even denominator has a walk that repairs only finitely often. Notation as on the parent: $t_m=\frac{1}{2^m-1}$; the walk of $x$ is $r_0=x$, $q_m=\lfloor r_m/t_m\rfloor$, $r_{m+1}=r_m-q_mt_m$; the chosen set is $A=\{m: q_m\ne0,\ r_{m+1}\le\sum_{j\ge0}t_{m+1+j}\}$; and index $m$ is a *repair* when $q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\ne0$.

This is one of two partials on the node. It has three holes. As of 12 November 2026 the second and third are proved on the graph and the first is open, so this route does not prove the node. The open hole is an existence statement for which the partial offers no candidate $x$.

## An even-denominator rational with finitely many repairs {steps: hall.s1.h_finite}

The open hole: there is a rational $x>0$ with even denominator whose walk has only finitely many repairs.

## The walk accounts for $x$ {steps: hall.s2.h_account}

For the walk of any rational $x\ge0$,

$$\sum_{m\in A}t_m=x-\sum_m\bigl(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m)\bigr)t_m .$$

This restates the sibling statement under the parent, which is proved on the graph.

## Parity of a finite repair sum {steps: hall.s3.h_parity}

For the walk of any rational $x\ge0$ that satisfies the accounting identity and has only finitely many repairs: either the denominator of $x$ is odd, or $A$ is infinite and the repair sum $\sum_m(q_m-\mathbf 1_A(m))t_m$ is rational. This hole is proved on the graph. The partial's comment gives the reason the denominator enters: a finite sum of terms $\frac{1}{2^m-1}$ has odd denominator.

## The assembly {steps: s2 s3 s4}

Take $x$ and its walk from the first hole. The second hole gives the accounting identity for this walk, and the walk has finitely many repairs, so the third hole applies: the denominator of $x$ is odd, or $A$ is infinite with rational repair sum. The denominator is even, so the first alternative is impossible. The second is the statement, with the same $x$ and walk.
