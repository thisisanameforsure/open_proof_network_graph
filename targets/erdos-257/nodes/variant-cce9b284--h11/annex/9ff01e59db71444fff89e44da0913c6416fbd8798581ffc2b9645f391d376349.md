The weakest prime-pattern hypothesis I can find that implies this hole, with a proof of the implication, and the matching necessary condition. It builds on the annex already on this node (b698a50b..., the j-independent prime-pair barrier and the excess form) and on the calm-points annex on variant-cce9b284 (3888b5d8...), whose section 3 says the tail at a constellation point "needs a bound that the standard conjecture does not state". Section 2 shows why: no fixed number of prime forms can be enough. Section 3 gives a hypothesis with a slowly growing number of forms that is enough, and proves the implication. Nothing here is formalised. Computations are exact integer arithmetic (numpy divisor sieve, sympy primality and factoring).

Notation as in annex b698a50b: c_t(m) = #{a : a | m+t, j < a < m}, G_j(m) = sum over t ≥ 1 of 2^-t c_t(m), calm means G_j(m) ≤ 99/100, E - 1 = sum over d ≥ 2 of 1/(2^d - 1) = 0.606695... For 1 ≤ t < m every proper divisor of m+t is below m, so c_t(m) = tau(m+t) - 1 - #{a ≤ j : a | m+t}.

## 1. Necessary conditions, and what j changes

(N1) From 2^-t c_t ≤ G and c_t ≥ tau(m+t) - 1 - j: a calm m has tau(m+t) ≤ 0.99 * 2^t + j + 1 for every 1 ≤ t < m, and the sum over 1 ≤ t < m of 2^-t tau(m+t) is at most j + 1.99.

(N2) (annex b698a50b, section 3) For m ≥ 2j^2 + 12j a calm m has m ≡ 0 mod 6, m+1 prime, and (m+2)/2 or (m+2)/4 prime. So h11, for any j, implies: every large n has a prime q with 2q - 1 prime, or with 4q - 1 prime, in a dyadic range. That is a two-form case of Dickson's conjecture, beyond sieve methods (parity), so h11 has no unconditional proof by current methods for any choice of j.

Checked by divisor sieve to 2*10^7: every calm point at or above 2j^2 + 12j has the shape (N2), for j = 20 (157 points), j = 100 (283) and j = 1000 (386); none is outside 0 mod 6. A larger j does not weaken the structure; it only moves the threshold 2j^2 + 12j up, and below it calm points are dense (6060 below 2*10^7 for j = 1000, consecutive ratio at most 1.062 beyond 2*10^6, against 1.824 for j = 20). The constant j + 1.99 in (N1) is the only place j survives asymptotically.

(N1) is a growing family of almost-prime conditions. A typical N near x has tau(N) about (log x)^(log 2) = 2^(loglog x), so the constraint tau(m+t) ≤ C 2^t is atypical exactly for t up to about (1 - o(1)) loglog x (natural logs): a calm point needs about loglog m consecutive integers m+1, m+2, ... each with at most about t + O(log j) prime factors.

## 2. No fixed number of prime forms suffices

Take m ≡ 0 mod lcm(1..k) with P_t = (m+t)/t prime for t ≤ k. The head (t ≤ k) costs at most E - 1 (section 3, Lemma A), but the tail sum over t > k of 2^-t tau(m+t) is about 2^-k (log m)^(log 2) at a typical such point, which tends to infinity. So for fixed k the proportion of pattern points that are calm should tend to 0, and a fixed-k Bateman-Horn statement (with any uniformity) does not give calm points by counting pattern points. Data, j = 20, consecutive pattern points from 10^e upward, G from the first 64 terms:

| k | x = 10^6 | 10^8 or 10^9 | 10^12 | 10^15 or 10^16 | 10^18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 (400 pts) | 3.0% calm, median G 2.08 | 0.75% (10^9), 2.92 | 0%, 3.80 | 0% (10^15), 4.24 | 0%, 5.43 |
| 5 (200 pts) | | 28.5% (10^8), 1.17 | 12.5%, 1.42 | 9.5% (10^16), 1.70 | |
| 7 (30 pts) | | 93% (10^9), 0.82 | | | |

The medians grow like 2^-k (log x)^0.69, as predicted. So the number of forms must grow with x; the next section shows that about log_2 log x forms are enough.

## 3. A sufficient hypothesis and the proof

For large x put K = K(x) = ceil(log_2 log x + 4 log_2 loglog x), L = lcm(1..K), y = x/L, f_i(u) = (L/i) u + 1 for 1 ≤ i ≤ K, and U(x) = {integers u in (y, 1.9y] : f_1(u), ..., f_K(u) all prime}. The family is admissible (all f_i(0) = 1); let S be its singular series. (K = 12 at x = 10^12, 14 at 10^20, 18 at 10^100; L is (log x)^(1.44 + o(1)).)

For d ≥ 1 and an integer t let w_t(d) be the Bateman-Horn local density of the extra condition d | Lu + t: the product over p^a || d of the conditional probability, for u uniform modulo a high power of p, that p^a | Lu + t given that p divides none of f_1(u), ..., f_K(u).

**Hypothesis H (uniform Bateman-Horn for this family).** There are B and x_0 such that for all x ≥ x_0:
(H1) #U(x) ≥ (1/2) S * 0.9y / (log 2x)^K;
(H2) for every K < t ≤ T_0 = 2 log_2 x + 10 and every d ≤ sqrt(2x), #{u in U(x) : d | Lu + t} ≤ B w_t(d) S * 0.9y / (log x)^K.

H1 is the lower half of Bateman-Horn for K(x) ~ 1.44 loglog x forms, uniformly in that range. H2 is the upper half in progressions to moduli up to sqrt(2x) = y^(1/2 + o(1)), with an absolute constant. Upper-bound sieves give H2 with a constant of size about 2^K K!, which is (log x)^(c logloglog x) and too large here, and only at level below 1/2; the uniformity in K and the level are what is conjectural.

**Theorem.** H implies: for every j ≥ 3 there is x_1 such that every x ≥ x_1 has a j-calm m in (x, 1.9x], hence in (n, 2n - 10] with n = x ≥ 100.

Proof. Let u in U(x), m = Lu, so x < m ≤ 1.9x. Split G_j(m) into t ≤ K, K < t < T_0, t ≥ T_0.

Lemma A (head). For t ≤ K, m + t = t q with q = f_t(u) prime and q > m/K > t, j. The divisors of tq are e and eq with e | t; eq < m exactly when e < t (once m > K^2). So c_t ≤ tau(t) - 1 + #{e | t : e > j}, and the head is at most the sum over t of 2^-t (tau(t) - 1) plus the sum over t > j of 2^-t tau(t), that is (E - 1) + delta_j, using the sum over t of 2^-t tau(t) = E. delta_20 < 4*10^-6.

Lemma B (far tail). For t ≥ T_0, c_t ≤ tau(m+t) ≤ 2 sqrt(m+t) (and c_t < m for t ≥ m), so this part is below 2^(-T_0 + 3) sqrt(x) + m 2^(1-m) < 0.001.

Lemma C (local densities). For K < t < T_0 and D ≥ 2K, the sum over d ≤ D of w_t(d) is at most C t^(3/2) K^2 log D / log K, C absolute. Proof: the sum is at most the product over p ≤ D of (1 + sum over a ≥ 1 of w_t(p^a)).
- p > K: the roots of the f_i mod p are u ≡ -i/L, distinct for i ≤ K < p, so nu_p = K. p | Lu + t fixes u ≡ -t/L, which is allowed only if t mod p is not in {1..K}; given an allowed class the conditional probability is 1/(p - K), and each further power of p costs 1/p. So the p-factor is at most 1 + (1/(p-K)) p/(p-1). Over K < p ≤ 2K the product is at most exp(2 sum 1/(p-K)) = O(K^2); over 2K < p ≤ D it is at most exp(sum 1/p + 4K sum over p > 2K of 1/p^2) = O(log D / log K).
- p ≤ K, e = v_p(L) (so p^e ≤ K < p^(e+1)): for a ≤ e, p^a | Lu + t iff p^a | t; for a > e one needs p^e | t, then u lies in one class mod p^(a-e), conditional probability at most 1 for a = e+1 and p^-(a-e-1) after. So the p-factor is at most v_p(t) + 1, times 3 if p^e | t. Those p^e are pairwise coprime divisors of t, each above sqrt K, so there are at most 2 log t / log K of them, and the product over p ≤ K is at most tau(t) 3^(2 log t / log K) ≤ 2 t^(3/2) for K ≥ 9.

Middle tail. Put M(u) = sum over K < t < T_0 of 2^-t tau(Lu + t). Since tau(N) ≤ 2 #{d | N : d ≤ sqrt N} and Lu + t ≤ 2x, H2 and Lemma C (D = sqrt(2x)) give, with (H1) and (log 2x / log x)^K ≤ 2,

sum over u in U(x) of M(u) ≤ 8 B C K^2 log x * (sum over t > K of 2^-t t^(3/2)) * #U(x) ≤ C' B K^(7/2) 2^-K log x * #U(x).

With 2^K ≥ log x (loglog x)^4 and K ≤ 2 loglog x, the factor is O(B (loglog x)^(-1/2)), which tends to 0; take x_1 so that it is below 0.01. By Markov at least half of U(x) has M(u) ≤ 0.02, and U(x) is non-empty by H1. At such u, G_j(m) ≤ (E - 1) + delta_j + 0.02 + 0.001. Here (E - 1) + delta_j is 0.9634 for j = 3, 0.7759 for j = 4, 0.6196 for j = 8 and 0.60670 for j = 20, so G_j(m) ≤ 0.99 for every j ≥ 3. (For j ≤ 2 the head bound exceeds 0.99: the divisors e of t with j < e < t then cost about E - 1 a second time.) ∎

## 4. From the theorem to the hole

h11 asks for one j and N with no early stall and a calm chain from N on. Take j = 20 and N = 1092: the walk of 2^-20 is checked in exact arithmetic to step 4000 (annex 3888b5d8), and every n from 1092 to 3.999*10^9 has a calm point in (n, 2n - 10] (annex e0c02cd3 on variant-cce9b284--h6, G in double precision, closest value 2.5*10^-5 from the cut). So H with an effective x_1 ≤ 4*10^9 gives h11. As stated, H is ineffective, and then it gives only "the calm chain holds eventually"; closing the hole from it needs an explicit x_1 (explicit B, x_0 and the constant C' above, or a longer computation), not a further idea.

## 5. The gap

Necessary (section 1): in every dyadic interval, m with m+1 prime, (m+2)/2 or (m+2)/4 prime, and tau(m+t) ≤ 0.99 * 2^t + j + 1 for all t: about loglog m consecutive integers, each an almost-prime with about t factors at position t. Sufficient (section 3): m ≡ 0 mod lcm(1..K) with (m+t)/t prime for every t ≤ K, K ~ log_2 log m + 4 log_2 loglog m, about 1.44 loglog m positions, plus the uniform upper bound H2 for the rest. The two match in shape (a constellation of length of order loglog m in every dyadic interval) and differ by the factor 1/log 2 in length, prime against almost-prime at each position, and the uniformity of H2. Already the first two positions of the necessary side are an open Dickson pair, so neither side is in reach of current sieve methods; a proof of h11 would settle infinitely many prime pairs (q, 2q - 1) or (q, 4q - 1).
