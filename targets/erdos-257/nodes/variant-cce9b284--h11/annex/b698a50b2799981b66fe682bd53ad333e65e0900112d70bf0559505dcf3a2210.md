The calm-point count through small divisors: its floor, the excess form of the calm-chain statement, the same prime-pair barrier for every j, and how often prime patterns give calm points. Written for this hole, the open step of the general-j calm-point route on variant-cce9b284 (some j and N in place of 20 and 1092). It adds to the calm-points annex on variant-cce9b284 (3888b5d8...) and to the two annexes on variant-cce9b284--h6 (5c758f8b..., e0c02cd3...); it proves no node. Sections 1 to 3 are elementary and written out; nothing here is formalised. The computations are in exact arithmetic unless said otherwise.

Notation. G_j(m) is the sum over i ≥ 0 of 2^-(i+1) times the number of a with j < a < m and a | m+i+1. A point m is calm for j when G_j(m) ≤ 99/100. E is the sum of 1/(2^n - 1) over n ≥ 1, about 1.6067.

## 1. The count through small divisors

For N = m+i+1 the map a -> N/a sends the divisors a of N with j < a < m onto the divisors d of N with N/m < d < N/j. So, exactly,

G_j(m) = sum over i ≥ 0 of 2^-(i+1) * #{ d : d | m+i+1, j*d < m+i+1 < d*m }.

Take d with 2 ≤ d ≤ m/j. The first condition always holds, and the second holds at every position i+1 < m. The positions with d | m+i+1 are i+1 = d - (m mod d), then every d steps, so d contributes 2^(m mod d) / (2^d - 1), less the positions past m. A divisor d > m/j needs m+i+1 ≥ (j+1)*d, so it contributes only at positions i+1 > m/j. Each count is below m, hence

| G_j(m) - sum over 2 ≤ d ≤ m/j of 2^(m mod d) / (2^d - 1) | ≤ m * 2^(2 - m/j).

For j = 20 and m ≥ 1092 the right side is below 2^-42. Checked against the definition at seven calm points for j = 20 (1092, 1200, 1380, 2016, 3060, 5580, 41919570): the two values agree to the six decimals computed.

## 2. The floor, and the excess form

Each term 2^(m mod d) / (2^d - 1) is at least 1/(2^d - 1), with equality exactly when d | m. So, up to the error of section 1,

G_j(m) ≥ sum over 2 ≤ d ≤ m/j of 1/(2^d - 1), which tends to E - 1 = 0.6067.

This is the constant the calm-points annex meets in its section 2 as the cost of the pattern m+i = i*q_i; here it is a lower bound for every m and every j. Write

X_j(m) = sum over 2 ≤ d ≤ m/j with d not dividing m of (2^(m mod d) - 1) / (2^d - 1).

Then G_j(m) = (E - 1) + X_j(m) up to an error below m * 2^(3 - m/j), and the calm-chain statement reads: every interval (n, 2n-10] past N contains an m with X_j(m) ≤ 0.3833. A divisor d that does not divide m costs about 2^-t, where m+t is the next multiple of d. Among the 2630 calm points for j = 20 below 4*10^9 (the list of annex e0c02cd3) the smallest value of G is 0.6523, at m = 668745000.

## 3. The prime-pair barrier does not depend on j

Annex e0c02cd3 proves for j = 20: a calm m ≥ 1092 has m ≡ 0 mod 6, m+1 prime, and (m+2)/2 or (m+2)/4 prime. Its three lower bounds count only the divisors d ≤ 12 of m+1, ..., m+24, which is the small-divisor count of section 1 cut at d ≤ 12, so they hold for every j once m ≥ 12j. I recomputed them over the 27720 residues in exact fractions: 1.2016 for odd m; 1.1025 for even m when m+1 has a divisor in (j, m); 0.99302 for even m when m+2 has three or more divisors d with 2 ≤ d < (m+2)/j. The remaining steps need only that a composite number has a prime factor below its square root. So for every j ≥ 4 and every m ≥ 2j^2 + 12j:

if G_j(m) ≤ 99/100 then m is even, m+1 is prime, and (m+2)/2 or (m+2)/4 is prime.

The steps, for the record. If m+1 is composite, its largest proper divisor is at least sqrt(m+1) > j, a divisor in (j, m), and the second bound applies. If m+2 = 2u and u has an odd prime factor l ≤ sqrt(u), then 2, l and 2l are three divisors below (m+2)/j, and the third bound applies. If u = 2v with v composite, then 2, 4 and a prime factor of v (or 8) are three such divisors. I have not re-derived m ≡ 0 mod 3 for general j.

Consequence for the general-j hole: for whichever j and N it is proved with, every n past max(N, 2j^2 + 12j) has a prime q with 2q-1 prime and n < 2q-2 ≤ 2n-10, or with 4q-1 prime and n < 4q-2 ≤ 2n-10. So leaving j open does not avoid the barrier recorded on h6. What a larger j does change is the density of calm points (the table in the calm-points annex: 393, 1154 and 6690 below 10^8 for j = 20, 100, 1000).

## 4. How often prime patterns give calm points (j = 20)

Write P_i = (m+i)/gcd(m, i). The pattern "P_1, ..., P_k all prime" is the zero-excess ideal for the divisors that are large; the data shows that no fixed k is either necessary or sufficient.

Multiples of 12 in [10^6, 1.2*10^7), G from the first 44 terms of the series:

| pattern | points | calm |
|---|---|---|
| P_1, P_2 prime | 31683 | 90 |
| P_1..P_3 prime | 3173 | 79 |
| P_1..P_4 prime | 159 | 38 |
| P_1..P_5 prime | 25 | 12 |
| P_1..P_6 prime | 1 | 1 |

The 2630 calm points below 4*10^9, by the length of the initial run of prime P_i: run 1: 12 (the points with m+2 = 4q), run 2: 327, run 3: 1022, run 4: 822, run 5: 380, run 6: 57, run 7: 10. Divisible by 12: 2618; by 60: 2209; by 420: 1330; by 2520: 474.

## 5. What a conditional proof would need

For L = lcm(1..k) and m = L*t, the numbers P_i for i ≤ k are the linear forms (L/i)*t + 1, an admissible family (all equal 1 at t = 0). The Bateman-Horn conjecture predicts on the order of x/(log x)^k values t ≤ x at which all are prime. At such a point G_j(m) is at most E - 1 plus the tail, the sum over i > k of 2^-i times the number of divisors of m+i not forced by gcd(m, i). The calm chain would follow if a fixed proportion of those points in each dyadic interval had a tail below 0.38. That is an upper-bound sieve statement; I have not derived it, and the calm-points annex (its section 3) records the same gap.
