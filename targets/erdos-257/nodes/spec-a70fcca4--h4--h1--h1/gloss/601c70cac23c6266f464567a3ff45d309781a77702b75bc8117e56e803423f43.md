$$m \ge 2 \ \wedge\ r_m < \frac{1}{2^m-1} \ \Longrightarrow\ \exists n,\ m < n \le 2m \ \wedge\ r_n < \frac{1}{2^n-1}$$

Here r is the greedy remainder of 1/2: r_0 = 1/2, r_(m+1) = r_m - 1/(2^m-1) if that term fits under r_m, else r_m. In words: the set A the walk chooses never contains the whole block m+1, ..., 2m right after an element m >= 2 it skipped. A purely combinatorial statement about runs of A. It is stronger than the parent (it also excludes 2^-m < r_m <= 2^-m + 4^-m/3 at a skipped m), and holds numerically to depth 5000 (longest run of consecutive chosen elements 13; the lead reports 20 at depth 10^6, against the m needed at step m).
