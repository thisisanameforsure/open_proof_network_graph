## What the assembly shows, and where it stands

The node says that $\sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is irrational for every nonempty $B\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ with $n\in B\iff n+P\in B$ for some $P\ge1$. The partial reduces this to one hole about primes, by Erdős's 1948 argument for $B=\mathbb{N}$ with the divisor function $d$ replaced by $f_B(n)$, the number of divisors of $n$ lying in $B$.

This route cannot be completed as stated. The hole (node `variant-5274cf38--h1--h1`) is false at $P=1$: in Lean `p % 1` is $0$, so no prime meets the hole's condition `p % P = 1`, while the hole asks for $m^2$ of them (defect claim, graph pull request #1325, 12 November 2026). The intended hypothesis is $2\le P$. The assembly applies the hole once, at the period $P$ it is handed, so it meets the false instance exactly when that period is $1$, that is, when $B=\mathbb{N}$. The node itself was proved on 12 November 2026 by a direct proof (graph pull request #1385) that uses neither partial. Everything else below is proved in the file.

## The hole: primes in a window

For every $P\ge1$ there is $m_0$ such that for every $m\ge m_0$ there is a set of $m^2$ primes $p$ with `p % P = 1` and $2^m<p\le 2^{m+m^2}$.

## Two facts about $f_B$

*Lambert identity.* $\sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=\sum_{m\ge0}f_B(m)/2^m$, for every $B$: the sum of $2^{-dk}$ over $d\in B$, $d,k\ge1$ is summed first over $k$, then by the product $dk$. (The term $n=0$ is $1/0=0$ in Lean, and $f_B(0)=0$.)

*Doubling.* If $p$ is prime, `p % P = 1`, $r\ne0$ and $p\nmid r$, then $f_B(pr)=2f_B(r)$: the divisors of $pr$ are the divisors $e$ of $r$ and the numbers $pe$, and $pe=e+P\lfloor p/P\rfloor e$ is in $B$ exactly when $e$ is. By induction on $|S|$: if $n\ne0$ and every $p\in S$ is such a prime with $p\mid n$, $p^2\nmid n$, then $2^{|S|}\mid f_B(n)$.

## Integer tails

Suppose the sum is $a/b$ with $b\ge1$, and put $T_j=b\sum_{i\ge1}f_B(j+i)/2^i$. The series converges because $f_B(n)\le n$, and cutting it after the term $j$ gives $T_j=a2^j-b\sum_{n\le j}f_B(n)2^{j-n}$, an integer. It is positive, since a nonempty periodic $B$ has an element above every $j$. If $2^m\mid f_B(n)$ for $k<n\le k+m$, then $2^m\mid T_{k+m}$, so $T_{k+m}\ge 2^m$.

## Forcing the divisibility

Take $m_0$ from the hole at $P$, choose $m\ge\max(b,m_0,1)$ with $15m^5<2^m$, and put $q=2(m+m^2)m^2$; then $m+3q<2^m$ and $2b(3+q)+1<2^m$. Index the hole's $m^2$ primes as $p_{i,j}$, $i,j<m$, and let $Q=\prod p_{i,j}^2\le 2^q$. By the Chinese remainder theorem there is $c<Q$ with $c+(i+1)\equiv p_{i,j}\pmod{p_{i,j}^2}$ for all $i,j$. For every $t$ and $i<m$ the number $c+tQ+(i+1)$ is then divisible exactly once by each of $p_{i,0},\dots,p_{i,m-1}$, so $2^m$ divides $f_B$ at it. Hence $T_{c+tQ+m}\ge2^m$ for every $t$.

## The average is too small

For $i<3q$ the number $c+m+i+1$ is coprime to $Q$: a prime $p_{i',j}$ dividing it would divide $m+i-i'$, which is positive and less than $m+3q<2^m<p_{i',j}$. Put $X=Q+m+3q$. For $t<X$, using $f_B\le d$ and keeping the first $3q$ terms, $T_{c+tQ+m}\le b\sum_{i<3q}d(c+m+i+1+tQ)/2^{i+1}+1$. For $r$ coprime to $Q$, $\sum_{t<X}d(r+tQ)\le 2X(2+\log X)$: each $d(n)$ is at most twice the number of divisors $e$ with $e^2\le n$, such $e$ are at most $X$, and at most $X/e+1$ values of $t$ have $e\mid r+tQ$. Summing over $t<X$ gives $X2^m\le 2bX(2+\log X)+X$, so, as $X\le2^{q+1}$, $2^m\le 2b(3+q)+1<2^m$, a contradiction.
