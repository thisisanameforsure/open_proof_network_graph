## The helper block and the one-line proof

Let $B\subseteq\mathbb N$ be nonempty and suppose there is $p\ge 1$ with $n\in B\iff n+p\in B$ for every $n$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is irrational (in Lean the term at $n=0$ is $\frac10=0$). The file's main theorem is one line, `exact pp_irr`. Everything else is a block of private declarations which, as the file's comment says, is copied from the merged proof of the parent node `variant-5274cf38`; its last theorem `pp_irr` is this statement. What follows is what that block proves.

**The Lambert series** (`lambert_B`). For a set $B$ and $u,n\in\mathbb N$ let $f_B(u,n)$ be the number of divisors $e$ of $n$ with $ue\in B$ (`fB`), and write $f(n)=f_B(1,n)$. Expanding $\frac{1}{2^d-1}=\sum_{c\ge 1}2^{-dc}$ and collecting the terms with $dc=n$ gives $\sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{2^n-1}=\sum_n f(n)/2^n$.

**The criterion** (`irrational_series`). Let $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ satisfy $f(n)\le d(n)$, the number of divisors of $n$; suppose every tail has a nonzero term, that is, for each $j$ some $f(j+i+1)>0$; and suppose there are $p$ and $A\ge 2$ such that $2^{|S|}\mid f(n)$ whenever $n\ne 0$ and $S$ is a finite set of primes $q>p$ each dividing $n$ exactly to the power $A-1$. Then $\sum_n f(n)/2^n$ is irrational.

Its proof: suppose the sum is $a/b$ and put $T(j)=b\sum_{i\ge 1}f(j+i)/2^i$ (`Tail`). Then $T(j)=a2^j-b\sum_{n\le j}f(n)2^{j-n}$ is an integer (`tail_eq_Zint`), positive (`tail_pos`), and divisible by $2^m$ at $j=k+m$ if $2^m\mid f(n)$ for $k<n\le k+m$ (`Zint_dvd`). Choose $m\ge\max(b,p)$ with $15Am^5<2^m$ (`exists_param`), set $q=A(m+m^2)m^2$, and by Bertrand's postulate take distinct primes $p_{ij}$, $i,j<m$, with $2^m<p_{ij}\le 2^{m+m^2}$. Let $Q=\prod p_{ij}^{A}\le 2^q$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives $c<Q$ with $c+tQ+(i+1)\equiv p_{ij}^{A-1}\pmod{p_{ij}^{A}}$ for all $t$ (`hk`). So each $n$ with $c+tQ<n\le c+tQ+m$ is divisible exactly to the power $A-1$ by $m$ primes above $p$, hence $2^m\mid f(n)$ (`hforced`) and $T(c+tQ+m)\ge 2^m$ for all $t$ (`hlow`). Also $c+m+i+1$ is coprime to $Q$ for $i<3q$ (`hcop`). `averaging_contra` then sums the bound of `tail_upper` over $t<X=Q+m+3q$ and uses $\sum_{t<X}d(r+tQ)\le 2X(2+\log X)$ for $\gcd(r,Q)=1$ (`sum_dd_AP_le`) to get $2^m\le 2b(3+q)+1$, which `param_facts` excludes.

**The hypotheses for $f=f_B(1,\cdot)$.** `fB_le_dd`: $f(n)\le d(n)$, since $f$ counts some of the divisors. `fB_pos_tail`: take $e_1=e_0+p\ge 1$ with $e_0\in B$; then $e_1\in B$ divides $e_1(j+1)\ge j+1$, so $f(e_1(j+1))>0$. `two_pow_dvd_fB`: if each prime of $S$ is coprime to $p$ and divides $n\ne 0$ exactly to the power $2\varphi(p)-1$, then $2^{|S|}\mid f_B(u,n)$ for every $u$. This is by induction on $S$. Write $n=q^{2\varphi(p)-1}r$ with $q\nmid r$. Sorting the divisors of $n$ by the power of $q$ they contain gives $f_B(u,n)=\sum_{k<2\varphi(p)}f_B(uq^k,r)$ (`fB_prime_pow_mul`). Since $q^{\varphi(p)}\equiv 1\pmod p$ and $B$ is $p$-periodic, $f_B(uq^{\varphi(p)+k},r)=f_B(uq^k,r)$ (`fB_periodic`), so the sum is $2\sum_{k<\varphi(p)}f_B(uq^k,r)$, and each term is divisible by $2^{|S|-1}$ by the induction hypothesis applied to $r$.

**`pp_irr`.** Rewrite the sum by `lambert_B` and apply `irrational_series` with $f=f_B(1,\cdot)$, the period $p$ and $A=2\varphi(p)\ge 2$. A prime $q>p$ does not divide $p$, so it is coprime to $p$ and `two_pow_dvd_fB` with $u=1$ gives the divisibility hypothesis.
