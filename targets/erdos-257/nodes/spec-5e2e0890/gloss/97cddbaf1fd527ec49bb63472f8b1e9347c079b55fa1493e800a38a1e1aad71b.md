label: subset sums determine the set (uniqueness)
---
$$A, B \subseteq \mathbb{N},\quad 0 \notin A,\quad 0 \notin B,\quad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \;=\; \sum_{n\in B}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A = B .$$

The map $A \mapsto \sum_{n\in A} \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is injective on sets of positive integers: a real number is the sum of at most one such set. The sums are Lean's `tsum` over the set; every such family is summable, so they are the ordinary sums. The hypotheses $0 \notin A$, $0 \notin B$ are needed in Lean: there $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so $A$ and $A \cup \{0\}$ would have the same sum.
