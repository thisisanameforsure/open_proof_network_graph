## Overview

Write $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$). Suppose $0 \notin A$, $0 \notin B$ and $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = \sum_{b\in B} t_b$, but $A \ne B$. Let $n$ be the least element of the symmetric difference $A \,\triangle\, B$. Then $A$ and $B$ agree below $n$, $n \ge 1$ (since $0$ is in neither set), and the gap lemma spec-ddd99827 gives $\sum_{k\ge0} t_{n+1+k} < t_n$. The comparison lemma (hole spec-5e2e0890--h2) then says the set containing $n$ has the strictly larger sum, contradicting the equality.

## The two facts taken from the holes {steps: h1, h3}

From the proved hole spec-5e2e0890--h1: the sequence $(t_m)_{m\ge0}$ is summable. From the proved hole spec-5e2e0890--h2, applied for a general $g$: if $g \ge 0$ is summable, $n \in S$, $n \notin T$, $S$ and $T$ agree on $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, and $\sum_{k\ge0} g(n+1+k) < g(n)$, then $\sum_{m\in T} g(m) < \sum_{m\in S} g(m)$. Both holes are stated under the theorem's own hypotheses on $A$ and $B$, which is how the proof calls them.

## The least point of difference {steps: hex, hn, hmin}

Arguing by contradiction from $A \ne B$: if no $n$ lay in exactly one of the sets, then $A = B$ by extensionality, so some $n$ lies in $A \setminus B$ or in $B \setminus A$. Take the least such $n$ (`Nat.find`). By minimality, every $m < n$ lies in both sets or in neither: $m \in A \iff m \in B$.

## Nonnegativity of the terms {steps: hnonneg}

For every $m$, $2^m \ge 1$, so $2^m - 1 \ge 0$ and $t_m = \frac{1}{2^m-1} \ge 0$ (at $m=0$ this is Lean's $\frac10 = 0$).

## The case n in A, not in B {steps: s7}

Since $0 \notin A$ and $n \in A$, $n \ne 0$, so $n \ge 1$. Apply the comparison with $g = t$, $S = A$, $T = B$, using summability, nonnegativity, the agreement below $n$, and the gap lemma $\sum_{k\ge0} t_{n+1+k} < t_n$ at this $n \ge 1$. It gives $\sum_{b\in B} t_b < \sum_{a\in A} t_a$, contradicting the hypothesis that the two sums are equal.

## The case n in B, not in A {steps: s8}

Symmetric: $n \ge 1$ because $0 \notin B$, and the comparison with $S = B$, $T = A$ (agreement below $n$ read the other way round) gives $\sum_{a\in A} t_a < \sum_{b\in B} t_b$, again contradicting the equality. Hence $A = B$.
