$$\exists\, A,B,S,T\subseteq\mathbb{N},\ g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R},\ n\in\mathbb{N}:\quad 0\notin A,\quad 0\notin B,\quad \sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{2^a-1}=\sum_{b\in B}\frac{1}{2^b-1},\quad g\ \text{summable},\quad g\ge 0,\quad n\in S,\quad n\notin T,\quad \forall m<n:\ (m\in S\iff m\in T),\quad \sum_{k\ge 0} g(n+1+k)<g(n).$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, so the witness shows they can be met. It takes $A=B=\emptyset$, the function $g$ with $g(0)=1$ and $g(m)=0$ for $m\ne 0$, $S=\{0\}$, $T=\emptyset$ and $n=0$. No $m$ is below $0$, and the tail sum is $0<1=g(0)$.
