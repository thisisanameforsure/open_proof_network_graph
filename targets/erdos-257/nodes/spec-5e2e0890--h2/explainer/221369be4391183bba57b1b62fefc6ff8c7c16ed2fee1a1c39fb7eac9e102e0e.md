## Overview

The hypotheses about $A$ and $B$ are carried along and not used. The content: let $g \ge 0$ be summable, $n \in S$, $n \notin T$, $S$ and $T$ agree on $\{m : m < n\}$, and $\sum_{k\ge0} g(n+1+k) < g(n)$. Split $T$ into its part below $n$ and its part above $n$ (it does not contain $n$), and $S$ into its part below $n$ and $\{n\}$ plus the rest. The parts below $n$ coincide; the part of $T$ above $n$ has sum at most the whole tail $\sum_{m>n} g(m) < g(n)$; so $\sum_T g < \sum_S g$.

## Preparations {steps: hsum, hL, hT_sub, hS_sup, htail, hmono, hdisj1, hdisj2}

Every restriction of $g$ to a subset $U \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ is summable. Agreement below $n$ gives $\{m<n\}\cap S = \{m<n\}\cap T$. Since $n \notin T$, every $m \in T$ is either below $n$ or above it: $T \subseteq (\{m<n\}\cap T) \cup \{m > n\}$. In the other direction $(\{m<n\}\cap S)\cup\{n\} \subseteq S$. The sum of $g$ over $\{m > n\}$ is $\sum_{k\ge0} g(n+1+k)$, by the bijection $k \mapsto n+1+k$ from $\mathbb{N}$ onto $\{m>n\}$. Because $g \ge 0$, the sum over a subset is at most the sum over a larger set. Finally $\{m<n\}\cap T$ is disjoint from $\{m>n\}$, and $\{m<n\}\cap S$ from $\{n\}$.

## The chain of inequalities {steps: s9, s10, s11, s12, s13, s14, s15}

$$\sum_{T} g \;\le\; \sum_{\{m<n\}\cap T} g + \sum_{m>n} g(m) \;=\; \sum_{\{m<n\}\cap T} g + \sum_{k\ge0} g(n+1+k) \;<\; \sum_{\{m<n\}\cap T} g + g(n)$$
$$=\; \sum_{\{m<n\}\cap S} g + \sum_{\{n\}} g \;=\; \sum_{(\{m<n\}\cap S)\cup\{n\}} g \;\le\; \sum_S g .$$
The first and last steps are monotonicity under inclusion, the sums over unions split by disjointness, the strict step is the hypothesis $\sum_{k\ge0} g(n+1+k) < g(n)$, and the middle equality uses $\{m<n\}\cap T = \{m<n\}\cap S$ and the one-point sum $\sum_{\{n\}} g = g(n)$.
