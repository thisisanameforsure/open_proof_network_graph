$$\Bigl(\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N} \text{ infinite}:\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr) \;\Longrightarrow\; \Bigl(\forall\, k \ge 1:\ \sum_{m \,\in\, \{n^k \,:\, n \in \mathbb{N}\}} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \text{ is irrational}\Bigr).$$

The root's statement implies this variant (relation `partial`): for each $k \ge 1$ the map $n \mapsto n^k$ is injective on $\mathbb{N}$, so the set of perfect $k$-th powers is infinite, and the root's statement applies to it.
