label: the case A = perfect k-th powers
---
$$\forall\, k \ge 1:\qquad \sum_{m \in \{n^k \,:\, n \in \mathbb{N}\}} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \ \text{ is irrational}.$$

The set of perfect $k$-th powers includes $0 = 0^k$ and $1$. Sums are Lean's `tsum` over the set, the ordinary sum of a convergent nonnegative series; the term at $m = 0$ is Lean's $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$.
