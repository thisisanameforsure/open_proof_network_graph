## What the assembly shows

The greedy walk for $\frac12$ skips a term infinitely often, provided that after every skipped index $m\ge1$ there is another skipped index in $(m,\,3m+2]$. Here $t_m=\frac{1}{2^m-1}$ (in Lean $t_0=\frac10=0$), and the walk is any sequence with $r_0=\frac12$ and

$$r_{m+1}=\begin{cases} r_m-t_m & \text{if } t_m\le r_m,\\ r_m & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

An index $n$ is *skipped* when $r_n<t_n$, that is, the term does not fit under the remainder. The statement is that for every $m$ there is a skipped $n>m$.

The partial has one hole, the window statement. It restates another node of the graph, which as of 12 November 2026 is open: that node carries a partial of its own, whose remaining hole (the window statement for $m>100$) is open. So this node is not proved. What the partial adds is the short induction that turns a bounded window into infinitely many skips; the bound $3m+2$ is not used, only that the next skip is later.

## The window statement {steps: hwin}

The hole: for every $r$ obeying the recursion from $\frac12$ and every $m\ge1$ with $r_m<t_m$, there is $n$ with $m<n\le 3m+2$ and $r_n<t_n$.

## Step 1 is skipped {steps: h1}

Since $t_0=0\le r_0$, the recursion gives $r_1=r_0-0=\frac12$, and $t_1=\frac{1}{2-1}=1$. So $r_1<t_1$.

## A chain of skips {steps: hchain}

For every $k$ there is a skipped index $s$ with $s\ge k+1$ (and $s\ge1$). For $k=0$ take $s=1$. If $s\ge k+1$ is skipped and $s\ge1$, the window statement gives a skipped $n>s$, so $n\ge k+2$ and $n\ge 1$.

## Conclusion {steps: s4 close}

Given $m$, the chain at $k=m$ gives a skipped index $s\ge m+1>m$.
