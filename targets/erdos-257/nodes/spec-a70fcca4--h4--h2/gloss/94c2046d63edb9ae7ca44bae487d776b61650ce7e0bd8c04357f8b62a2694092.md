$$\exists\, r:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R},\ m\in\mathbb{N}:\quad r_0=\tfrac12,\qquad r_{m+1}=\begin{cases} r_m-\frac{1}{2^m-1} & \text{if } \frac{1}{2^m-1}\le r_m,\\ r_m & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\quad(\forall m).$$

These are the hypotheses of the statement, with the $m$ it quantifies over, so the witness shows they can be met. It defines $r$ by the recursion itself, starting from $\tfrac12$, so both equations hold by definition, and takes $m=0$. In Lean the term at $m=0$ is $\frac10=0$.
