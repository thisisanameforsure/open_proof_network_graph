## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $r\ge 1$, put $q=2r+1$ and $R=q^{\,r+1}$, and let $c_0,\dots,c_{r-1}:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ satisfy $c_i(0)>0$ and $R\,c_i(j)\le c_i(j+1)$ for all $i,j$. Let $A=\bigcup_i\{c_i(j):j\in\mathbb N\}$ and $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

The proof applies a Liouville-type criterion, proved in the file as the private lemma `r2_liou`: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$ and $2^CL\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* Its proof: suppose the sum is a rational $\rho$ with denominator $d$ and take $m$, $L$ for $C=4d$. Write $\rho=\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}t_a+T'$ with $T'$ the tail over $n\ge m$. Then $T'>0$, and $T'\le 4/2^m$ by comparison with a geometric series (`lac_f_le`, `lac_tail_le`). By `liou_int`, $L$ times the finite part is an integer, so $dLT'$ is an integer; it is positive, so $dLT'\ge 1$. But $2^{4d}L\le 2^m$ and $4d<2^{4d}$ give $dLT'\le 4dL/2^m<1$, a contradiction.

The criterion is used with $L=\prod_{a\in A,\,a<m}(2^a-1)$, so what is needed is an $m\in A$ exceeding the sum of the elements of $A$ below it by at least $C$. Five lemmas prepare this.

`lu_geo_sum`: if $S$ is a finite set of natural numbers, all at most $T$, and $2a\le b$ whenever $a<b$ in $S$, then $\sum_{a\in S}a\le 2T$. By strong induction on $T$: remove the maximum $M$; the rest lie in $[0,M/2]$ and sum to at most $M$.

`lu_gt`, `lu_mono`, `lu_ratio`: for one sequence $c$ with $c(0)>0$ and $R\,c(j)\le c(j+1)$, $R\ge 2$: $c(j)>j$; $c$ is strictly increasing; and $R\,c(i)\le c(j)$ for $i<j$.

`lu_window`: for every $T$ there is $k\le r$ such that every $c_i(j)$ is either $\le q^kT$ or $>q^{k+1}T$. Otherwise each of the $r+1$ windows $(q^kT,\,q^{k+1}T]$, $k=0,\dots,r$, contains some $c_i(j)$; by the pigeonhole principle two windows $k_1\ne k_2$ give the same sequence $i$. If the two indices $j$ differ, the larger element is at least $R$ times the smaller, which exceeds $R\,T$, while every window lies below $q^{r+1}T=RT$. If they are equal, one number lies in two disjoint windows. Both are impossible.

## $A$ is infinite and does not contain $0$ {steps: hR2 hmemA hinf h0A}

First $R\ge q\ge 2$. A number is in $A$ exactly when it is in the range of some $c_i$. By `lu_mono` the sequence $c_0$ is injective (this uses $r>0$), so $A$ is infinite. By `lu_gt` every $c_i(j)>j\ge 0$, so $0\notin A$. So `r2_liou` applies once its last hypothesis is checked for an arbitrary $C$.

## Choosing $m$ above an empty window {steps: s5 hCT s7 hex s9 hbelow hmbig}

Fix $C$. By `lu_window` (with $C$ in place of $T$) there is $k$ such that no element of $A$ lies in $(q^kC,\,q^{k+1}C]$. Put $T=q^kC$, so $C\le T$. Since $A$ is infinite it has an element above $T$; let $m$ be the least one. Then every $a\in A$ with $a<m$ satisfies $a\le T$ (`hbelow`). And $m$ is some $c_i(j)$ with $m>T$, so by the choice of $k$

$$m>q^{k+1}C=(2r+1)\,T$$

(`hmbig`).

## The elements of $A$ below $m$ sum to at most $2rT$ {steps: hFmem hg hfib hsum}

Let $F=\{a\in A:a<m\}$, and for each $a\in A$ choose an index $g(a)$ of a sequence whose range contains $a$. Fix $i$ and look at $F_i=\{a\in F:g(a)=i\}$. Its elements are at most $T$. If $a<b$ are in $F_i$ then $a=c_i(j_a)$, $b=c_i(j_b)$ with $j_a<j_b$ because $c_i$ is strictly increasing, so $2a\le R\,a\le b$ by `lu_ratio`. Hence $\sum_{a\in F_i}a\le 2T$ by `lu_geo_sum` (`hfib`). Summing over the $r$ values of $i$,

$$\sum_{a\in F}a\;\le\;r\cdot 2T$$

(`hsum`).

## The common multiple $L$ {steps: s16 s17}

Take $L=\prod_{a\in F}(2^a-1)$. Each $a\in F$ is in $A$, so $a\ne 0$ and $2^a-1>0$; hence $L>0$. If $a\in A$ and $a<m$ then $a\in F$, and a factor of a product divides it.

## The size condition $2^CL\le 2^m$ {steps: s18}

Each factor is at most $2^a$, so $L\le 2^{\sum_{a\in F}a}$ (`s18.hL`), and

$$2^C L\;\le\;2^{\,C+\sum_{a\in F}a}\;\le\;2^m ,$$

because $C+\sum_{a\in F}a\le T+2rT=(2r+1)T<m$.
