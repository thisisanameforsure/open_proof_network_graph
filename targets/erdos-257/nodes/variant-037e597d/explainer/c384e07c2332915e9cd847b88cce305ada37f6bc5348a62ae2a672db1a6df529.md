## Statement, idea and the helper lemmas

Let $A=\{2^k:k\ge 0\}\cup\{3^k:k\ge 0\}$ and $t_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}$. The claim is that $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.

The proof applies a Liouville-type criterion, proved in the file as the private lemma `r2_liou`: *if $A$ is infinite, $0\notin A$, and for every $C$ there are $m\in A$ and $L>0$ with $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$ and $2^CL\le 2^m$, then $\sum_{n\in A}t_n$ is irrational.* Its proof: suppose the sum is a rational $r$ with denominator $q$ and take $m$, $L$ for $C=4q$. Write $r=\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}t_a+T$ with $T$ the tail over $n\ge m$. Then $T>0$, and $T\le 4/2^m$ by comparison with a geometric series (`lac_f_le`, `lac_tail_le`). By `liou_int`, $L$ times the finite part is an integer, so $qLT$ is an integer; it is positive, so $qLT\ge 1$. But $2^{4q}L\le 2^m$ and $4q<2^{4q}$ give $qLT\le 4qL/2^m<1$, a contradiction.

Three arithmetic lemmas supply $m$.

`u23_gap`: for all $a,b$, $\;2^b\le|2^a-3\cdot 2^b|$. If $a\le b$ the difference is at most $-2\cdot 2^b$. If $a=b+d+1$ it is $2^b(2\cdot 2^d-3)$, and $|2\cdot 2^d-3|\ge 1$ (it is $1$ for $d=0$ and positive for $d\ge 1$).

`u23_bad`: if no $k$ has $3^J+C\le 2^k$ and $2^k+C\le 2\cdot 3^J$, then some $x$ has $|2^x-3^J|<C$. Take $k$ with $2^k\le 3^J<2^{k+1}$. If $3^J+C\le 2^{k+1}$, the second inequality must fail for $k+1$, so $2\cdot 3^J<2\cdot 2^k+C$ and $x=k$ works. Otherwise $3^J<2^{k+1}<3^J+C$ and $x=k+1$ works.

`u23_good`: for every $C$ there are $J,k$ with $3^J+C\le 2^k$ and $2^k+C\le 2\cdot 3^J$. Put $J=5C+1$, so $3^J\ge 5C+1$. If there were no such pair, `u23_bad` at $J$ and at $J+1$ gives $x,y$ with $|2^x-3^J|<C$ and $|2^y-3^{J+1}|<C$. Since $2^y-3\cdot 2^x=(2^y-3^{J+1})-3(2^x-3^J)$, we get $|2^y-3\cdot 2^x|<4C$. But $2^x>3^J-C\ge 4C$, and `u23_gap` gives $|2^y-3\cdot 2^x|\ge 2^x$, a contradiction.

## $A$ is infinite and does not contain $0$ {steps: hinf h0}

$A$ contains the powers of two, which are the range of the injective map $k\mapsto 2^k$, so $A$ is infinite. No power of $2$ or of $3$ is $0$. So `r2_liou` applies once its last hypothesis is checked for an arbitrary $C$.

## Choosing $m$ and $L$ {steps: s3}

Fix $C$. By `u23_good` there are $J,k$ with

$$3^J+C\le 2^k\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2^k+C\le 2\cdot 3^J .$$

Take

$$m=\min\bigl(2^{k+1},\,3^{J+1}\bigr),\qquad L=\bigl(2^{2^k}-1\bigr)\bigl(2^{3^J}-1\bigr).$$

## $m\in A$ and $L>0$ {steps: s4 s5}

The minimum is one of the two numbers, a power of $2$ or a power of $3$, so $m\in A$. Both $2^k$ and $3^J$ are nonzero, so $2^{2^k}>1$ and $2^{3^J}>1$, and both factors of $L$ are positive.

## Every $2^a-1$ with $a\in A$, $a<m$ divides $L$ {steps: s6}

If $a=2^i<m$ then $2^i<2^{k+1}$, so $i\le k$ and $2^i\mid 2^k$. Since $u\mid v$ implies $(2^u-1)\mid(2^v-1)$, the number $2^a-1$ divides the first factor of $L$. If $a=3^i<m$ then $3^i<3^{J+1}$, so $i\le J$, $3^i\mid 3^J$, and $2^a-1$ divides the second factor.

## The size condition $2^CL\le 2^m$ {steps: s7}

Dropping the two $-1$'s,

$$2^C L\;\le\;2^C\cdot 2^{2^k}\cdot 2^{3^J}\;=\;2^{\,C+2^k+3^J}.$$

It remains to see $C+2^k+3^J\le m$, that is, both $C+2^k+3^J\le 2\cdot 2^k$ and $C+2^k+3^J\le 3\cdot 3^J$. The first is $3^J+C\le 2^k$ and the second is $2^k+C\le 2\cdot 3^J$, the two inequalities from `u23_good`.
