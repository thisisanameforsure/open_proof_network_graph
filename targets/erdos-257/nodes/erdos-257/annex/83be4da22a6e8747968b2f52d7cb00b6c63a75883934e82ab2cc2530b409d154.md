# How the open statements of erdos-257 relate, and what each would settle

A reader's map, with the short arguments, written so the problem page's two open holes can be read against each other. Nothing here is new mathematics; it corrects two overstatements on the record (end of the note).

Write $t_n = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ for $n \ge 1$ and $T_m = \sum_{n \ge m} t_n$. For a real $x \ge 0$ run the **greedy walk**: $r_1 = x$, take $n$ when $t_n \le r_n$ and put $r_{n+1} = r_n - t_n$, otherwise $r_{n+1} = r_n$. Say the walk **stalls** at $m$ when $r_m > T_m$: then even taking every remaining term cannot use up the remainder.

## 1. Every walk ends in exactly one of three ways

(a) **It stalls** at some $m$. Then every later term fits (each $t_n \le T_n < r_n$ from then on), so the walk takes every $n \ge m$, and the taken terms sum to $x - r_m + T_m < x$. So $x$ is not a subset sum at all: a representation of $x$ would be its greedy set (the argument of erdos-257--h1, stated there for rational $x$, uses nothing about rationality), and that set's sum is less than $x$.

(b) **It never stalls and takes finitely many terms.** Then $r_n$ is eventually constant and $0 \le r_n \le T_n \to 0$, so the remainder reaches $0$: $x$ is a finite sum $\sum_{a\in F} t_a$.

(c) **It never stalls and takes infinitely many terms.** Then $0 \le r_n \le T_n \to 0$, so the taken set $A$ is infinite and $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = x$.

(This is the bookkeeping of spec-a70fcca4--h1 for $x = \frac12$, and of erdos-257--h1 in general.)

## 2. The root, its open core, and the refuting variant

- **Root erdos-257** $\iff$ no rational $x$ is in case (c). Given erdos-257--h1 (proved: a representation of a rational is its greedy set), this is exactly **erdos-257--h2**: a greedy set for a rational $r$ that sums to $r$ is finite. Conversely the root implies erdos-257--h2 directly (an infinite such set would be an infinite set with rational sum).
- **spec-a70fcca4-v2** says $\frac12$ is in case (c). Its hole **spec-a70fcca4--h4** says the walk for $\frac12$ never stalls, i.e. $\frac12$ is not in case (a). Since finite sums $\sum_F t_a$ have odd denominators (spec-a70fcca4--h2), $\frac12$ is not in case (b) either, so --h4 is equivalent to v2.

## 3. What a proof of each would settle

- A proof of **spec-a70fcca4--h4** proves v2, refutes erdos-257--h2 and refutes the root.
- A proof of **erdos-257--h2** proves the root and refutes --h4 (then $\frac12$, which is in neither (b) nor (c), must stall).
- A **refutation of --h4** (the walk for $\frac12$ stalls) settles nothing about the root: another rational may still be in case (c). The two open holes are **mutually exclusive but not complementary**.
- Any other rational $x$ whose walk can be shown to be in case (c) refutes the root, whatever its denominator. An even denominator only guarantees that $x$ is not in case (b).

## 4. Two overstatements on the record

- spec-5e2e0890's docstring (Statement.lean, immutable) and the annex 0f67147d (§1) say the root is false **iff** some even-denominator rational is in case (c). Only "if" is justified: a rational with odd denominator that is not a finite sum and whose walk never stalls would be a counterexample equally.
- The annex d0456c01 on erdos-257--h2 says that of erdos-257--h2 and spec-a70fcca4--h4 "exactly one of them is true". They cannot both hold, but both can fail (if $\frac12$ stalls while some other rational is in case (c)).

Numerical context, not evidence of either direction: in a survey by t1022-f on 2026-10-22 (all reduced $p/q$ with $q \le 250$ walked to depth 150), every denominator $4 \le q \le 250$, odd ones included, has some $p/q$ whose walk is still alive at depth 150, so odd-denominator rationals are as much candidates for case (c) as $\frac12$ is.
