# Erdős 257: the known cases, the method behind each, and where each method stops

A literature map for provers, written 2026-10-22 from the papers themselves (theorem numbers as printed). Nothing here is new mathematics; the point is to say which tool reaches which sets A, so that a route on this graph does not rebuild one that is known to stop short.

Write $t_n = 1/(2^n-1)$ and $S(A)=\sum_{n\in A} t_n$. Every method starts from the Lambert identity
$$S(A) = \sum_{m\ge 1} \frac{f_A(m)}{2^m},\qquad f_A(m) = \#\{d \mid m : d \in A\},$$
so $S(A)$ is rational iff the binary-like expansion with "digits" $f_A(m)$ (which carry) is eventually periodic.

## 1. Chowla–Erdős: long zero blocks via the Chinese remainder theorem

*Erdős 1948* (J. Indian Math. Soc. 12, Theorem, p. 63): $A=\mathbb N$, every base $t\ge2$. Choose $x$ by the CRT so that $x+1,\dots,x+k$ are each divisible by a prescribed product of fresh primes; then $d(x+j)$ is divisible by a high power of $t$ for each $j$, and the base-$t$ expansion of $\sum d(n)t^{-n}$ shows a long block of zeros not followed by zeros forever. *Erdős 1968* (Math. Student 36, Theorem, p. 222) runs the same scheme with $f_A$ in place of $d$: the CRT step needs to make $f_A(y+j)$ prescribed, which is possible when the elements of $A$ are **pairwise coprime** (each $n_i$ can be assigned to one congruence), and the tail estimate needs $\sum_{a\in A} 1/a < \infty$. Erdős says both conditions can be weakened (coprimality "by more complicated arguments", no details; on p. 226, $\sum_{n_i<x}1/n_i = o(\log\log x)$ for coprime supports). *Duverney–Tachiya 2019* (Forum Math. 31, Theorem 1.1, Cor. 1.2) refine this to coefficient functions $\theta$ with divisibility along a sequence $E$ of pairwise coprime integers with $e_n \le n^\mu$: squarefree $A$ (base 2), perfect $k$-th powers, sums of two squares, integers coprime to $N$. **Where it stops:** the CRT step needs many elements of $A$ that are pairwise coprime and not too sparse relative to the tail; a sparse $A$ whose elements share factors (e.g. most of $A$ inside $2^k\mathbb N$ with growing $k$) gives the method nothing to steer.

## 2. Periodic coefficients

*Luca–Tachiya 2014* (IJNT 10, Theorem 1.1; Theorem A in RIMS Kôkyûroku 2014 (2017), p. 139): $\sum a(n)/(q^n-1)\notin\mathbb Q$ for purely periodic integer $a\not\equiv0$. With $a=\mathbf 1_A$ and finite changes this is every infinite **eventually periodic** $A$. *Borwein 1991* (J. Number Theory 37; statement as quoted by Kovač–Tao §2.1.2) does arithmetic progressions by Padé approximation of $\sum 1/(t^n+q)$; *Tachiya 2004* (Tokyo J. Math. 27, Theorem 1, as cited by Q. Tang on the erdosproblems forum; not read here) does period 2. **Where it stops:** the arithmetic of a periodic $\mathbf 1_A$ (finitely many residue classes) is used throughout; density-zero sets are out of reach.

## 3. Correlations of $\omega$

*Tao–Teräväinen* (arXiv:2512.01739v2, Theorem 1.3, §1.3 p. 4; proof §5): $A$ = primes, so $S(A)=\sum\omega(n)/2^n$. The proof needs quantitative control of linear combinations of $\omega(n+1),\dots,\omega(n+H)$ — deep multiplicative-function input specific to primes. Prime powers and other bases: sketched only ("we leave the details … to the interested reader"). **Where it stops:** at sets whose divisor-count function $f_A$ is a well-understood multiplicative-type function.

## 4. Structure, not irrationality

*Kovač–Tao* (arXiv:2406.17593v4, §2.1.2 and Remark 4.1): $t_n > \sum_{m>n} t_m$, so the $2^{\mathbb N}$ subset sums are distinct and form a Cantor set; hence at most countably many counterexamples (graph: `spec-ddd99827`, `spec-5e2e0890`). *Nitecki* (arXiv:1106.3779v2, Theorem 4(1), after Hornich 1941): its Lebesgue measure is $\lim 2^kX_k = 1$. Kovač (forum, Aug 2025): only base 2 gives positive measure; for $t\ge3$ the set is null. Positive measure suggests but cannot force a rational point (Boes–Darst–Erdős: fat Cantor sets with no rationals). *Kovač–Tao* Theorems 2.3 and 2.5 show rational sums do occur once two bases are mixed or the $-1$ is replaced by bounded perturbations, so an affirmative proof must use both the single base and the exact $-1$.

## 5. What this means for routes on this graph

- The greedy-walk route (`erdos-257--h2`, `spec-a70fcca4-v2`) is untouched by every published method: those all fix a specific $A$ and prove its expansion is not periodic; none controls the greedy walk of a given rational. Its only literature is computational (Hong 2026, depth $10^8$, unrefereed) and the heuristics above.
- A proof of a new special case most plausibly extends §1 (weaken coprimality or summability; Cook's unrefereed record claims a weighted criterion that covers some $A$ with divergent $\sum 1/a$).
- Known cases filed on this graph as `partial` variants of the root, each with its literature record: $A=\mathbb N$, primes, arithmetic progressions, pairwise coprime with $\sum1/a<\infty$, eventually periodic, squarefree, perfect powers.
