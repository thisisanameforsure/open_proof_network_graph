# Erdős 257: the greedy walk, the carry bookkeeping, and what a disproof would have to look like

Working notes from a two-hour session (2026-10-19), drafted by a model and checked by
computation where stated. Nothing here proves or refutes the root. The aim is to record the
structure every attack on this problem has to go through, the two ideas the owner proposed,
and what the computations say about them.

## 1. The subset-sum set is a Cantor set with unique representations

Write $t_n = 1/(2^n-1)$ and $S = \{\sum_{a\in A} t_a : A \subseteq \mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}\}$.

**Gap lemma** (crux `spec-ddd99827`, Lean-checked on the hosted checker; Kovač–Tao
arXiv:2406.17593, Remark 4.1): for every $n \ge 1$,
$$\sum_{m>n} \frac{1}{2^m-1} \;<\; \frac{1}{2^n-1}.$$
Proof: for $m \ge n+1$, $2^m - 1 \ge 2^m(1-2^{-(n+1)})$, so the tail is at most
$\frac{2^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}-1}\cdot 2^{-n} = \frac{2}{2^{n+1}-1} < \frac{1}{2^n-1}$.

Consequences.

* **Uniqueness** (crux `spec-5e2e0890`): if $\sum_A t_a = \sum_B t_b$ with $0\notin A,B$ then
  $A = B$. Look at the least element $n$ of the symmetric difference, say $n \in A\setminus B$:
  then $\sum_A - \sum_B \ge t_n - \sum_{m>n} t_m > 0$.
* **The greedy walk is the only candidate.** For a target $x$, put $r_0 = x$ and, for
  $n = 1, 2, \dots$, take $n$ iff $r_{n-1} \ge t_n$. Then $x \in S$ iff the walk never
  *stalls*, i.e. never leaves $r_n > \sum_{m>n} t_m$. The stall window after step $n$ has width
  $t_{n+1} - \sum_{m>n+1} t_m \approx \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 4^{-n}$ on a scale where $r_n \approx 2^{-n}$.
* **$S$ has measure 1** (fat Cantor set): the $2^n$ level-$n$ intervals have total length
  $2^n \sum_{m>n} t_m \to 1$, while $S \subseteq [0, 1.6067]$. This is why Kovač expects $S$ to
  contain rationals (forum, Aug 2025) and why no measure argument can find one: fat Cantor sets
  with no rationals exist (Boes–Darst–Erdős 1981).
* **Even denominators force infinitude.** A finite sum $\sum_{a\in F} t_a$ has odd denominator
  (every $2^a - 1$ is odd), so a rational with even denominator is in $S$ only through an
  infinite $A$. Hence the root (the affirmative statement) is false iff some even-denominator
  rational, for example $1/2$, has a greedy walk that never stalls (crux `spec-a70fcca4`).
  Conversely a rational with odd denominator might be a finite sum ($1/3 = t_2$, $1 = t_1$),
  and $2/3$, $3/4$ are in the first gap $(1.6067 - 1,\ 1)$ and not in $S$ at all.

## 2. Idea 1: the greedy walk to $a/b$ — find a formula or a contradiction

The greedy walk for $1/2$ is $A = \{2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 20, 21, 26, 27, 32, 33, 34, 35, 38, 39, 42,
44, \dots\}$. Run in exact rational arithmetic to $n = 400$ and in 5000-bit arithmetic to
$n = 2500$ it never stalls; $|A \cap [1,2500]| = 1246$. The same holds to $n = 2500$ for
$1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 3/8, 1/6, 1/10, 1/12, 1/14, 1/22$ and $1/21$, each with density close to $1/2$.
Hong (Zenodo 23287959, 10 Oct 2026, unrefereed) reports the walk for $1/2$ to depth $10^8$
without stalling. Under the natural random model the probability of a stall after step $n$ is
about $2^{-n}/6$ per step, so the computations are consistent with $1/2 \in S$ and say nothing
rigorous.

**Carry bookkeeping.** Since $t_a = \sum_{j\ge1} 2^{-aj}$, every subset sum is
$\sum_{m\ge1} d_A(m)\,2^{-m}$ with $d_A(m) = \#\{a\in A : a \mid m\}$. For $x = 1/2$ let
$c_m = 2^{m-1}\bigl(\tfrac12 - \sum_{j<m} d_A(j)2^{-j}\bigr)$ be the carry into position $m$.
Then $\sum_A t_a = 1/2$ **iff** $c_2 = 1$ and the integer recursion
$$c_{m+1} = 2c_m - d_A(m)$$
stays non-negative for ever (telescoping gives $\sum_{j\ge m} d_A(j)2^{-j} = c_m 2^{-(m-1)}$).
Along the greedy walk for $1/2$ the carries over $m \le 400$ take the values
$1$ (42 times), $2$ (194), $3$ (103), $4$ (42), $5$ (12), $6$ (4), $7$ (once). So a counterexample
is an infinite $A$ whose divisor-count function $d_A$ tracks a bounded carry sequence:
$d_A(m) = 2c_m - c_{m+1}$ with $c_m \ge 1$. What this rules out: $c$ eventually constant is
impossible (a prime $p$ has $d_A(p) \le 1$, so $c_{p+1} \ge 2c_p - 1$, and $d_A(m) \equiv c$ for
all large $m$ fails at $m = 6$ already); the carry must climb at primes and be paid down at
numbers with many divisors in $A$. The sets found have density about $1/2$ and many non-coprime
pairs, as they must: Erdős 1968 shows a pairwise coprime $A$ with $\sum 1/a < \infty$ gives an
irrational sum, and every arithmetic progression, the evens, the odds, the primes and the
prime powers are known irrational, so a rational-sum $A$ can be neither sparse-and-coprime nor
periodic. No formula was found for the walk of $1/2$ or any other target; the gap sequence
$1, 3, 1, 7, 6, 1, 5, 1, 5, 1, 1, 1, 3, \dots$ shows no periodicity and the walks for different
targets share no visible structure. The walk for $1/6$ is the walk for $1/2$ without the
element $2$, since $1/2 = t_2 + 1/6$; nothing deeper was seen.

**A periodicity criterion.** For $x = p/q$ with $q$ odd and $P$ a multiple of the order of $2$
modulo $q$, $(2^P - 1)x \in \mathbb{Z}$, and $(2^P-1)/(2^a-1)$ is an integer whenever $a \mid P$.
So $x \in \mathbb{Q}$ with odd denominator iff for some $P \ge 1$
$$\sum_{a\in A,\ a\nmid P} \frac{2^{P \bmod a} - 1}{2^a - 1} \in \mathbb{Z}$$
(with a factor $2^k$ inside for even denominators). The terms with $a > P$ equal
$(2^P-1)\,t_a$ and tend to $0$, so a rational sum is a finite-looking sum of fractional parts
landing exactly on an integer. This is the shape Erdős's 1948 and 1968 arguments break: choosing
$P$ with many divisors in $A$ kills many terms, and a long block of small $d_A$ values gives a
run of zeros in the binary expansion. Both arguments need control of $d_A$ on a block of
consecutive integers, which is what the known cases ($A = \mathbb{N}$, $A$ coprime and sparse,
$A$ the primes via Tao–Teräväinen's correlation estimates) provide and an arbitrary $A$ does not.

## 3. Idea 2: subsets one has reason to suspect converge to a rational

Uniqueness turns this idea on its head: a set cannot be designed to have a rational sum, because
each rational determines its set. Three families were looked at.

* **Geometric sets** $A = \{a\cdot d^k\}$: $\sum_k 1/(2^{a d^k}-1)$ is $f(2^{-a})$ for the Mahler
  function $f(z) = \sum_{k\ge0} z^{d^k}/(1-z^{d^k})$, which satisfies the functional equation
  $f(z) - f(z^d) = z/(1-z)$; Mahler's method is the expected route to transcendence of such
  values, though no source applying it to exactly this sum was found. Not a candidate for a
  rational sum.
* **Periodic sets** (finite unions of residue classes, finite modifications): known irrational
  for one class (Erdős 1948 at base $2^d$, Borwein 1991, Tachiya 2004); the general periodic case
  was not found in print but is the same Lambert-series territory and is the opposite of a
  candidate.
* **Sets with a telescoping identity.** The identities available, such as
  $\sum_{k\ge0} 2^k/(2^{2^k a}+1) = 1/(2^a-1)$, carry coefficients $2^k$ or the sign $+1$, never a
  plain subset of the Mersenne reciprocals. No subset identity $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = $ rational with
  $A$ infinite was found, in line with Erdős's 1968 remark that he knew none "though it seems
  possible that this can happen".

So the only concrete candidates remain the greedy sets of §2, which are evidence, not structure.
