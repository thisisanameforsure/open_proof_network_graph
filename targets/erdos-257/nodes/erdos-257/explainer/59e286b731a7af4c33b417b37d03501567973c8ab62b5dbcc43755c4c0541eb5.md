## Overview

Write $t_n = \frac{1}{2^n-1}$ (Lean's $t_0 = \frac10 = 0$). The partial proof reduces the root to its second hole. Let $A$ be infinite and suppose, for contradiction, that $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = r$ with $r \in \mathbb{Q}$. Removing $0$ from $A$ changes neither the sum nor infiniteness. By the first hole, $A \setminus \{0\}$ is the greedy set for $r$; by the second, a greedy set for a rational that sums to it is finite. That contradicts infiniteness, so the sum is irrational. The first hole is proved; the second, erdos-257--h2, is open.

## The two holes {steps: h_greedy, h_core}

- h_greedy (erdos-257--h1, proved): if $0 \notin A$ and $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = r \in \mathbb{Q}$, then for every $n \ge 1$, $n \in A \iff t_n \le r - \sum_{a\in A,\ a<n} t_a$. In words: a representation of a rational is the greedy one.
- h_core (erdos-257--h2, open): if $0 \notin G$, $G$ satisfies that same greedy condition for a rational $r$, and $\sum_{a\in G} t_a = r$, then $G$ is finite.

## Removing zero changes nothing {steps: h0, hA'}

Write both sums as sums over all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ of the indicator of the set times $t$. The two indicator functions agree at every $x \ne 0$ (membership in $A \setminus\{0\}$ and in $A$ coincide there), and at $x = 0$ both are $0$ because $t_0 = 0$ in Lean. So $\sum_{a\in A} t_a = \sum_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}} t_a$. Removing one point from an infinite set leaves it infinite.

## Applying the holes {steps: hsum, hgr, close}

From the assumption, $\sum_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}} t_a = r$. Since $0 \notin A \setminus \{0\}$, h_greedy says $A \setminus \{0\}$ satisfies the greedy condition for $r$, and then h_core says it is finite, contradicting the previous section. Hence no rational $r$ equals the sum: it is irrational.
