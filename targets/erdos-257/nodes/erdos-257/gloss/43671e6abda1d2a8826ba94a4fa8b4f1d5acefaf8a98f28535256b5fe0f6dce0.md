$$\forall\, A \subseteq \mathbb{N}\ \text{infinite}:\qquad \sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1} \ \text{ is irrational}.$$

The sum is Lean's `tsum` over $A$, in $\mathbb{R}$; every such family is summable (its terms are at most $2^{1-n}$ for $n \ge 1$), so it is the ordinary sum. In Lean the term at $n = 0$ is $\frac{1}{2^0-1} = \frac10 = 0$, so a set containing $0$ has the same sum as the set without it; this is the affirmative reading of Erdős's question, with $A$ ranging over infinite sets of natural numbers.
