## What the assembly shows

Split the infinite sets $A$ into fifteen classes, for each of which the irrationality of $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is a separate statement already on the graph, and one remaining case, every other set. The partial has sixteen holes and no mathematics of its own beyond the case split. It proves the root only if all sixteen hold. As of 12 November 2026 fourteen of the fifteen class statements are proved on the graph, the case of the primes is open, and the last hole, which carries the whole problem for every set outside the classes, is open. The root is not proved.

## The fifteen classes {steps: h_naturals h_progressions h_periodic h_coprime h_squarefree h_powers h_two_squares h_primes h_ratio_two h_liouville h_long_gaps h_two_and_three h_two_bases h_two_chains h_lacunary_union}

Each hole states that the sum is irrational for one class of sets, word for word as a variant node states it:

1. $A=\mathbb{N}$ (Erdős 1948);
2. $A=\{a+dn\}$ with $d\ge 1$;
3. $A$ infinite and eventually periodic;
4. $A$ infinite, pairwise coprime, with $\sum_{a\in A}1/a<\infty$;
5. the squarefree numbers;
6. the perfect $k$-th powers, $k\ge1$;
7. the sums of two squares;
8. the primes (Tao–Teräväinen), the one class hole still open;
9. $0\notin A$ infinite with $m\ge 2n$ for all $n<m$ in $A$ beyond some $N$;
10. $0\notin A$ infinite meeting the Liouville criterion: for every $C$ some $m\in A$ and $L>0$ have $(2^a-1)\mid L$ for all $a\in A$ below $m$, and $2^CL\le 2^m$;
11. $0\notin A$ infinite with, for every $C$, some $m\in A$ with $m\ge C+\sum_{a\in A,\,a<m}a$;
12. the powers of $2$ together with the powers of $3$;
13. the powers of $a$ together with the powers of $b$, $2\le a<b$;
14. the union of two sequences $x$, $y$ with $x_j\mid x_{j+1}$, $3x_j\le x_{j+1}$, and the same for $y$;
15. a union of $r\ge1$ sequences $c_i$ with $c_i(j+1)\ge(2r+1)^{r+1}c_i(j)$.

The classes overlap: the partial's own comment notes that 12 is contained in 13, that 1 and 2 are contained in 3, and that 9 and 11 follow from 10.

## What is left {steps: h_rest}

The last hole says: every infinite $A$ is in one of the fifteen classes, or $\sum_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^n-1}$ is irrational. It is stated as a sixteen-way disjunction. For a set in none of the classes it asserts exactly what the problem asks, so this hole is no easier than the problem restricted to those sets; the root also implies it at once.

## The assembly {steps: s17 s18 s19 s20 s21 s22 s23 s24 s25 s26 s27 s28 s29 s30 s31 s32}

Given an infinite $A$, apply the last hole and split into its sixteen cases. In case $k\le 15$ the set is of class $k$ (in the cases where the class is a single family, $A$ is replaced by the family's set) and hole $k$ gives the irrationality. In the first case the sum over the members of the whole of $\mathbb{N}$ is first rewritten as a sum over $n\in\mathbb{N}$, where the term at $n=0$ is $\frac10=0$ in Lean. In the sixteenth case the irrationality is the disjunct itself.
