Informal argument (Euclid, Elements IX.20, in its original form: no finite list contains all the primes).

Let l be a finite list of natural numbers and let s be the sum of its entries.
Lemma 1 (bound). Every entry x of l satisfies x <= s, because the other entries are non-negative.
Lemma 2 (euclid). For every n there is a prime p with n < p: let N = (n+1)! + 1 and let p be the least divisor of N that is at least 2. p is prime. If p <= n+1 then p divides (n+1)!, hence p divides N - (n+1)! = 1, which is impossible; so p > n+1 > n.
Assembly. Apply Lemma 2 with n = s to get a prime p > s. If p were an entry of l, Lemma 1 would give p <= s, a contradiction. So p is a prime not in l.
