## Overview

Write $\theta = 21/40$ and, for integers $n$ and $k \ge 1$,
$$B(k,n) = \Bigl\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} : k < m < k + k^\theta,\ \bigl|\tfrac nm - \mathrm{round}(\tfrac nm)\bigr| < k^{\theta - 1} \Bigr\}.$$
The claim is that for every $C > 0$ and all large $k$, $\#B(k,n) < C k^\theta/\log k$ whenever $\tfrac12 k^{2+\theta} < n \le \exp\bigl(\log^2 k/(20\log\log k)\bigr)$. As on the medium-large range, the proof is one application of the counting bound of the used node `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1` (van Doorn and Tang's application of Konyagin's theorem to $f(x) = (-1)^r n/(k+x)$; below, Konyagin's bound): for real $\lambda \ge 1$ and integer $2 \le r \le \tfrac12 k^{1-\theta}$,
$$\#B(k,n) < 65536\,k^\theta\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}{k^{r+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r-1}} + \Bigl(\frac{k^{r+\theta}}{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}\Bigr)^{\frac1{r-1}} + \Bigl(\frac{(r+1)\lambda}{k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r}}\Bigr] + 2r\lambda .$$
Here $n$ may be as large as a power of $k$ whose exponent grows with $k$, so the order $r$ must grow with $n$: it is the least $r$ with $n\,r! \le k^{r+\theta}$.

## The order r {steps: s3 s15}

This is the helper lemma `large_r_data` (with `large_r0_bounds` and `large_r0_P`). Let $r_0 = \lceil \log k/(10\log\log k)\rceil$. For large $k$ and $0 < n \le \exp\bigl(\log^2 k/(20\log\log k)\bigr)$ one has $n\,r_0! \le k^{r_0+\theta}$, so the least $r$ with $n\,r! \le k^{r+\theta}$ exists and is at most $r_0$. Since $n > \tfrac12 k^{2+\theta}$, the values $r = 0, 1, 2$ fail, so $r \ge 3$; and minimality gives $n\,(r-1)! > k^{r-1+\theta}$. From $r \le r_0$, for large $k$:
$$3r - 2 < \tfrac{7}{20}\frac{\log k}{\log\log k},\qquad r \le \tfrac{3}{20}\frac{\log k}{\log\log k},\qquad r \le \log k,\qquad r \le \tfrac12 k^{1-\theta},\qquad r+1 \le k^{\theta/r}.$$

## The choice of λ and the raw bound {steps: hK hraw}

This is the helper lemma `large_card_raw` (with `lamLarge_pow` and `lamLarge_ge_one`). Put $E_1 = (1-\theta)\dfrac{2r-1}{3r-2}$ and choose $\lambda$ by $\lambda^r = \dfrac{k^{r+1-E_1}}{n\,r!}$. Then $\lambda \ge 1$, because $n\,r! \le k^{r+\theta}$ and $E_1 \le 1 - \theta$. The first two Konyagin terms become equal:
$$\Bigl(\frac{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}{k^{r+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r-1}} = k^{-E_1/(2r-1)} = k^{\frac{\theta-1}{3r-2}},\qquad \Bigl(\frac{k^{r+\theta}}{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}\Bigr)^{\frac1{r-1}} = k^{\frac{\theta - 1 + E_1}{r-1}} = k^{\frac{\theta-1}{3r-2}} .$$
So
$$\#B(k,n) < 65536\,k^\theta\Bigl[2k^{\frac{\theta-1}{3r-2}} + \Bigl(\frac{(r+1)\lambda}{k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r}}\Bigr] + 2r\lambda .$$

## The three terms are small {steps: s2 hbnd}

This is the helper lemma `large_asym` (with `lamLarge_lt`, `large_term1_le` and `large_term3_le`). From $n\,(r-1)! > k^{r-1+\theta}$ one gets $\lambda < k^{(2-\theta)/r}$. Then, for large $k$:

- $k^{(\theta-1)/(3r-2)} \le (\log k)^{-19/14}$, because $3r - 2 < \tfrac{7}{20}\log k/\log\log k$ gives $\dfrac{(1-\theta)\log k}{3r-2} > \dfrac{19}{14}\log\log k$ (here $1 - \theta = 19/40$);
- $\bigl((r+1)\lambda/k\bigr)^{1/(2r)} \le (\log k)^{-10/9}$, from $(r+1)\lambda < k^{2/r}$, hence a base below $k^{2/r - 1}$, and $r \le \tfrac{3}{20}\log k/\log\log k$;
- $2r\lambda \le 2\log k \cdot k^{(2-\theta)/3}$, from $r \le \log k$ and $r \ge 3$.

Each of $k^\theta(\log k)^{-19/14}$, $k^\theta(\log k)^{-10/9}$ and $k^{(2-\theta)/3}\log k$ is $o(k^\theta/\log k)$: the first two because the exponent of $\log k$ is below $-1$, the third because $(2-\theta)/3 = 59/120 < \theta$. So the right-hand side is at most $C k^\theta/\log k$ for large $k$, uniformly in $n$ and $r$.

## Conclusion {steps: hki1 hki2 hk2le hk1 hk1' hkR hk2R hkpow hknR hkn hn0 hcard hbS close}

Take $k_0$ past the thresholds of the two previous sections, and at least $2$. For $k \ge k_0$ and $n$ in the range: $n > \tfrac12 k^{2+\theta} \ge k$, so $n > k > 0$; pick $r$ as above; the raw bound and the estimate of its right-hand side give $\#B(k,n) < C k^\theta/\log k$. The last lines rewrite the helper's set, which filters the integer interval $(k, k + \lfloor k^\theta\rfloor + 2)$ by the two conditions on $m$, as the statement's set, which also repeats $k < m$.
