$$\neg\,\text{BHP}\ \ \lor\ \ \exists k_0\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall k\ge k_0\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}:\ \ 2k\le n\le\tfrac12\bigl(\tfrac{k}{2}\bigr)^{2-21/40}+2k\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists p\text{ prime},\ \ k<2p,\ \ (n\bmod p)+p<k$$

Either the Baker–Harman–Pintz short-interval prime count fails, or for all large $k$ and every natural number $n$ with $2k\le n\le\tfrac12(k/2)^{2-21/40}+2k$ there is a prime $p>k/2$ with $(n\bmod p)+p<k$. Here BHP is the statement of the node `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h1`, word for word: $\exists C>0\ \exists k_0\ \forall k\ge k_0:\ C\,k^{21/40}/\log k\le\#\{p\text{ prime}:k<p<k+k^{21/40}\}$, the primes counted as the integers of the interval $(\lfloor k\rfloor,\lceil k+k^{21/40}\rceil)$ that are positive, prime in absolute value and lie strictly between $k$ and $k+k^{21/40}$ over the reals.

$k$, $n$ and $p$ are natural numbers and $n\bmod p$ is the remainder of $n$ on division by $p$. The upper bound on $n$ is an inequality of real numbers: $k/2$ is the real number (not rounded), $2-21/40$ is a real exponent and $\log$ is the natural logarithm.

Since $n\bmod p\ge 0$, the conclusion forces $k/2<p<k$. The inequality $(n\bmod p)+p<k$ says exactly that the two largest multiples of $p$ not exceeding $n$, namely $n-(n\bmod p)$ and $n-(n\bmod p)-p$, both lie among the $k$ consecutive integers $n-k+1,\dots,n$.
