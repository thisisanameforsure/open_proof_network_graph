## Overview

Write $\theta = 21/40$ and, for integers $n$ and $k \ge 1$,
$$B(k,n) = \Bigl\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} : k < m < k + k^\theta,\ \bigl|\tfrac nm - \mathrm{round}(\tfrac nm)\bigr| < k^{\theta - 1} \Bigr\}.$$
The claim is that for every $C > 0$ and all large $k$, $\#B(k,n) < C k^\theta/\log k$ whenever $k^2/\log^2 k < n \le \tfrac12 k^{2+\theta}$. The proof is one application of the counting bound of the used node `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1` (van Doorn and Tang's application of Konyagin's theorem to $f(x) = (-1)^r n/(k+x)$; below, Konyagin's bound), which for real $\lambda \ge 1$ and integer $2 \le r \le \tfrac12 k^{1-\theta}$ gives
$$\#B(k,n) < 65536\,k^\theta\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}{k^{r+1}}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r-1}} + \Bigl(\frac{k^{r+\theta}}{n\,r!\,\lambda^r}\Bigr)^{\frac1{r-1}} + \Bigl(\frac{(r+1)\lambda}{k}\Bigr)^{\frac1{2r}}\Bigr] + 2r\lambda ,$$
taken at $r = 2$ and $\lambda = \sqrt{k^{2+\theta}/(2n)}\,\log k$, followed by four comparisons of a power of $k$ times a power of $\log k$ with $k^\theta/\log k$.

## The choice of λ and the bound g(k) {steps: s1}

This is the helper lemma `ml_card_bound`. With $r = 2$ and $\lambda^2 = \dfrac{k^{2+\theta}}{2n}\log^2 k$ we have $n \cdot 2! \cdot \lambda^2 = k^{2+\theta}\log^2 k$, so the first two terms no longer involve $n$:
$$\Bigl(\frac{2n\lambda^2}{k^3}\Bigr)^{1/3} = \bigl(k^{\theta-1}\log^2 k\bigr)^{1/3}, \qquad \frac{k^{2+\theta}}{2n\lambda^2} = \log^{-2} k .$$
The hypotheses of the bound hold for large $k$: $\lambda \ge 1$ because $n \le \tfrac12 k^{2+\theta}$ makes the square root at least $1$ and $\log k \ge 1$ for $k \ge 3$; $2 \le \tfrac12 k^{1-\theta}$ eventually; and $k < n$ because $n > k^2/\log^2 k > k$ once $\log^2 k < k$. The lower bound $n > k^2/\log^2 k$ gives $\lambda \le \lambda^* := 2^{-1/2} k^{\theta/2}\log^2 k$, which bounds the two terms that still contain $\lambda$. Altogether
$$\#B(k,n) < g(k) := 65536\,k^\theta\Bigl[\bigl(k^{\theta-1}\log^2 k\bigr)^{1/3} + \log^{-2}k + \Bigl(\frac{3\lambda^*}{k}\Bigr)^{1/4}\Bigr] + 4\lambda^* .$$

## g(k) is below C k^θ / log k {steps: s2}

This is the helper lemma `ml_rhs_le`. Each of the four terms of $g$ is at most $\tfrac C4\,k^\theta/\log k$ for large $k$:

- $65536\,k^{(4\theta-1)/3}\log^{2/3}k$, and $(4\theta-1)/3 = 11/30 < \theta$;
- $65536\,k^\theta\log^{-2}k$, which is $k^\theta/\log k$ times $65536/\log k \to 0$;
- $65536\,(3/\sqrt2)^{1/4}\,k^{(9\theta-2)/8}\log^{1/2}k$, and $(9\theta-2)/8 = 109/320 < \theta$;
- $(4/\sqrt2)\,k^{\theta/2}\log^2 k$, and $\theta/2 < \theta$.

The first, third and fourth use that $k^{\alpha}\log^{\beta}k = o(k^{\theta}/\log k)$ for $\alpha < \theta$ (from $\log^b x = o(x^c)$, $c > 0$).

## Conclusion {steps: hcard hbS close}

Take $k_0$ to be the larger of the two thresholds. For $k \ge k_0$ and $n$ in the range, $\#B(k,n) < g(k) \le C k^\theta/\log k$. The last lines rewrite the helper's set, which filters the integer interval $(k, k + \lfloor k^\theta\rfloor + 2)$ by the two conditions on $m$, as the statement's set, which also repeats $k < m$; the two filters agree on that interval.
