**Source of the argument.** The statement is, for large $k$, the range $k^3\le n\le\exp(c\log^2k)$ of Konyagin's theorem $g(k)\ge\exp(c\log^2k)$ (Mathematika 46 (1999) 41–55; abstract read, paper not read), where $g(k)$ is the least $n>k+1$ with every prime factor of $\binom nk$ above $k$. The proof merged here was written without his paper. It agrees with the sketch of his proof in O. Trifonov, *Integer points close to a smooth curve*, Serdica Math. J. 24 (1998) 319–338, §4.3 (open access; from Konyagin's 1997 preprint): "for every integer $w\ge2$ and every prime $p\in\bigl(\tfrac kw,\tfrac{k+k^{1-\beta}}w\bigr)$ … there exists an integer $v$ such that $0<\tfrac vw-\tfrac n{wp}<k^{-\beta}$", with $\gg k^{1-\beta}$ such integers $wp$, $w\le k^\gamma$, against a bound for the integers $u$ with $n/u$ within $k^{-\beta}$ of a rational of denominator at most $k^\gamma$. The review Zbl 0972.11010 (O. Ramaré) confirms that the printed paper's Theorem 2 bounds "the number of rational points with small denominators close to a smooth curve" and is what gives the bound for $g(k)$. Differences: here $1-\beta=3/5$ and $\gamma=1/100$ (there $\beta<0.21$, $\gamma=\beta/10$); the primes come from Baker–Harman–Pintz (2001) at length $k^{21/40}$, patched over blocks (there Baker–Harman 1996, exponent $0.535$); the counting theorem is used in the form of `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1--h1`; and $n<k^3$ is handled by a separate argument (`erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1`), where his proof runs from about $k^2/\log k$ and cites Erdős, Lacampagne and Selfridge (1993) below that.
