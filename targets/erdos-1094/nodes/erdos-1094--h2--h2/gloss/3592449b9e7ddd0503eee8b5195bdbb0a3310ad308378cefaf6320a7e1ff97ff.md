There is a natural number K such that, for all natural numbers n and k with k positive and n at least k cubed, if the least prime factor of the binomial coefficient C(n, k) is greater than the floor of n/k, then k is at most K.

In other words: once n is at least k³, so that ⌊n/k⌋ is at least k², the pairs whose binomial coefficient has no prime factor up to ⌊n/k⌋ all have k below one constant. No such pair is known at all. Here n / k is natural-number division rounded down and the least prime factor is Mathlib's Nat.minFac.
