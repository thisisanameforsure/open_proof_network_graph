$$\forall c > 0\ \ \exists K \in \mathbb{N}\ \ \forall n, k \in \mathbb{N}:\quad 0 < k,\ \ k^3 \le n,\ \ \exp(c \log^2 k) < n,\ \ \operatorname{lpf}\tbinom{n}{k} > \lfloor n/k \rfloor,$$
$$\Bigl(\forall p \text{ prime},\ k < p \le \lfloor n/k \rfloor,\ \forall m \in (n-k,\, n],\ p \nmid m\Bigr),\quad \prod_{m=n-k+1}^{n}\ \prod_{p \le k \text{ prime}} p^{v_p(m)} = k!\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ k \le K.$$

Conventions: $\log$ is the natural logarithm and $\exp(c\log^2 k) < n$ is an inequality between real numbers; $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ is Lean's floor division `n / k`; $\operatorname{lpf}$ is `Nat.minFac`, the least prime factor (2 at 0, 1 at 1); $n - k$ is truncated subtraction, which is harmless here since $k \le n$; $v_p(m)$ is `Nat.factorization m p`, the exponent of $p$ in $m$; the inner product runs over the primes $p$ with $1 \le p \le k$, so it is the $k$-smooth part of $m$.

The statement is the parent's question restricted to the case where, in addition, no integer of the window $n-k+1, \ldots, n$ has a prime factor in $(k, \lfloor n/k \rfloor]$, and the $k$-smooth parts of the window multiply to exactly $k!$. The merged partial derives both extra hypotheses from the parent's (the first by the sieve step, the second from the sibling identity), so a proof of this statement proves the parent.
