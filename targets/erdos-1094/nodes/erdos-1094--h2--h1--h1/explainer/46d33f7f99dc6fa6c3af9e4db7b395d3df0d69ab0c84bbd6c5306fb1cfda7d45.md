## The argument

Throughout $\theta = 21/40$, $\|x\|$ is the distance from the real number $x$ to the nearest integer, and for integers $K \ge 1$ and $N$

$$T(K, N) = \{\, m \in \mathbb{Z} : K < m < K + K^\theta,\ \|N/m\| < K^{\theta - 1} \,\}.$$

The theorem's body applies one lemma, `G_final`, to the five holes; everything below is the proof of that lemma, in the helper lemmas that precede the theorem in the file.

**The five inputs.** (BHP, hole `h_bhp`) There are $C > 0$ and $k_0$ such that $(k, k + k^\theta)$ holds at least $C k^\theta/\log k$ primes for every $k \ge k_0$. (Medium, `h_med`) For $k$ large and $\tfrac12 k^{2-\theta} < N \le k^2/\log^2 k$: $\#T(k, N) < 3 + 56k^{2\theta - 1} + k^\theta/\log^2 k$. (Medium-large, `h_ml`) For every $C > 0$, every large $k$ and $k^2/\log^2 k < N \le \tfrac12 k^{2+\theta}$: $\#T(k, N) < C k^\theta/\log k$. (Large, `h_large`) The same bound for $\tfrac12 k^{2+\theta} < N \le \exp(\log^2 k/(20 \log\log k))$. (Small, `h_small`) Either BHP fails, or for $k$ large and $2k \le n \le \tfrac12 (k/2)^{2-\theta} + 2k$ there is a prime $p$ with $k < 2p$ and $(n \bmod p) + p < k$.

**Two multiples give a prime factor** (`G_carry_dvd`). Let $p$ be prime with $k < 2p$ and $(n \bmod p) + p < k$. Then $p < k < 2p$, so $k \bmod p = k - p > n \bmod p$. By Lucas' theorem

$$\binom nk \equiv \binom{n \bmod p}{k \bmod p}\binom{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}{\lfloor k/p\rfloor} \pmod p,$$

and the first factor is $0$ because its lower index exceeds its upper index. Hence $p \mid \binom nk$. (The hypothesis says that the two multiples $n - (n \bmod p)$ and $n - (n \bmod p) - p$ of $p$ both lie among $n-k+1, \dots, n$.)

**The shift** (`G_shift`). Let $K \ge 1$, $2K \le k$, and let $p$ be an integer with $K < p < K + K^\theta$ and $(n \bmod p) + p \ge k$. Put $s = \lfloor K^\theta \rfloor$ and $N = n - k - s$, and write $n = qp + r$ with $r = n \bmod p$. Then

$$N = (q-1)p + e, \qquad e = r + p - k - s .$$

From $r + p \ge k$ we get $e \ge -s$. From $r \le p - 1$ and $k \ge 2K$ we get $e \le 2(p - K) - 1 - s$; the integer $p - K$ is smaller than $K^\theta$, hence at most $s$, so $e \le s$. Therefore $|e| \le s \le K^\theta$, and

$$\|N/p\| \le \left|\frac Np - (q-1)\right| = \frac{|e|}{p} \le \frac{K^\theta}{p} < \frac{K^\theta}{K} = K^{\theta-1}.$$

So $p \in T(K, N)$.

**A prime outside $T$** (`G_exists_prime`, `G_main`). Suppose $\#T(K, N) < C K^\theta/\log K$ and $C K^\theta/\log K \le \#\{p \text{ prime} : K < p < K + K^\theta\}$. Every such prime is one of the integers $m$ that $T(K, N)$ ranges over, so if all of them lay in $T(K, N)$ the set of primes would be a subset of it, against the two cardinalities. Hence some prime $p \in (K, K + K^\theta)$ has $\|N/p\| \ge K^{\theta-1}$. If moreover $2K \le k \le 2K + 1$ and $N = n - k - \lfloor K^\theta\rfloor$, the shift, read in the contrapositive, gives $(n \bmod p) + p < k$; and $p \ge K + 1$ gives $k \le 2K + 1 < 2p$.

**The range of $N$** (`G_big`, `G_exp_bound`, `med_rhs_le`). Let $K = \lfloor k/2 \rfloor \ge 4$, so $2K \le k \le 2K+1$, and suppose $\tfrac12 (k/2)^{2-\theta} + 2k < n < k^3$. Since $\lfloor K^\theta \rfloor \le K^\theta \le K \le k/2$,

$$N = n - k - \lfloor K^\theta \rfloor > \tfrac12 (k/2)^{2-\theta} + k - K \ge \tfrac12 K^{2-\theta},$$

and $N \le n < k^3 \le (2K+2)^3$. For $K$ large, $2K + 2 \le K^2$ and so $(2K+2)^3 \le K^6 = e^{6\log K}$, while $\log y = o(y)$ applied to $y = \log K$ gives $120 \log\log K \le \log K$, that is $6 \log K \le \log^2 K/(20\log\log K)$. So $\tfrac12 K^{2-\theta} < N \le \exp(\log^2 K/(20\log\log K))$, and $N$ lies in the medium, the medium-large or the large range for $K$. In each of them $\#T(K, N) < C K^\theta/\log K$, where $C$ is the constant of BHP: in the medium range because $3 + 56K^{2\theta-1} + K^\theta/\log^2 K \le C K^\theta/\log K$ for $K$ large (`med_rhs_le`), in the other two by the holes applied to this $C$. The previous paragraph then gives a prime $p$ with $k < 2p$ and $(n \bmod p) + p < k$.

**Assembly** (`G_final`). Take $C$ and its threshold from BHP; since BHP holds, `h_small` gives its second alternative. Let $K_0$ be twice the sum of all the thresholds met above, plus the threshold of `h_small`, plus a constant. Let $k > K_0$ and $2k \le n < k^3$. If $n \le \tfrac12 (k/2)^{2-\theta} + 2k$, `h_small` gives a prime $p$ with $k < 2p$ and $(n \bmod p) + p < k$; otherwise the previous paragraph does, with $K = \lfloor k/2 \rfloor$. In both cases $p \mid \binom nk$ and $p < k$, so the least prime factor of $\binom nk$ is at most $p$, hence not larger than $k$. So every pair with $2k \le n < k^3$ and least prime factor of $\binom nk$ above $k$ has $k \le K_0$.
