# This is the open core, restated — not a piece of it

This node is the leaf `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h3--h1--h1` (van Doorn–Tang's bound beyond their range, with $n/\gcd(n,k)$ prime and $k\nmid n$) with its hypothesis $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ rewritten as the two residue facts it is equivalent to:

- at every prime $p\le k$, every base-$p$ digit of $k$ is at most the corresponding digit of $n$ ($k\bmod p^i\le n\bmod p^i$ for all $i$; Kummer: no carry adding $k$ and $n-k$, so $p\nmid\binom nk$);
- at every prime $k<p\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$, $n\bmod p\ge k$ (no multiple of $p$ among $n-k+1,\dots,n$, so $p\nmid\binom nk$).

Together they give back $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$, so nothing has been carved off: the two partials above this node (#1444, #1463) are exact reformulations with their reductions machine-checked, and **a proof of this node is a proof of Erdős 1094 beyond van Doorn–Tang's range** (every sufficient set of the target is Baker–Harman–Pintz plus this chain). Cutting further by restating is not progress; the next partial here should carve something whose proof is real work.

## What a proof has to beat

The digit conditions at primes $\le k$ are jointly satisfiable for every $k$ (by the Chinese remainder theorem), and $(n,k)=(62,6)$ satisfies all of them together with $n\bmod p\ge k$ at the primes $7$ and $\lfloor 62/6\rfloor=10$: no argument that uses only residues modulo primes $\le k$ can finish. The size hypotheses must enter: $k^3\le n$, $\exp(\log^2k/(20\log\log k))<n+1$, and $n<k!+k$ (from $k\lfloor n/k\rfloor\mid k!$).

Under these, the window $W=\{n-k+1,\dots,n\}$ is rigid (annex `60bbe5f8af29…` on `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h3--h1--h1`): each element is $k$-smooth or $s\cdot P$ with $s<k$ and $P>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ prime; the $k$-smooth parts multiply to exactly $k!$; $k\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ is a smooth element and, once $n>\sqrt{k!}+k$, the only one; for each prime $p\in(k/2,k]$ not dividing $k\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ the unique multiple of $p$ in $W$ is $p\cdot P$ with $P$ prime.

## Two directions that are real work

1. **Extend Konyagin's count.** The proved nodes `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1` and below give Konyagin's bound on the number of $m\in(k,k+k^\theta)$ with $n/m$ within $k/m$ of an integer; van Doorn–Tang use it with a parameter $r$ tied to $\log n/\log k$, which is what stops at $\exp(\log^2k/(20\log\log k))$. Choosing $r$ as a function of $\log n$ up to $\log(k!)$ and checking the bound still beats the Baker–Harman–Pintz count of primes in $(k,k+k^{21/40})$ would close this node from what the graph already holds.
2. **The top range by counting smooth parts.** For $n>\sqrt{k!}+k$ the $k-1$ non-smooth elements of $W$ have smooth parts $s<k$ with product $(k-1)!/\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ and with every prime power $p^i\le k$ occurring exactly $\lfloor k/p^i\rfloor$ times across $W$; a counting argument on which multisets $\{s\}$ a window of $k$ consecutive integers can realise might bound $k$ there, leaving the middle range to (1).
