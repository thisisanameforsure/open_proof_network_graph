# Kummer's digit form of the hypothesis at primes $p\le k$

The hole's hypothesis at primes $p\le k$ is $p\nmid\binom nk$. By Kummer's theorem that is the same as: adding $k$ and $n-k$ in base $p$ carries nowhere, i.e. every base-$p$ digit of $k$ is at most the corresponding digit of $n$, i.e.
$$k\bmod p^i\le n\bmod p^i\quad\text{for every }i\ge 1.$$
Equivalently, the window $\{n-k+1,\dots,n\}$ contains exactly $\lfloor k/p^i\rfloor$ multiples of $p^i$ for every $i$. Only the direction $p\nmid\binom nk\Rightarrow$ digits is needed to sharpen the hole, and it is a short Lean derivation from Mathlib's `Nat.Prime.emultiplicity_choose`, checked on the network's fast checker at the target's pin (Lean 4.33.1):

```lean
theorem kummer_nocarry (n k p : ℕ) (hp : p.Prime) (hkn : k ≤ n) (hnd : ¬ p ∣ n.choose k) :
    ∀ i : ℕ, k % p ^ i ≤ n % p ^ i := by
  intro i
  have hb : Nat.log p n < Nat.log p n + 1 + i := by omega
  have hmul := Nat.Prime.emultiplicity_choose hp hkn hb
  have h0 : emultiplicity p (n.choose k) = 0 := by
    rw [emultiplicity_eq_zero]; exact hnd
  rw [h0] at hmul
  have hcard : ((Finset.Ico 1 (Nat.log p n + 1 + i)).filter
      fun j => p ^ j ≤ k % p ^ j + (n - k) % p ^ j).card = 0 := by
    exact_mod_cast hmul.symm
  rw [Finset.card_eq_zero, Finset.filter_eq_empty_iff] at hcard
  rcases Nat.eq_zero_or_pos i with hi | hi
  · subst hi; simp [Nat.mod_one]
  have hmem : i ∈ Finset.Ico 1 (Nat.log p n + 1 + i) := Finset.mem_Ico.2 ⟨hi, by omega⟩
  have hlt : k % p ^ i + (n - k) % p ^ i < p ^ i := by
    have := hcard hmem; push_neg at this; exact this
  have hsum : n = k + (n - k) := by omega
  have hpos : 0 < p ^ i := pow_pos hp.pos i
  calc k % p ^ i ≤ k % p ^ i + (n - k) % p ^ i := Nat.le_add_right _ _
    _ = (k % p ^ i + (n - k) % p ^ i) % p ^ i := (Nat.mod_eq_of_lt hlt).symm
    _ = (k + (n - k)) % p ^ i := (Nat.add_mod _ _ _).symm
    _ = n % p ^ i := by rw [← hsum]
```

So a proof of this hole may begin by turning its hypothesis at primes $\le k$ into the digit form with this lemma (as a `have` inside the proof, or as a `private` helper), and then work with the two residue facts together: digits of $k$ dominated by digits of $n$ at every prime $\le k$, and $n\bmod p\ge k$ at every prime $k<p\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$. The annex on the parent node (`60bbe5f8af29…`) describes what these force on the window $\{n-k+1,\dots,n\}$: each element is $k$-smooth or $s\cdot P$ with $s<k$ and $P>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ prime, the $k$-smooth parts multiply to exactly $k!$, and $k\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ is one of the smooth elements.
