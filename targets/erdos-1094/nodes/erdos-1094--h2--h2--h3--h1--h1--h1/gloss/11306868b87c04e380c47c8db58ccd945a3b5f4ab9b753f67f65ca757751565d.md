The hypothesis at primes $p\le k$ is sharpened from $p\nmid\binom nk$ to Kummer's digit condition, and the leaf is left in that digit form as the one hole.

**What is proved here.** For a prime $p$ and $k\le n$, Kummer's theorem (Mathlib's `Nat.Prime.emultiplicity_choose`) says the exponent of $p$ in $\binom nk$ is the number of $i\ge1$ with $k\bmod p^i+(n-k)\bmod p^i\ge p^i$, the carries when $k$ and $n-k$ are added in base $p$. If $p\nmid\binom nk$ that number is $0$, so for every $i$ the two residues add without overflow, and then $n\bmod p^i = k\bmod p^i+(n-k)\bmod p^i\ge k\bmod p^i$. (For $i=0$ both sides are $0$.) Applied at every prime $p\le k$, which is $\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ because $k^3\le n$, the parent's hypothesis "no prime $p\le k$ divides $\binom nk$" gives the digit condition.
