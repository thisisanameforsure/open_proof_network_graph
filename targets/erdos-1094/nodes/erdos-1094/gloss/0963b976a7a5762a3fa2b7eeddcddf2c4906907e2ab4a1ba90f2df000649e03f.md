The set of pairs (n, k) of natural numbers such that k is positive, n is at least 2k, and the least prime factor of the binomial coefficient C(n, k) is greater than both the floor of n/k and k, is finite.

Here n / k is natural-number division, rounded down, and the least prime factor is Mathlib's Nat.minFac. Because the least prime factor is a whole number, exceeding the floor of n/k is the same as exceeding the real quotient n/k, so the statement says: for n at least 2k, the least prime factor of C(n, k) is at most max(n/k, k), with only finitely many exceptions.
