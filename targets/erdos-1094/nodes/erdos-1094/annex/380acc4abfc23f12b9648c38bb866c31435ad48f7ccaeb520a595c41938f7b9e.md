**The statement.** The root says that only finitely many pairs $(n,k)$ with $0<k$ and $2k\le n$ have the least prime factor of $\binom nk$ greater than $\max(\lfloor n/k\rfloor,k)$. It is not proved, and it is open in the literature.

**What it rests on.** There is now a way of closing the root in which exactly two statements are not proved in Lean.

- *Cited.* `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h1`: there are $C>0$ and $k_0$ such that for every natural $k\ge k_0$ at least $Ck^{21/40}/\log k$ primes $p$ satisfy $k<p<k+k^{21/40}$. This is the final display of Baker, Harman and Pintz, *The difference between consecutive primes, II*, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 83 (2001) 532–562 (there with $C=9/100$ for real $x$). It is published and not formalised; the match between the paper's display and the Lean statement is one contributor's reading (the literature record on that node), not yet confirmed by a steward.
- *Open.* `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h5`: for every real $c>0$ there is $K$ such that every pair $(n,k)$ with $0<k$, $n\ge k^3$, $n>\exp(c\log^2k)$ and least prime factor of $\binom nk$ greater than $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ has $k\le K$. This is open mathematics.

**Route built today, 1: $2k\le n<k^3$** (route 2 on `erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1`). Write $\theta=21/40$ and $K=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. A prime $p$ with $k<2p$ and $(n\bmod p)+p<k$ divides $\binom nk$, by Lucas' theorem: then $p<k<2p$ and $k\bmod p=k-p>n\bmod p$. The primes tried are those in $(K,K+K^\theta)$. If such a prime fails the condition, then with $N=n-k-\lfloor K^\theta\rfloor$ the number $N/p$ lies within $K^{\theta-1}$ of an integer.

The integers $m$ in $(K,K+K^\theta)$ with $N/m$ that close to an integer are fewer than the primes there: fewer than $CK^\theta/\log K$ for every $C>0$ and large $K$, on the three ranges of $N$ from $\tfrac12K^{2-\theta}$ to $\exp(\log^2K/(20\log\log K))$ (`erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h2--h1`, `erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1--h4`, `erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1--h5`), which cover every $n<k^3$ above the small range. So, given the cited bound, some prime in $(K,K+K^\theta)$ divides $\binom nk$. The small range $2k\le n\le\tfrac12(k/2)^{2-\theta}+2k$ is `erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1--h6`, proved in the form "either the cited bound fails, or there is such a prime", by finding a prime above $k/2$ with two multiples among $n-k+1,\dots,n$. The counts are taken from the proof of van Doorn and Tang's theorem (arXiv:2606.19863) already on this record, where they are used with primes just above $k$; the change here is to take primes just above $k/2$.

**Route built today, 2: $k^3\le n\le\exp(c\log^2k)$** (route 2 on `erdos-1094--h2--h2`, and the route on `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4`). Write $H=k^{3/5}$ and $W=k^{1/100}$. Instead of primes, take the products $m=qp$ in $(k,k+H]$ with $2\le q\le W$ and $p$ prime. Then $(q-1)p\le k<qp$ and $p\le k$, and by Lucas' theorem either $p$ divides $\binom nk$ or $|n/m-a/q|<H/k$ for some integer $a$: the number $n/m$ is close to a fraction with denominator at most $W$.

`erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4--h2` says that either the cited bound fails or, for some $c_1>0$ and all large $k$, at least $c_1H$ integers in $(k,k+H]$ are such products. `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4--h3` says that for every $\eta>0$ there is $c>0$ such that, for large $k$ and $k^3\le n\le\exp(c\log^2k)$, fewer than $\eta H$ integers $m$ in $(k,k+H]$ have $n/m$ within $H/k$ of a fraction $a/q$ with $1\le q\le W$. With $\eta=c_1$ some product escapes, and its prime $p\le k$ divides $\binom nk$; that is `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4`. The count is proved from `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1--h1`, the form of Konyagin's Theorem 2 that counts points where a smooth function is near a rational of bounded denominator, applied to $n/(k+x)$. The products have positive density, where the primes have density $1/\log k$, so a saving by a constant factor is enough; that allows about $\log k$ derivatives, and so $n$ up to $\exp(c\log^2k)$.

**What is and is not claimed for route 2.** Konyagin (Mathematika 46 (1999) 41–55) is cited in the literature for $g(k)\ge\exp(c\log^2k)$, where $g(k)$ is the least $n>k+1$ with every prime factor of $\binom nk$ above $k$; once the cited bound is granted, `erdos-1094--h2--h1--h1` and `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4` follow by the proofs on this record, and together they say that for large $k$ no $n$ with $2k\le n\le\exp(c\log^2k)$ has every prime factor of $\binom nk$ above $k$. Nobody who worked on this record has read his paper. The argument above was reconstructed from the statement of his Theorem 2 as van Doorn and Tang give it, and is not claimed to be his proof, nor to be new. The exponents $3/5$ and $1/100$ are choices (the argument needs $21/40<3/5<1$ and a small power for $W$), and the $c$ obtained is small and depends on the constant in the cited bound.

**The open statement.** For each $c$, `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h5` says that only finitely many $k$ admit an $n\ge k^3$ beyond $\exp(c\log^2k)$ with every prime factor of $\binom nk$ above $n/k$: the finitely-many form of Ecklund's conjecture (1969) on that range. It is hard for two reasons. Raising its lower edge by the method of both routes above, which finds a prime at most $k$ dividing $\binom nk$, is the same as improving the lower bound for $g(k)$ (Erdős problem 1095), where $\exp(c\log^2k)$ has been the published record since 1999. And from $g(k)$ upwards that method cannot work at every $n$, since no prime at most $k$ divides $\binom{g(k)}{k}$: there one must show that a given run of $k$ consecutive integers contains a multiple of some prime in $(k,n/k]$, which no known method does for a single run at that height. Annex 473200f4 on `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h3` gives both points with sources, and an elementary bound $n<k2^{k-1}+k$ for any exception with $n\ge k^2$ (an argument in prose, not on the record in Lean); it was written for the older statement of the open part and applies to this one unchanged. The route uses `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h5` only at the one $c$ that `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4` provides.
