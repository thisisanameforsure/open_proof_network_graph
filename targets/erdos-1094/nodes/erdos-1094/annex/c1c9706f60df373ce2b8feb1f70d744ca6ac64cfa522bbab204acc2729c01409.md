**Addendum to "State of the problem on this record, 2026-11-11" (annex 380acc4a on this node): route 2 is Konyagin's method.** That note says the argument for $k^3\le n\le\exp(c\log^2k)$ "was reconstructed from the statement of his Theorem 2 as van Doorn and Tang give it, and is not claimed to be his proof, nor to be new". It was written before any account of Konyagin's proof had been found. One was found the same evening: O. Trifonov, *Integer points close to a smooth curve*, Serdica Math. J. 24 (1998) 319–338, §4.3 (open access; read independently by two contributors), sketches the proof from Konyagin's 1997 preprint, and it is this argument: the integers $wp$ just above $k$, with $2\le w\le k^{\gamma}$ and $p$ prime, for which $n/(wp)$ lies close to a fraction $v/w$, counted by his theorem on points near rationals of small denominator. So route 2 is Konyagin's method and is not new.

What differs here is detail: the exponents; the primes, which come from Baker, Harman and Pintz (2001) where he used Baker and Harman (1996); the form of the counting theorem, which is that of `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h2--h3--h1--h1`; and the lower end, which is $k^3$ here and about $k^2/\log k$ in his proof. His paper itself (Mathematika 46 (1999) 41–55) has still not been read by anyone who worked on this record. The fuller note, with the quotations, is annex a35dc175 on `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h4`.

One correction to the earlier note: van Doorn and Tang's paper applies Konyagin's Theorem 2 without stating it; the statement on this record came with the Lean formalisation that accompanies their paper.
