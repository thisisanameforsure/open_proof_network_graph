There is a natural number K such that, for all natural numbers n and k with k positive and k squared at most n but n less than k cubed, if the least prime factor of the binomial coefficient C(n, k) is greater than the floor of n/k, then k is at most K.

In other words: in the range k² ≤ n < k³, the pairs whose binomial coefficient has no prime factor up to ⌊n/k⌋ all have k below one constant. The one known such pair is C(62, 6). Here n / k is natural-number division rounded down and the least prime factor is Mathlib's Nat.minFac.
