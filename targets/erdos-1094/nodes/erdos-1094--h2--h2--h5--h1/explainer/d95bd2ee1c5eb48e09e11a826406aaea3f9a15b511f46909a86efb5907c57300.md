## Overview

Fix $0 < k \le n$ and write $S$ for the set of primes $p \le k$. The claim is
$$\prod_{m=n-k+1}^{n}\ \prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p(m)} \;=\; k!\cdot \prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p\binom{n}{k}},$$
where $v_p$ is the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorisation (`Nat.factorization`). The proof compares, prime by prime, the exponent of $p$ on the two sides: on the left it is $\sum_{m} v_p(m) = v_p\bigl(n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)\bigr)$, and the falling product is $k!\binom{n}{k}$, so the exponent is $v_p(k!) + v_p\binom{n}{k}$; on the right, the primes $p \le k$ are exactly the primes of $k!$, so $\prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p(k!)} = k!$.

## Reindexing the window

The window $\{n-k+1, \ldots, n\}$ (`Finset.Ioc (n-k) n`) is put in bijection with $\{0, \ldots, k-1\}$ by $m \mapsto n - m$, with inverse $i \mapsto n - i$; both maps land in the right sets and invert each other because $k \le n$, so the double product over $m$ becomes a double product over $i < k$ of the factors of $n - i$. The order of the two products is then swapped (`Finset.prod_comm`), so the outer product runs over the primes $p \in S$.

## The exponent of one prime

For a fixed prime $p \in S$, the product $\prod_{i<k} p^{v_p(n-i)}$ is $p$ to the power $\sum_{i<k} v_p(n-i)$. Since every $n - i$ with $i < k \le n$ is non-zero, the exponent of $p$ in a product is the sum of the exponents (`Nat.factorization_prod`), so the sum equals $v_p\bigl(\prod_{i<k}(n-i)\bigr)$. That product is the descending factorial $n^{\underline{k}}$ (`Nat.descFactorial_eq_prod_range`), which equals $k!\,\binom{n}{k}$ (`Nat.descFactorial_eq_factorial_mul_choose`); as both factors are non-zero, $v_p$ of the product is $v_p(k!) + v_p\binom{n}{k}$ (`Nat.factorization_mul`). Hence the factor for $p$ is $p^{v_p(k!)} \cdot p^{v_p\binom{n}{k}}$.

## Assembling

Multiplying over $p \in S$ and distributing (`Finset.prod_mul_distrib`), the left side is $\bigl(\prod_{p\in S} p^{v_p(k!)}\bigr)\bigl(\prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p\binom{n}{k}}\bigr)$. It remains to see $\prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p(k!)} = k!$. The support of the factorisation of $k!$ is its set of prime factors, and a prime divides $k!$ exactly when it is at most $k$ (`Nat.Prime.dvd_factorial`), so that support lies in $S$; a product over a superset of the support, with the extra factors equal to $p^0 = 1$, is the factorisation's own product (`Finsupp.prod_of_support_subset`), which rebuilds $k!$ (`Nat.prod_factorization_pow_eq_self`).
