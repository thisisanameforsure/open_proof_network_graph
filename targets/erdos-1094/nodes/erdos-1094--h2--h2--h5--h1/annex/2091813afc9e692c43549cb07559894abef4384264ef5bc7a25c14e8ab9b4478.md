## The k-smooth parts of a window of k consecutive integers

Fix $0 < k \le n$ and write $S$ for the set of primes $p \le k$. For an integer $m \ge 1$ let $s_k(m) = \prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p(m)}$ be its $k$-smooth part, where $v_p$ is the exponent of $p$ in $m$. The claim is

$$\prod_{m=n-k+1}^{n} s_k(m) \;=\; k!\cdot s_k\!\left(\binom{n}{k}\right).$$

**Proof.** Both sides are products of powers of primes in $S$, so it suffices to compare the exponent of each $p \in S$. On the left the exponent is $\sum_{m=n-k+1}^{n} v_p(m) = v_p\bigl(n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)\bigr)$, and the falling product is $k!\binom{n}{k}$, so the exponent is $v_p(k!) + v_p\binom{n}{k}$. On the right, $k! = \prod_{p \in S} p^{v_p(k!)}$ because every prime factor of $k!$ is at most $k$, so the exponent of $p$ is again $v_p(k!) + v_p\binom{n}{k}$. $\square$

**How the parent uses it.** Under the parent's hypothesis that the least prime factor of $\binom{n}{k}$ exceeds $\lfloor n/k \rfloor \ge k$, no prime $p \le k$ divides $\binom{n}{k}$, so $s_k\binom{n}{k} = 1$ and the window's smooth parts multiply to exactly $k!$. Combined with the sieve step (no integer of the window has a prime factor in $(k, n/k]$), each $m$ in the window is $s_k(m)\cdot t_m$ with every prime factor of $t_m$ above $n/k$, so $t_m = 1$ or $t_m > n/k$, and then $s_k(m) < k$. The $k$ smooth parts are therefore each below $k$ and multiply to $k!$: this is the arithmetic shape of the open core.
