Under $k^2\le n$ and $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk > \lfloor n/k\rfloor$, the number $n/\gcd(n,k)$ is prime; so the leaf beyond van Doorn–Tang's range reduces to the same statement with that primality added as a hypothesis.

**What is proved here.** Write $g=\gcd(n,k)$. From the identity $n\binom{n-1}{k-1} = k\binom nk$ one gets $(n/g)\binom{n-1}{k-1} = (k/g)\binom nk$, and $n/g$ is coprime to $k/g$, so $n/g$ divides $\binom nk$. Hence every prime factor of $n/g$ is a prime factor of $\binom nk$, and so exceeds $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$. If $n/g$ were composite its least prime factor $q$ would satisfy $q^2\le n/g\le n$, while $q\ge \lfloor n/k\rfloor+1$ gives $q^2\ge(\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1)^2 > (\lfloor n/k\rfloor+1)\,k > n$ (using $k\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$, which is $k^2\le n$). So $n/g$ is prime. The degenerate case $n/g\le 1$ forces $k=1$ and $n=1$, where $\binom 11=1$ has no least prime factor above $1$.

**The hole.** `h_gp`: the parent's hypotheses ($0<k$, $k^3\le n$, $\exp(\log^2k/(20\log\log k))<n+1$, $k\lfloor n/k\rfloor\mid k!$, $\operatorname{minFac}\binom nk>\lfloor n/k\rfloor$) together with $n/\gcd(n,k)$ prime imply $k\le K$. Its node is `erdos-1094--h2--h2--h3--h1`, which the next partial splits by whether $k\mid n$.

**The assembly.** Given the hole's bound $K$, apply it to any $(n,k)$ satisfying the parent's hypotheses, supplying the primality from the lemma (the lemma's $k^2\le n$ follows from $k^3\le n$ and $k\ge1$).
