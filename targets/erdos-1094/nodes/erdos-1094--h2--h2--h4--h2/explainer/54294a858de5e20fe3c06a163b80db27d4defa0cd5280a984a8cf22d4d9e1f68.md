## The proof

Write $\theta=21/40$, $H=k^{3/5}$, $W=k^{1/100}$. If BHP fails the first alternative holds (the Lean's `by_cases`); assume BHP with its constant $C>0$ and threshold $k_b$. We show the second alternative with $c_1=C/3200$. The work is in ten helper lemmas before the theorem, described here in the order of the argument.

### Primes of a BHP interval as natural numbers (`ks_nat`)

BHP counts integers $p>0$ with $|p|$ prime in $(y,\,y+y^\theta)$. Taking absolute values maps that set injectively into
$$T(y)=\{p\in\mathbb{N}:\ p\text{ prime},\ y<p<y+\lceil y^\theta\rceil,\ p<y+y^\theta\},$$
so $\#T(y)\ge C\,y^\theta/\log y$ for natural $y\ge k_b$.

### The set and the blocks (`ks_main`, first half)

Let $Q=\lfloor W\rfloor$ and $H_0=\lfloor H\rfloor$, and let
$$S=\{m\in\mathbb{N}:\ k<m\le k+H_0,\ m=qp\text{ for some }q\ge2\text{ with }q\le W\text{ and some prime }p\}.$$
Every element of $S$ has the required form, so only $\#S$ is to be bounded below. For $2\le q\le Q$ put $x_q=\lfloor k/q\rfloor$, $B_q=\lceil(2x_q)^\theta\rceil$ and $I_q=\lfloor H_0/(qB_q)\rfloor$, and consider the blocks starting at
$$y_{q,i}=x_q+iB_q,\qquad 0\le i<I_q .$$
For $i<I_q$ we have $iB_q\le H_0/q\le x_q$ (`ks_iB`), so $x_q\le y_{q,i}\le 2x_q$ and $\lceil y_{q,i}^\theta\rceil\le B_q$: a prime $p\in T(y_{q,i})$ satisfies $y_{q,i}<p<y_{q,i+1}$ (`ks_q_mem`). For such $p$, $qp\ge q\,(x_q+1)>k$ (as $k<q\,x_q+q$) and $qp\le q\,x_q+q(i+1)B_q\le k+H_0$, so $qp\in S$.

### Different triples give different products (`ks_card`)

Suppose $qp=q'p'$ with $p\in T(y_{q,i})$, $p'\in T(y_{q',i'})$. For large $k$ we have $(Q+1)Q\le k$, hence $x_q>Q$ for every $q\le Q$, so $p>Q\ge q'$ and $p'>Q\ge q$. The prime $p$ divides $q'p'$ and cannot divide $q'<p$, so $p=p'$, then $q=q'$, and since the blocks of one $q$ are disjoint, $i=i'$. Therefore
$$\#S\ \ge\ \sum_{q=2}^{Q}\ \sum_{i<I_q}\#T(y_{q,i}) .$$

### The count for one q (`ks_B_le`, `ks_q_sum`)

For large $k$, $x_q\ge k_b$ and $2x_q\le k$. For $i<I_q$, BHP at $y=y_{q,i}$ gives $\#T(y)\ge C\,y^\theta/\log y\ge C\,x_q^\theta/\log k$, as $x_q\le y\le k$. Next $B_q<(2x_q)^\theta+1\le 4x_q^\theta$, and $H_0<qB_q(I_q+1)$ by the definition of $I_q$, so $I_q\,x_q^\theta>H_0/(4q)-x_q^\theta\ge H_0/(4q)-k^\theta$. Hence
$$\sum_{i<I_q}\#T(y_{q,i})\ \ge\ \frac{C}{\log k}\Bigl(\frac{H_0}{4q}-k^\theta\Bigr).$$

### Summing over q (`KS_harmonic`, `ks_main`, second half)

With $\sum_{q=2}^{Q}1/q\ge\log(Q+1)-1$ (from Mathlib's $\log(Q+1)\le H_Q$ for the harmonic number) and at most $Q$ values of $q$,
$$\#S\ \ge\ \frac{C}{\log k}\Bigl(\frac{H_0}{4}\bigl(\log(Q+1)-1\bigr)-Q\,k^\theta\Bigr).$$

### Large k (`ks_final_ineq`, `ks_supply`)

Take $k$ so large that $W\ge\max(k_b,2)$, $\log k\ge200$, $k^{13/200}\ge16$ and $H\ge2$. Then $(Q+1)Q\le(W+1)W\le W^{100}=k$ and $k_bQ\le W^2\le k$, which are the two size conditions used above. Further $H_0\ge H-1\ge H/2$; $\log(Q+1)\ge\log W=\tfrac1{100}\log k$; and $Q\,k^\theta\le k^{\theta+1/100}=H/k^{13/200}\le H/16$. So the bracket is at least
$$\frac H8\Bigl(\frac{\log k}{100}-1\Bigr)-\frac H{16}\ \ge\ \frac{H\log k}{1600}-\frac{H\log k}{3200}=\frac{H\log k}{3200},$$
using $\log k\ge200$ twice, and $\#S\ge C\,H/3200$.
