$$\forall n, m \in \mathbb{N}:\quad 6 \le n \to 12 \le m \to \tbinom{n}{3} \ne \tbinom{m}{6}$$

Here $n$, $m$ are natural numbers; the bounds $6 \le n$ and $12 \le m$ are the half-triangle conditions $2 \cdot 3 \le n$ and $2 \cdot 6 \le m$ for the fixed columns $k = 3$, $l = 6$. The inequation is the conclusion, not a hypothesis.

In words: no number is both $\binom{n}{3}$ and $\binom{m}{6}$ with $(n, 3)$ and $(m, 6)$ in the half triangle: the collision equation for the column pair $(3, 6)$ has no solutions there.
