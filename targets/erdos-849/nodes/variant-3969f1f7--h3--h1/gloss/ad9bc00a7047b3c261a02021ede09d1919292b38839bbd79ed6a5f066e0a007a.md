$$\exists a_1 \in \mathbb{N}\ \ \forall a \ge a_1\ \ \forall n, k, n', k' \in \mathbb{N}:\quad \Bigl(1 \le k,\ e^{(\log n)^{5/6}} \le k,\ 2k \le n,\ \tbinom{n}{k} = a\Bigr) \to \Bigl(1 \le k',\ e^{(\log n')^{5/6}} \le k',\ 2k' \le n',\ \tbinom{n'}{k'} = a\Bigr) \to |n - n'| + |k - k'| \le e^{(\log \log a)^{9/10}}$$

Here $a_1$, $a$, $n$, $k$, $n'$, $k'$ are natural numbers; $\log$ is the natural logarithm of the number cast to $\mathbb{R}$, the two thresholds $e^{(\log n)^{5/6}} \le k$ and $e^{(\log n')^{5/6}} \le k'$ are compared in $\mathbb{R}$, and the absolute values and the sum are taken in $\mathbb{R}$ after casting. The two solutions are not required to be distinct.

In words: for all sufficiently large $a$, any two solutions $(n, k)$, $(n', k')$ of $\binom{n}{k} = a$ in the interior region $e^{(\log n)^{5/6}} \le k \le n/2$ lie within distance $e^{(\log \log a)^{9/10}}$ of each other, in the sum of the row distance and the column distance. The threshold $5/6$ is $2/3 + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon = 1/6$, and $9/10$ is one admissible exponent $1 - \varepsilon'$ (any $\varepsilon' < \varepsilon/(2/3+\varepsilon) = 1/5$ works, and a smaller one absorbs the implied constant).
