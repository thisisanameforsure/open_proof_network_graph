$$\binom{n}{k} = \binom{m}{l},\ 2 \le k \le n/2,\ 2 \le l \le m/2,\ k < l \Rightarrow (k,l) \in \{(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(2,6),(2,8),(3,4),(3,6),(4,6),(4,8)\} \text{ or } (m,l) = (n-1,\ k+1) \text{ or } n \le 10^6$$

Open: this is exactly what remains of de Weger's conjecture after Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer, de Weger (2017). Also known but unusable as a hole (no witness): there are no collisions of the shapes (m,l) = (n−1,k+2), (n−2,k+1) (Stroeker and de Weger, Acta Arith. 87 (1999) 339–365) and none with C(n,k) ≤ 10^60 beyond the list (BBdW §2.2). Siegel's theorem (Beukers, Shorey, Tijdeman 1999) gives finitely many collisions for each fixed pair (k,l), ineffectively; nothing published is uniform in (k,l).
