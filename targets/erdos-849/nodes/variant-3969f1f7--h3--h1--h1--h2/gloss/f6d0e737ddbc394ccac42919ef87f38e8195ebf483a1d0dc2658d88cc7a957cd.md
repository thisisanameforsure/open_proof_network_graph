$$\exists C\in\mathbb R\ \exists P_0\in\mathbb R\ \forall P\ge P_0\ \forall n,k,n',k'\in\mathbb N:\quad 1\le k'<k,\ n<n',\ 2k\le n,\ 2k'\le n',\ \binom nk=\binom{n'}{k'},\ \text{(i)},\ \text{(ii)}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{(B) with constant }C.$$

Here $L=\log P$, and for a configuration $(n,k,n',k')$ of natural numbers:

- (i) $k,\,k',\,n,\,n' \le e^{L^{299/200}}$;
- (ii) for all integers $x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2$ with $|x_1|,|x_2|,|y_1|,|y_2| \le L^{1/100}$ and every natural $j \ge 1$: $|x_1k+x_2k'+y_1n+y_2n'| \le P^j/L^{1000}$ or $|x_1k+x_2k'+y_1n+y_2n'| \ge P^jL^{1000}$;
- (iii) $k-k' \ge PL^{100}$, or $k \ge PL^{100}$, $k' \ge PL^{100}$ and $n'-n \ge PL^{100}$;
- (B) with $S$ the set of primes $p$ with $\lceil P\rceil \le p \le \lfloor P+P/L^{100}\rfloor$ and, for real $N, M$ and natural $j \ge 1$,
$$c_j(N,M)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{p\in S}\Bigl\{\frac Np\Bigr\}\Bigl\{\frac M{p^j}\Bigr\}-\Bigl(\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{p\in S}\Bigl\{\frac Np\Bigr\}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{p\in S}\Bigl\{\frac M{p^j}\Bigr\}\Bigr),$$
for all $N, M \in \{k,\ n,\ n-k,\ k',\ n',\ n'-k'\}$ and all $j$ with $1 \le j \le L^{1/2}$:
(B1) if $j \ge 2$ then $|c_j(N,M)| \le C/L^{10}$;
(B2) if $j=1$ and $|N| \le P/L^{1000}$ or $|M| \le P/L^{1000}$, then $|c_1(N,M)| \le C/L^{1000}$;
(B3) if $j=1$, $|N|,|M| > P/L^{1000}$, and $a,b$ are coprime natural numbers with $1 \le a,b \le L^{1/100}$ and $|aN-bM| \le P/L^{1000}$, then $|c_1(N,M)-\frac{1}{12ab}| \le C/L^{1/1000}$;
(B4) if $j=1$, $|N|,|M| > P/L^{1000}$, and $|aN-bM| > P/L^{1000}$ for every such pair $a,b$, then $|c_1(N,M)| \le C/L^{1/1000}$.

$\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the fractional part. The averages are Lean's division by $|S|$, which is $0$ when $S$ is empty; $n-k$ and $n'-k'$ are differences of reals, here nonnegative since $2k\le n$ and $2k'\le n'$.

This is Proposition 3.2 of Matomäki, Radziwiłł, Shao, Tao and Teräväinen (Quart. J. Math. 73 (2022)) with $\varepsilon = 1/20$: (i) and (ii) are parts (i) and (ii) of their Lemma 3.1 (their $m, m'$ are $k, k'$), the uniform random prime $\mathbf p$ of $[P, P+P\log^{-100}P]$ is the average over $S$, their $\ll$ is the constant $C$, and their set $\{m,n,m-n,m',n',n'-m'\}$ is written with $n-k$, the complementary index.
