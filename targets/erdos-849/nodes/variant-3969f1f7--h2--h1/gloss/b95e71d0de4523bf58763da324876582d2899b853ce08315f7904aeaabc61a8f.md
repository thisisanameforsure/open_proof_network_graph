$$\binom{n}{k} = \binom{m}{l},\ 2 \le k \le n/2,\ 2 \le l \le m/2,\ k < l \Rightarrow (n,k,m,l) \in \{(78,2,15,5), (78,2,14,6), (16,2,10,3), (21,2,10,4), (56,2,22,3), (120,2,36,3), (153,2,19,5), (221,2,17,8)\}$ or $(n,k,m,l) = (F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3},\ F_{2i}F_{2i+3},\ F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3}-1,\ F_{2i}F_{2i+3}+1)$ for some $i \ge 1$$

Open. B. M. M. de Weger, "Equal binomial coefficients: some elementary considerations", J. Number Theory 63 (1997) 373–386, stated as Conjecture 2.1 of Blokhuis, Brouwer, de Weger, "Binomial collisions and near collisions", Integers 17 (2017) #A64 (arXiv:1707.06893): a collision is (n,k,m,l) with 2 ≤ k ≤ n/2, 2 ≤ l ≤ m/2, k < l and C(n,k) = C(m,l); the known ones are the six sporadic values 120, 210, 1540, 7140, 11628, 24310, the double collision 3003 = C(78,2) = C(15,5) = C(14,6), and Lind's family C(F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3}, F_{2i}F_{2i+3}) = C(F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3} - 1, F_{2i}F_{2i+3} + 1), i ≥ 1 (which holds (15,5,14,6)). Verified by Theorem 2.2 there for n ≤ 10^6 and C(n,k) ≤ 10^60. MRSTT, Quart. J. Math. 73 (2022), Remark 1.5: it implies at most one solution of C(n,m) = t with 2 ≤ m ≤ exp(log^{2/3+ε} n) for t large; the assembly below needs only the bounded form.
