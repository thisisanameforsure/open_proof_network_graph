## The rows are the images of the solutions {steps: himg}

Fix $a$. Write $P = \{(n,k) : 1 \le k,\ 2k \le n,\ \binom{n}{k} = a\}$ for the solutions and $R = \{n : \exists k \ge 1,\ 2k \le n,\ \binom{n}{k} = a\}$ for the rows. The first projection $(n,k) \mapsto n$ maps $P$ onto $R$: a solution $(n,k)$ gives the row $n$, and a row $n$ with its witness $k$ is the image of $(n,k)$. So $R = \pi_1(P)$, and it remains to show that $\pi_1$ is injective on $P$, since an injective map preserves the number of elements (`Set.ncard_image_of_injOn`).

## Injectivity: a row holds $a$ at most once in range {steps: this close}

Let $(n, k_1)$ and $(n, k_2)$ be two solutions in the same row. Then $\binom{n}{k_1} = a = \binom{n}{k_2}$ with $2k_1 \le n$ and $2k_2 \le n$, and the lemma below gives $k_1 = k_2$.

The lemma (helpers `step_lt`, `mono_lt`, `row_unique`): $k \mapsto \binom{n}{k}$ is strictly increasing on $0 \le k \le n/2$. For one step, if $2(k+1) \le n$ then
$$\binom{n}{k+1}(k+1) = \binom{n}{k}(n-k)$$
and $n - k > k + 1$, so $\binom{n}{k+1} > \binom{n}{k}$, using $\binom{n}{k} > 0$. Chaining the steps by induction on $j$ gives $\binom{n}{i} < \binom{n}{j}$ whenever $i < j$ and $2j \le n$. If $k_1 \ne k_2$, the smaller one gives a strictly smaller value, contradicting $\binom{n}{k_1} = \binom{n}{k_2}$.

So $|R| = |P|$ for every $a$, which is the statement: the root's count of rows equals the problem's count of solutions.
