## Overview

The node says that $\binom{n}{2} = \binom{m}{6}$ with $n \ge 4$, $m \ge 12$ forces $(n, m) = (78, 14)$. The assembly maps every such pair to an integral point of the elliptic curve $E : y^2 + y = x^3 + x^2 - 58x + 1294$ (LMFDB 14475.a1, its minimal model) by $x = \tfrac{5}{2}m(m-5) + 8$, $y = 75n - 38$, and reads the solution off the curve's integral points, which are the one hole.

## The hole: the integral points of 14475.a1 {steps: h_14475a1}

The hole states that every $(x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ on $E$ is one of the twenty-four integral points the LMFDB lists for 14475.a1: $x \in \{-13, -7, -2, 2, 8, 14, 23, 68, 133, 233, 323, 2234\}$, each with its two $y$ values ($y$ and $-1-y$).

## Clearing the binomials {steps: s2 s3 hA hB e0 e1 f0 f1 f2 f3 f4 f5 hA' hB'}

Write $n = 4 + a$, $m = 12 + b$. The identities $n(n-1) = 2\binom{n}{2}$ and $m(m-1)\cdots(m-5) = 720\binom{m}{6}$ are instantiated, the truncated subtractions are rewritten as exact ones, and both are cast to the integers.

## The point on the curve {steps: s16}

With $w = m(m-5)$ one has $(m-1)(m-4) = w + 4$ and $(m-2)(m-3) = w + 6$, so $720\binom{m}{6} = w(w+4)(w+6)$. Split on the parity of $b$ so that $w/2$ is a polynomial: $w/2 = (6+c)(7+2c)$ for $b = 2c$, and $(13+2c)(4+c)$ for $b = 2c+1$. With $x = 5\cdot w/2 + 8$ and $y = 75n - 38$,
$$8\bigl(y^2 + y - (x^3 + x^2 - 58x + 1294)\bigr) = 125\bigl(360\,n(n-1) - w(w+4)(w+6)\bigr) = 125\bigl(720\tbinom{n}{2} - 720\tbinom{m}{6}\bigr) = 0,$$
checked by `linear_combination`. The hole then gives the twenty-four cases.

## The two parity cases {steps: s17 s18}

In each parity case (even $b$, then odd $b$) the identity above is proved and the hole applied; then the case analysis below closes it.

Here $y = 75n - 38 \ge 262$, and $y \equiv 37 \pmod{75}$. Every case but two fails one of these linear conditions (`omega`). The two left are $(233, 3562)$, i.e. $n = 48$, where $w/2 = 45$ has no solution with $c \ge 0$, and $(323, 5812)$, i.e. $n = 78$, where $w/2 = 63$ forces $c = 1$, so $m = 14$ in the even case and is impossible in the odd one; both are settled by bounding $c \le 1$ and checking the two values.
