$$\binom{n}{k} = \binom{m}{l},\ 2 \le k \le n/2,\ 2 \le l \le m/2,\ k < l \Rightarrow (k,l) \in \{(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(2,6),(2,8),(3,4),(3,6),(4,6),(4,8)\} \text{ or } (m,l) = (n-1,\ k+1) \text{ or } n \le 10^6 \text{ or } \binom{n}{k} \le 10^{60}$$

Open: de Weger's conjecture (BBdW Conjecture 2.1) with every proved case of Theorem 2.2 removed from its scope. The remaining proved facts are pure non-existence statements and cannot be holes: no collisions of the shapes (m,l) = (n−1,k+2), (n−2,k+1) (Stroeker and de Weger, Acta Arith. 87 (1999) 339–365). Siegel's theorem (Beukers, Shorey, Tijdeman 1999) gives finitely many collisions per fixed pair (k,l), ineffectively; nothing published is uniform in (k,l).
