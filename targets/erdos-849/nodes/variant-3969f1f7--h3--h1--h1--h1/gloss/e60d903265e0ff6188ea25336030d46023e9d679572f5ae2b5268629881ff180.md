$$\forall P_2\,\exists a_0\,\forall a\ge a_0:\ \binom{n}{k}=\binom{n'}{k'}=a,\ 1\le k'<k,\ n<n',\ 2k\le n,\ 2k'\le n',\ \bigl(k-k'\ge T\ \text{or}\ k,k',n'-n\ge T\bigr),\ T=e^{(\log n')^{43/60}}\ \Rightarrow\ \exists P\ge P_2,\ P>1:\ \text{(i) }k,k',n,n'\le e^{(\log P)^{299/200}};\ \text{(ii) }\forall x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2\in\mathbb{Z},\ |x_i|,|y_i|\le(\log P)^{1/100},\ \forall j\ge1:\ |x_1k+x_2k'+y_1n+y_2n'|\le\frac{P^j}{(\log P)^{1000}}\ \text{or}\ \ge P^j(\log P)^{1000};\ \text{(iii) }k-k'\ge P(\log P)^{100}\ \text{or}\ k,k',n'-n\ge P(\log P)^{100}$$

Matomäki, Radziwiłł, Shao, Tao, Teräväinen, Quart. J. Math. 73 (2022), Lemma 3.1, with ε = 1/20 (so 2/3 + ε = 43/60 and 3/2 − ε/10 = 299/200); the paper's "P sufficiently large" is P ≥ P₂. Its proof chooses P a power of two in [exp(log^(83/120) n'), exp(2 log^(83/120) n')] and counts the exceptions to (ii).
