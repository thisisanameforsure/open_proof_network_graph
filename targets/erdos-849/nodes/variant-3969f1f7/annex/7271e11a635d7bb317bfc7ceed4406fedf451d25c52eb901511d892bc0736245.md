Informal decomposition of Singmaster's conjecture in the row form of Erdős 849 (∃ B, every a lies in at most B rows n of the half triangle 1 ≤ k ≤ n/2).

Fix a. Split the rows n holding a by the k with C(n,k) = a (in the half triangle each row holds a at most once, so each row has one such k):

1. k = 1: only the row n = a.
2. 2 ≤ k ≤ K (step h_fixed): for each fixed k, C(·,k) is strictly increasing on n ≥ 2k, so at most one row per k, hence at most K − 1 ≤ K rows. Elementary.
3. exp((log n)^{5/6}) ≤ k ≤ n/2, the interior (step h_interior): by Matomäki, Radziwiłł, Shao, Tao and Teräväinen, Singmaster's conjecture in the interior of Pascal's triangle, Quart. J. Math. 73 (2022) 1137–1177, arXiv:2106.03335, Theorem 1.3 with ε = 1/6, for a ≥ a₀ there are at most two solutions (n,k) in this region, hence at most two rows. Known, not formalised.
4. K < k < exp((log n)^{5/6}), the middle (step h_middle): OPEN. This is the whole difficulty; Singmaster's conjecture implies it, and under de Weger's conjecture (Blokhuis–Brouwer–de Weger, Integers 17 (2017) #A64, Conjecture 2.1) Matomäki et al. (Remark 1.5) give at most one solution with 2 ≤ k ≤ exp((log n)^{2/3+ε}) for large a. Exact search finds at most 3 rows with k ≥ 2 for every a ≤ 10^37.

Assembly: for a ≥ a₀, rows(a) ≤ 1 + K + B + 2; for a < a₀, every row holding a lies in [2, a], so rows(a) ≤ a₀. Take the maximum.
