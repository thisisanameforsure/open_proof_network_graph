$$\binom{n}{k} = \binom{m}{l} \le 10^{60},\ 2 \le k \le n/2,\ 2 \le l \le m/2,\ k < l \Rightarrow (n,k,m,l) \in \{(78,2,15,5), (78,2,14,6), (16,2,10,3), (21,2,10,4), (56,2,22,3), (120,2,36,3), (153,2,19,5), (221,2,17,8)\} \text{ or } (n,k,m,l) = (F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3},\ F_{2i}F_{2i+3},\ F_{2i+2}F_{2i+3}-1,\ F_{2i}F_{2i+3}+1),\ i \ge 1$$

Known by computation: Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer, de Weger, Integers 17 (2017) #A64, case C(n,k) ≤ 10^60, by the sieve of §2.2 there (all primes below 500, about 375 CPU hours). Earlier: C(n,k) ≤ 10^30, de Weger, J. Number Theory 63 (1997) 373–386. Not formalised.
