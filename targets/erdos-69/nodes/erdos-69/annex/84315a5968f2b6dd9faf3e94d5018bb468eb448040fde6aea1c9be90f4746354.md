Informal argument for Erdős problem 69 (Erdős 1948), as decomposed in the accompanying skeleton.

Claim. S = ∑_{n ≥ 2} ω(n)/2^n is irrational, where ω(n) is the number of distinct prime divisors of n.

Lemma 1 (Lambert-series identity; Tao's remark under Erdős 257). ∑_{n ≥ 1} ω(n)/2^n = ∑_{p prime} 1/(2^p − 1).
Proof sketch. ω(n) = ∑_{p | n} 1, so ∑_n ω(n)/2^n = ∑_n ∑_{p | n} 2^{−n}. All terms are non-negative and the row sums are bounded by ∑ n/2^n < ∞, so the double sum may be rearranged: summing first over the prime p and then over the multiples n = pk (k ≥ 1) gives ∑_p ∑_{k ≥ 1} 2^{−pk} = ∑_p 2^{−p}/(1 − 2^{−p}) = ∑_p 1/(2^p − 1). The terms n = 0 and n = 1 contribute nothing since ω(0) = ω(1) = 0, so the sum starting at n = 2 equals the sum over all n.

Lemma 2 (Erdős 1948). ∑_{p prime} 1/(2^p − 1) is irrational.
Proof sketch, in the form the statement's sum suggests. Suppose the sum equals a/b with a, b positive integers. By Lemma 1 the sum is ∑_n ω(n)/2^n, so for every N the number b · 2^N · ∑_{n > N} ω(n)/2^n = b · ∑_{k ≥ 1} ω(N + k)/2^k is an integer. Erdős chooses N (by the Chinese remainder theorem, with N divisible by the primes below a chosen bound and with N + 1, …, N + K carrying prescribed prime divisors) so that the values ω(N + k) for small k are controlled closely enough that b · ∑_{k ≥ 1} ω(N + k)/2^k lies strictly between two consecutive integers, a contradiction. The count that drives it is of integers of the form pk in a window of residues, which is where the prime sum ∑_p ∑_k 2^{−pk} enters.

Assembly. Lemma 1 rewrites the statement's sum as the prime sum; Lemma 2 says that sum is irrational.

References. P. Erdős, On arithmetical properties of Lambert series, J. Indian Math. Soc. 12 (1948), 63–66. erdosproblems.com/69 and the registry file FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/69.lean (the variant specialisation_of_erdos_257 states Lemma 1 in the shifted form).
