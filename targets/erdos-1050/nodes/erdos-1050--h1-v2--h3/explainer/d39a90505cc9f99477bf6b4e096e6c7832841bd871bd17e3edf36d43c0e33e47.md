## Idea and method

The target statement fixes three explicit rational families. The first is
$$Q_c(n,k)=(-1)^k\,2^{k(k-1)/2}\prod_{i<k}\frac{2^{n-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}\;\prod_{i<n}\frac{2^{2n-k-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}.$$
The second is $Q_x(n)=\sum_{k\le n}Q_c(n,k)\,(3/2^n)^k$. The third is
$$A_q(n)=\sum_{k\le n}\Big(\sum_{j\le k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{k-j}-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{3}{2^n}\Big)^k+Q_x(n)\sum_{j<n}\frac{3}{2^{j+1}-3}.$$
The claim is that for every $n$ there is a positive integer $d$ with $d^2\le 2^{3n^2}$ such that both $d\,Q_x(n)$ and $d\,A_q(n)$ are integers. Exponents are natural numbers, so subtraction in them is truncated. Division by $0$ is $0$, as in Lean; in particular the $j=k$ term of the inner sum is $Q_c(n,k)/0=0$.

The proof has four parts.

1. **Integrality of the coefficients.** Each $Q_c(n,j)$ is a power of $2$ times a product of two Gaussian binomials at $q=2$, and these products are integers.
2. **Rewriting the inner sums.** This uses polynomial interpolation. The polynomial $N=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(X-2^K)$ is written in Lagrange form at the nodes $2^0,\dots,2^n$, and its derivative is evaluated at a node in two ways. This rewrites $\sum_{j\le k}Q_c(n,j)/(2^{k-j}-1)$ through sums of $1/(2^k-2^K)$ and a tail over $j>k$.
3. **Clearing denominators.** The odd denominators are cleared by the odd number $\Omega=\prod_{t=1}^{2n}(2^t-1)$. Separately, $\prod_{n/2<m\le n}(2^m-1)$ clears every $2^{k-j}-1$, because each $s\le n$ divides some $m\in(\lfloor n/2\rfloor,n]$. A coprimality argument then merges the two pieces of information. The extra sum $\sum_j 3/(2^{j+1}-3)$ is cleared by $\prod_{2\le j<n}(2^{j+1}-3)$.
4. **The witness and its size.** The witness $d$ is a power of $2$ times these products. Two exceptional values replace the products by explicit numerals. Its size is controlled by bounding each factor by a power of $2$ and doing exponent arithmetic.

Throughout, write $P_m=\prod_{s<m}(2^{s+1}-1)$ and $\binom{n}{k}_2=\prod_{i<k}\frac{2^{n-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}$.

## Telescoping products of $2^a-1$ {steps: id1_test id1_test.s1 id1_test.s2 id1_test.s2.ih' id1_test.s2.s2 lower_test lower_test.h1 lower_test.h1.hi' lower_test.this}

`id1_test` states that for $j\le n$
$$\prod_{i<j}(2^{n-i}-1)\cdot P_{n-j}=P_n .$$
It is proved by induction on $j$.

- **Base case $j=0$.** The goal is the statement with the empty first product. This is routine (closed by simp).
- **Successor case.** The goal is the identity for $j+1$, given the induction hypothesis under $j\le n$ and the assumption $j+1\le n$.
  - `ih'` discharges the hypothesis $j\le n$, giving the identity at $j$.
  - Then $n-j=m+1$ is obtained.
  - The goal becomes the identity with the last factor $2^{n-j}-1$ split off the first product (peeling off the top index of a range product). Comparing with `ih'` closes it.

`lower_test` states that
$$\prod_{i<j}(2^j-2^i)=2^{j(j-1)/2}P_j .$$
Its children establish two facts:

- `h1`: for $i<j$ (the fact `hi'`), $2^j-2^i=2^i\,(2^{\,j-1-i+1}-1)$.
- `this`: $2\sum_{i<j}i=j(j-1)$, by Gauss' summation formula. Hence $\sum_{i<j}i=j(j-1)/2$.

The step then splits the product into $\prod 2^i=2^{\sum i}$ and $\prod_{i<j}(2^{j-i}-1)$. It reflects the index of the latter to obtain $P_j$.

## Products over the upper intervals {steps: upper_test upper_test.e upper_test.h1 rhs_test rhs_test.e rhs_test.h1}

`upper_test` states that for $j\le n$
$$\prod_{j<i\le n}(2^j-2^i)=(-1)^{n-j}\,2^{j(n-j)}\,P_{n-j}.$$
The proof has three moves:

- `e` rewrites $(j,n]$ as $[j+1,n+1)$.
- After re-indexing to $i=j+1+t$ with $t<n-j$, `h1` gives $2^j-2^{j+1+t}=-2^j(2^{t+1}-1)$ for each factor.
- The constant factors are collected as powers, using the cardinality $n-j$.

`rhs_test` states that for $j\le n$
$$\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K)\cdot P_{n-j}=(-1)^n\,2^{jn}\,P_{2n-j}.$$
It follows the same pattern:

- `e` converts the interval.
- `h1` gives $2^j-2^{n+1+t}=-2^j(2^{n-j+t+1}-1)$ for $t<n$.
- The resulting product $\prod_{t<n}(2^{n-j+t+1}-1)$ is concatenated with $P_{n-j}$ to give $P_{2n-j}$.

## The interpolation weights of $Q_c$ {steps: id1_full id1_full.hPne id1_full.hPne.this id1_full.hG1 id1_full.hG2 id1_full.hsplit id1_full.hdisj id1_full.hR id1_full.s7 id1_full.hsign id1_full.ha id1_full.hexp id1_full.hexp.s1 id1_full.hexp.s2}

`id1_full` states that for $j\le n$, with $Q_c(n,j)$ written out,
$$Q_c(n,j)\,2^j\prod_{i\le n,\ i\ne j}(2^j-2^i)=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K).$$

The ingredients are as follows.

- **Nonvanishing.** `hPne` shows $P_m\ne0$ for all $m$, since every factor satisfies $1<2^{s+1}$ (the fact `this`).
- **Quotient forms.** `hG1` writes $\binom{n}{j}_2=P_n/(P_{n-j}P_j)$. `hG2` writes $\prod_{i<n}\frac{2^{2n-j-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}=P_{2n-j}/(P_{n-j}P_n)$.
- **Splitting the index set.** `hsplit` writes $\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j\}=\{0,\dots,j-1\}\cup(j,n]$. `hdisj` records that this union is disjoint.
- **The right-hand side.** `hR` is the instance of the upper-interval identity for $\prod_{n<K\le 2n}$.
- **Substitution.** `s7` writes $n=j+m$, and every hypothesis is restated with $j+m$ in place of $n$.
- **Signs.** `hsign` gives $(-1)^{j+m}=(-1)^j(-1)^{j+m-j}$.
- **Halving.** `ha` gives $2\cdot\big(j(j-1)/2\big)=j(j-1)$, using that $j(j-1)$ is even.
- **Exponent bookkeeping.** `hexp` gives $j(j+m)=j(j-1)/2+j+j(j-1)/2+j\,m$ by induction on $j$. Both of its cases are routine: the zero case by simp, and the successor case by simp and nlinarith.

The recorded closing tools are three: splitting a product over a disjoint union, cancelling a nonzero right factor, and the law of exponents. With them, the left side becomes $P_n/(P_{n-j}P_j)\cdot P_{2n-j}/(P_{n-j}P_n)$ times the two lower and upper products. These were evaluated in the preceding sections, and the result is matched against `hR`.

## Lagrange interpolation by values at the nodes {steps: lagr_eval lagr_eval.hinj lagr_eval.s2 lagr_eval.s2.s1 lagr_eval.s2.s2 lagr_eval.s2.s2.this lagr_eval.s3 lagr_eval.s3.s1 lagr_eval.s3.hk' lagr_eval.s3.hk'.this lagr_eval.s3.s3 lagr_eval.s3.s4 lagr_eval.s3.s5}

`lagr_eval` has three hypotheses:

- $x_0,\dots,x_n$ are pairwise distinct;
- $N\in\mathbb{Q}[X]$ has $\deg N\le n$;
- the weights satisfy $r_j\prod_{i\ne j}(x_j-x_i)=N(x_j)$ for all $j\le n$.

Its conclusion is
$$N=\sum_{j\le n} r_j\prod_{i\le n,\ i\ne j}(X-x_i).$$

The method is that two polynomials which agree on a finite set of points, and whose difference has degree below the number of points, are equal.

**Distinct points.** `hinj` turns distinctness into injectivity of $x$ on $\{0,\dots,n\}$. Hence the image has $n+1$ points.

**Degree bound (`s2`).** The goal is that the difference has degree $<n+1$. It is bounded by the larger of the two degrees.

- `s2.s1` gives $\deg N<n+1$.
- `s2.s2` gives that the Lagrange sum has degree $<n+1$. It bounds the degree of each summand by $0$ plus the sum of the degrees of the linear factors. That sum equals $n$ by the fact `this`.

**Agreement at the nodes (`s3`).** The goal is that $N$ and the sum agree at every point of the image.

- `s3.s1` writes the point as $x_k$ with $k\le n$; this bound is the content of `hk'` and its child.
- The sum is evaluated term by term and reduced to the single term $j=k$.
- `s3.s3` is routine (simp): it evaluates the $k$-th term to $r_k\prod_{i\ne k}(x_k-x_i)$.
- `s3.s4` shows that every other term vanishes, because its product contains the factor $x_k-x_k$.
- `s3.s5` dismisses the case $k\notin\{0,\dots,n\}$ by contradiction.

## Derivative of the interpolant at a node {steps: deriv_prod_eval deriv_prod_eval_node deriv_prod_eval_node.s1 deriv_prod_eval_node.s2 lagr_deriv lagr_deriv.hkmem lagr_deriv.s2 lagr_deriv.s2.hjk lagr_deriv.s3}

`deriv_prod_eval` states that for a finite set $S$ and any $z$,
$$\Big(\prod_{i\in S}(X-x_i)\Big)'(z)=\sum_{l\in S}\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{l\}}(z-x_i).$$
It uses the product rule for finite products and the fact that polynomial evaluation commutes with `Finset.prod` and with finite sums.

`deriv_prod_eval_node` specialises this to $z=x_k$ with $k\in S$. The value is $\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{k\}}(x_k-x_i)$.

- By `s1`, every summand with $l\ne k$ contains the factor $x_k-x_k$, so it vanishes.
- By `s2`, the case $k\notin S$ is contradictory.

`lagr_deriv` assumes the hypotheses of the interpolation step and fixes $k\le n$. It concludes
$$N'(x_k)=\sum_{j\ne k} r_j\prod_{i\ne j,k}(x_k-x_i)\;+\;r_k\sum_{l\ne k}\ \prod_{i\ne k,l}(x_k-x_i).$$
The proof differentiates the Lagrange form term by term and separates the term $j=k$ from the sum over the others (the `Finset.add_sum_erase` lemma).

- `hkmem` records that $k$ lies in the index set.
- By `s2`, for $j\ne k$ (the fact `hjk`), the derivative of $r_j\prod_{i\ne j}(X-x_i)$ evaluated at $x_k$ is $r_j\prod_{i\ne j,k}(x_k-x_i)$.
- By `s3`, the term $j=k$ gives $r_k$ times the derivative formula above.

## The partial-fraction identity: setting up {steps: formula3 formula3.hxinj formula3.hxinj.h' formula3.hxne formula3.hNdeg formula3.hkmem formula3.hr formula3.hD formula3.hD2 formula3.hPne formula3.hNk formula3.hNk'}

`formula3` fixes $k\le n$ and any $Q_c$ satisfying, for all $j\le n$, the weight identity $Q_c(n,j)2^j\prod_{i\ne j}(2^j-2^i)=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K)$. It concludes
$$\sum_{j\le k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{k-j}-1}=Q_c(n,k)\,2^k\Big(\sum_{n<K\le 2n}\frac{1}{2^k-2^K}-\sum_{i\le n,\,i\ne k}\frac{1}{2^k-2^i}\Big)-\sum_{k<j\le n}\frac{Q_c(n,j)\,2^{j-k}}{1-2^{j-k}}.$$

The steps work with the following abbreviations:

- nodes $x_j$, behaving as $2^j$;
- $T=(n,2n]$ and $S=\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k\}$;
- a polynomial $N$, whose value at $x_k$ is $\prod_{K\in T}(x_k-2^K)$;
- a number $P$, which by `hr` and `hNk` plays the role of $\prod_{i\in S}(x_k-x_i)$.

The setup steps are these.

- **Distinct nodes.** `hxinj` reduces $x_i=x_j$ to $2^i=2^j$ (the fact `h'`), and then to $i=j$ by injectivity of powers of $2$. `hxne` gives $2^a-2^b\ne0$ for $a\ne b$.
- **Degree and weights.** `hNdeg` gives $\deg N\le n$: the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees, each factor $X-C$ has degree $1$, and $T$ has $n$ elements. `hr` turns the hypothesis into the weight condition $r_j=Q_c(n,j)2^j$ for $N$. `hkmem` records $k\in\{0,\dots,n\}$.
- **First evaluation of $N'(x_k)$.** `hD` is the derivative formula of the previous section with these weights.
- **Second evaluation of $N'(x_k)$.** `hD2` gives $N'(x_k)=\sum_{K\in T}\prod_{a\in T\setminus\{K\}}(x_k-2^a)$, from the product form of $N$.
- **Values of $N$ and $P$.** `hPne` gives $P\ne0$. `hNk` gives $N(x_k)=Q_c(n,k)2^kP$. `hNk'` gives $N(x_k)=\prod_{K\in T}(x_k-2^K)$.

## The partial-fraction identity: dividing out $P$ {steps: formula3.ha formula3.ha.hne formula3.hb formula3.hb.hne formula3.hc formula3.hc.hKn formula3.hc.hne formula3.hD3 formula3.hD4 formula3.key formula3.key.e1 formula3.key.e2 formula3.key.e3}

|---|---|---|
| `ha` | for $j\in S$: $\prod_{i\ne j,k}(x_k-x_i)=P/(x_k-x_j)$ | `hne`: $x_k-x_j\ne0$ |
| `hb` | for $l\in S$: $\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{l\}}(x_k-x_i)=P/(x_k-x_l)$ | `hne` |
| `hc` | for $K\in T$: $\prod_{a\in T\setminus\{K\}}(x_k-2^a)=N(x_k)/(x_k-2^K)$ | `hKn` ($n<K$) and `hne` |

It is proved by cancelling the nonzero factor $P$ on the left. The steps `e1`, `e2` and `e3` pull $P$ out of the three sums involved by distributing it across them.

  It uses $2^{k-j}-1\ne0$, from $1<2^{k-j}$ (the steps `hne` and `this`), and $2^k-2^j\ne0$ (the step `hne2`).

## Gaussian binomials at $q=2$ are integers {steps: gauss gauss.hD gauss.hD.h1 gauss.hDk gauss.key gauss.key.hshift gauss.key.hDj gauss.key.hj gauss.key.s4 gauss.key.s4.e gauss.key.s5 gauss.key.s5.hz gauss.main gauss.main.s1 gauss.main.s1.s1 gauss.main.s1.s2 gauss.main.s2 gauss.main.s2.s1 gauss.main.s2.s2 gauss.main.s2.s2.s1 gauss.main.s2.s2.s2}

`gauss` states that for all $n,k$ there is an integer equal to $\binom{n}{k}_2=\prod_{i<k}\frac{2^{n-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}$.

**Nonvanishing denominators.** `hD` gives $2^{j+1}-1\ne0$, since $1<2^{j+1}$ (the step `h1`). `hDk` gives $P_k\ne0$.

**The $q$-Pascal recursion (`key`).**
$$\binom{m+1}{j+1}_2=\binom{m}{j}_2+2^{j+1}\binom{m}{j+1}_2 .$$
Both sides are written as a single product quotient.

- `hshift` gives $m+1-(i+1)=m-i$.
- `hDj` and `hj` give $P_j\ne0$ and $2^{j+1}-1\ne0$.
- The resulting rational identity is settled in two cases:
  - in `s4`, with $2^{m+1}=2^{m-j}2^{j+1}$ (the step `e`);
  - in `s5`, where the numerator product $\prod_{i<j}(2^{m-i}-1)$ is $0$ (the step `hz`).

**Integrality (`main`).** This is the statement for all $m,j$, by induction on $m$ and then on $j$.

- **$m=0$** (`s1`).
  - For $j=0$ the product is empty, and an integer witness is given (`s1.s1`).
  - For $j+1$ the product is $0$, since it contains the factor $2^0-1$ (`s1.s2`).
- **$m+1$** (`s2`), with the induction hypothesis for all $j$.
  - For $j=0$ the product is empty (`s2.s1`).
  - For $j+1$, let $a$ and $b$ be the integers given by the hypothesis for $\binom{m}{j}_2$ and $\binom{m}{j+1}_2$. The recursion then shows that $a+2^{j+1}b$ works.

## Clearing the denominators $2^{k-j}-1$ {steps: hodd hodd.hint hodd.hint.hjk hodd.hint.s2 hodd.hint.s3 hodd.hint.s3.s1 hodd.hint.s3.hdn hodd.hint.s3.ht hodd.hint.s3.hP1 hodd.hint.s3.hP2 hodd.hint.s3.hP2.this hodd.hint.s3.hP3 hodd.hint.s3.hmem hodd.hint.s3.hdvd hodd.hint.s3.hdvd.this hodd.hint.s3.s9 hodd.hint.s3.hne hodd.hint.s3.hne.this hodd.hint.s3.hcast}

`hodd` states that for $k\le n$ and any integers $c_j$,
$$\prod_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<m\le n}(2^m-1)\cdot\sum_{j\le k}\frac{c_j}{2^{k-j}-1}$$
is an integer. Distributing the product over the sum reduces this to the termwise claim `hint`: each term, multiplied by the product, is an integer.

Fix $j$ in the sum, so $j\le k$ (the fact `hjk`). There are two cases.

- **Case `s2`.** An integer witness is supplied directly.
- **Case `s3`.** Write $k-j=d+1$ (step `s1`).
  1. `hdn` is routine (omega): it gives $d+1\le n$.
  2. Let $t=(d+1)\lfloor n/(d+1)\rfloor$. Then $\lfloor n/(d+1)\rfloor\ge1$ (`ht`), $t\le n$ (`hP1`), $n<t+(d+1)$ (`hP2` with its child) and $d+1\le t$ (`hP3`).
  3. Together these give $t\in(\lfloor n/2\rfloor,n]$ (`hmem`).
  4. `hdvd` gives that $2^{d+1}-1$ divides the product. It divides $(2^{d+1})^{t/(d+1)}-1^{t/(d+1)}=2^t-1$ (`this`), and $2^t-1$ is one of the factors.
  5. `s9` writes the product as $(2^{d+1}-1)q$ with $q$ an integer.
  6. With $2^{d+1}-1\ne0$ (`hne` and its child) and the cast identity `hcast`, the term equals $c_jq$, which is the integer witness.

## Merging odd and $2$-adic information {steps: combine combine.hint combine.hint.this combine.s2 combine.hcop combine.hdvd combine.s5}

`combine` states: let $\Omega$ be odd with $\Omega p=2^aA$, and suppose $B=Mp$ and $m=M$ for integers $A,B,m$. Then there is an integer $z$ with $z\,2^a=Mp$.

1. `hint` gives $\Omega B=2^a(mA)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$. It is proved by checking the identity after casting to $\mathbb{Q}$ (the step `this`).
2. `s2` writes $\Omega=2t+1$.
3. `hcop` gives that $2^a$ and $\Omega$ are coprime.
4. `hdvd` gives $2^a\mid B\Omega$.
5. By coprimality, $2^a\mid B$. Step `s5` obtains $c$ with $B=2^ac$.

## The core estimate: the odd number $\Omega$ {steps: hcore hcore.hQcint hcore.hQcint.s1 hcore.hQcint.s2 hcore.h_x3a9div hcore.h_x3a9odd hcore.h_x3a9odd.s1 hcore.h_x3a9odd.s2 hcore.h_x3a9odd.s3 hcore.h_x3a9odd.s3.ht1 hcore.h_x3a9odd.s3.ht1.this}

`hcore` states that for $k\le n$ there is an integer $z$ with
$$z\,2^{k(k-1)/2}=\prod_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor<m\le n}(2^m-1)\cdot\sum_{j\le k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{k-j}-1}.$$

The first ingredients are these.

- **Coefficients (`hQcint`).** For every $j$ there is an integer $g_j$ with $Q_c(n,j)=2^{j(j-1)/2}g_j$. It is obtained from integer witnesses for the two Gaussian-binomial products (`s1` and `s2`).
- **Divisibility (`hΩdiv` in the Lean; the outline's step id is `hcore.h_x3a9div`).** With $\Omega=\prod_{t=1}^{2n}(2^t-1)$: for $1\le s\le 2n$ there is an integer $w$ with $w(2^s-1)=\Omega$. It is obtained by taking the factor $t=s$ out of the product.
- **Oddness (`hΩodd` in the Lean; step id `hcore.h_x3a9odd`).** $\Omega$ is odd. The proof establishes a property of the product by showing that it is multiplicative and holds on factors:
  - products of odd integers are odd (`s1`);
  - $1$ is odd (`s2`);
  - each $2^t-1$ with $t\ge1$ is odd, as an even number minus an odd one (`s3`, with $t\ne0$ from $1\le t$).

## The core estimate: integral reciprocal terms {steps: hcore.key1 hcore.key1.s1 hcore.key1.s1.s1 hcore.key1.s1.s2 hcore.key1.s1.hne hcore.key1.s1.hne.this hcore.key1.s1.h2a hcore.key1.s1.this hcore.key1.s2 hcore.key1.s2.s1 hcore.key1.s2.s2 hcore.key1.s2.hne hcore.key1.s2.hne.this hcore.key1.s2.h2b hcore.key1.s2.this hcore.hK hcore.hK.this hcore.hI hcore.hI.h1 hcore.hI.h2}

`key1` states that for $a\ne b$, both $\le 2n$, the number $\Omega\,2^a\cdot\frac{1}{2^a-2^b}$ is an integer. The proof splits according to the order of $a$ and $b$.

- **Case $a<b$ (`s1`).**
  - Write $b=a+d+1$ (`s1.s1`).
  - Obtain an integer $w$ with $w(2^{d+1}-1)=\Omega$ (`s1.s2`).
  - Record $2^{d+1}-1\ne0$ (`hne`, with `this`).
  - `h2a` is routine (positivity): $2^a\ne0$.
  - The second `this` is routine (ring): $2^a-2^a2^{d+1}=-(2^a(2^{d+1}-1))$.
