## Idea and method

The statement fixes three rational quantities. Write $G(m,j)=\prod_{i<j}\frac{2^{m-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}$, where $m-i$ is truncated natural subtraction. Then
$$\mathrm{Qc}(n,k)=(-1)^k\,2^{k(k-1)/2}\,G(n,k)\prod_{i<n}\frac{2^{2n-k-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1},\qquad \mathrm{Qx}(n)=\sum_{k\le n}\mathrm{Qc}(n,k)\Big(\frac{3}{2^n}\Big)^k,$$
$$\mathrm{Aq}(n)=\sum_{k\le n}\Big(\sum_{j\le k}\frac{\mathrm{Qc}(n,j)}{2^{k-j}-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{3}{2^n}\Big)^k+\mathrm{Qx}(n)\sum_{j<n}\frac{3}{2^{j+1}-3}.$$
The claim is that for every $n$ there is an integer $d\ge 1$ with $d^2\le 2^{3n^2}$ such that $d\,\mathrm{Qx}(n)$ and $d\,\mathrm{Aq}(n)$ are both integers.

The proof starts from one observation. Take the nodes $x_i=2^i$ for $0\le i\le n$ and the polynomial $N(X)=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(X-2^K)$. Then the numbers $2^k\,\mathrm{Qc}(n,k)$ are exactly the Lagrange interpolation coefficients of $N$ at these nodes.

The proof computes the value of $N'$ at a node in two ways, once from the interpolation formula and once from the product rule. Comparing the two expresses the awkward inner sums $\sum_{j\le k}\mathrm{Qc}(n,j)/(2^{k-j}-1)$ through reciprocals $1/(2^a-2^b)$.

Denominators are then cleared in three ways:

- an odd integer $\Omega=\prod_{1\le t\le 2n}(2^t-1)$, whose oddness lets 2-adic divisibility pass through;
- the integrality of the Gaussian binomials $G(m,j)$;
- the divisibility of $\prod_{n/2<m\le n}(2^m-1)$ by every $2^{s}-1$ with $1\le s\le n$.

The factor $\prod_{2\le j<n}(2^{j+1}-3)$ clears the last sum. Finally the size of the resulting $d$ is bounded by comparing exponents of $2$. The values $n=5$ and $n=7$ are given special constants, which are checked by computation.

## A telescoping product identity {steps: id1_test id1_test.s1 id1_test.s2 id1_test.s2.ih' id1_test.s2.s2}

Write $P_m=\prod_{s<m}(2^{s+1}-1)$. The step `id1_test` shows that for $j\le n$
$$\Big(\prod_{i<j}(2^{n-i}-1)\Big)\,P_{n-j}=P_n .$$

The proof is by induction on $j$.

- **Base case** ($j=0$): routine (closed by simplification).
- **Inductive step.** Assume $j+1\le n$. Then the hypothesis $j\le n$ needed by the induction hypothesis holds, which gives `id1_test.s2.ih'`. Next, `id1_test.s2.s2` writes $n-j=m+1$. The goal becomes the statement that the new factor $2^{n-j}-1$, multiplied into $P_{n-(j+1)}$, reproduces $P_{n-j}$. This is the last factor of $P_{m+1}$ peeled off.

## Products of differences of powers of two {steps: lower_test lower_test.h1 lower_test.h1.hi' lower_test.this upper_test upper_test.e upper_test.h1 rhs_test rhs_test.e rhs_test.h1}

Three closed forms for products of the shape $\prod(2^j-2^i)$ follow.

**`lower_test`.** It states $\prod_{i<j}(2^j-2^i)=2^{j(j-1)/2}P_j$.
- `lower_test.h1` factors each term, using $i<j$ from `lower_test.h1.hi'`:
$$2^j-2^i=2^i\,(2^{\,j-1-i+1}-1).$$
- The reflected product of the second factors is $P_j$.
- The powers of two collect to $2^{\sum_{i<j}i}$. The step `lower_test.this` uses Gauss' summation formula to show $2\sum_{i<j}i=j(j-1)$, which leaves the goal $\sum_{i<j}i=j(j-1)/2$.

**`upper_test`.** For $j\le n$ it states
$$\prod_{j<i\le n}(2^j-2^i)=(-1)^{n-j}\,2^{j(n-j)}\,P_{n-j}.$$
- `upper_test.e` rewrites $(j,n]$ as $[j+1,n+1)$, and the product is reindexed over $t<n-j$.
- `upper_test.h1` writes each factor as $2^j-2^{j+1+t}=-2^j(2^{t+1}-1)$.

**`rhs_test`.** For $j\le n$ it states
$$\Big(\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K)\Big)P_{n-j}=(-1)^n\,2^{jn}\,P_{2n-j}.$$
- `rhs_test.e` rewrites $(n,2n]=[n+1,2n+1)$.
- `rhs_test.h1` writes $2^j-2^{n+1+t}=-2^j(2^{n-j+t+1}-1)$ for $t<n$.
- The resulting $n$ factors $2^{n-j+t+1}-1$ concatenate with $P_{n-j}$ into $P_{2n-j}$.

## $2^j\,\mathrm{Qc}(n,j)$ is a Lagrange coefficient {steps: id1_full id1_full.hPne id1_full.hPne.this id1_full.hG1 id1_full.hG2 id1_full.hsplit id1_full.hdisj id1_full.hR id1_full.s7 id1_full.hsign id1_full.ha id1_full.hexp id1_full.hexp.s1 id1_full.hexp.s2}

The step `id1_full` proves, for $j\le n$, with $\mathrm{Qc}(n,j)$ written out:
$$\mathrm{Qc}(n,j)\cdot 2^j\prod_{\substack{i\le n\\ i\ne j}}(2^j-2^i)=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K).$$

The proof assembles the following pieces.

- **Nonvanishing.** `id1_full.hPne` shows $P_m\neq 0$, because each factor exceeds $0$ by `id1_full.hPne.this` ($1<2^{s+1}$).
- **The two products in $\mathrm{Qc}$ as ratios.**
  - `id1_full.hG1`: $G(n,j)=P_n/(P_{n-j}P_j)$.
  - `id1_full.hG2`: $\prod_{i<n}\frac{2^{2n-j-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}=P_{2n-j}/(P_{n-j}P_n)$.
- **Splitting the index set.** `id1_full.hsplit` and `id1_full.hdisj` split $\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{j\}$ into the disjoint pieces $[0,j)$ and $(j,n]$. The two resulting products are covered by the closed forms of the previous section.
- **Right-hand side.** `id1_full.hR` is the instance of the identity from `rhs_test` needed here.
- **Exponent bookkeeping.**
  - `id1_full.s7` writes $n=j+m$.
  - `id1_full.hsign` gives $(-1)^{j+m}=(-1)^j(-1)^m$.
  - `id1_full.ha` gives $2\cdot\frac{j(j-1)}{2}=j(j-1)$, using that $j(j-1)$ is even.
  - `id1_full.hexp` gives the exponent identity $j(j+m)=\frac{j(j-1)}2+j+\frac{j(j-1)}2+jm$. It is proved by induction on $j$, and both its base case and its inductive step are routine (closed by simplification, with nonlinear arithmetic for the step).

Splitting the product over the union and cancelling a nonzero common factor then yields the identity.

## Lagrange interpolation {steps: lagr_eval lagr_eval.hinj lagr_eval.s2 lagr_eval.s2.s1 lagr_eval.s2.s2 lagr_eval.s2.s2.this lagr_eval.s3 lagr_eval.s3.s1 lagr_eval.s3.hk' lagr_eval.s3.hk'.this lagr_eval.s3.s3 lagr_eval.s3.s4 lagr_eval.s3.s5}

The step `lagr_eval` is the uniqueness half of Lagrange interpolation over $\mathbb Q$. The hypotheses are:
- the nodes $x_0,\dots,x_n$ are distinct;
- $\deg N\le n$;
- the numbers $r_j$ satisfy $r_j\prod_{i\ne j}(x_j-x_i)=N(x_j)$ for all $j\le n$.

The conclusion is
$$N=\sum_{j\le n} r_j\prod_{i\ne j}(X-x_i).$$

The proof uses the fact that two polynomials agreeing on more points than the degree of their difference are equal.

- `lagr_eval.hinj` turns distinctness into injectivity of $x$ on $\{0,\dots,n\}$, so the image has $n+1$ points.
- `lagr_eval.s2` reduces the degree requirement to showing that the degree of the difference is $<n+1$. It handles the two terms separately:
  - `lagr_eval.s2.s1`: $\deg N<n+1$.
  - `lagr_eval.s2.s2`: the interpolating sum has degree $<n+1$. Each summand is a constant times $n$ linear factors, and `lagr_eval.s2.s2.this` shows their degrees add up to $n$.
- `lagr_eval.s3` asks for agreement at every image point.
  - `lagr_eval.s3.s1` picks $k$ with $y=x_k$.
  - `lagr_eval.s3.hk'` derives $k\le n$, using `lagr_eval.s3.hk'.this`.
  - `lagr_eval.s3.s3` is routine (simplification). It evaluates the $k$-th summand as $r_k\prod_{i\ne k}(x_k-x_i)$, which is $N(x_k)$ by hypothesis.
  - `lagr_eval.s3.s4` shows every other summand vanishes at $x_k$, since its product contains the factor $x_k-x_k=0$.
  - `lagr_eval.s3.s5` disposes of the impossible case $k\notin\{0,\dots,n\}$.
