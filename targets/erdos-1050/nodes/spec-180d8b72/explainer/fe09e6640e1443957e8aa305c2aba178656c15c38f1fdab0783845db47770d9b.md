## How the proof goes

Write $T=\sum_{m\ge0}1/(2^{m+3}-3)$, $x_n=3/2^n$ and $R_n=3T\,Q_x(n)-A_q(n)$, as in the statement. The statement has no hypothesis about denominators; apart from that one missing hypothesis (and so one argument fewer in `intro Qc hQc Qx hQx Aq hAq n`) this proof's tactic script is the same, line for line, as that of `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h4`.

The idea is that $R_n$ is the sum of a convergent series in $x_n$ whose first $2n+1$ coefficients vanish:
$$R_n=\sum_{K>2n}\frac{\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K-2^L)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(2^K-2^i)}\,x_n^K\qquad(n\ge1).$$
The vanishing comes from Lagrange interpolation: the numbers $2^jQ_c(n,j)$ are the interpolation weights of $N(X)=\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(X-2^L)$ at the nodes $2^0,\dots,2^n$, and $N(2^K)=0$ for $n<K\le 2n$. Each remaining coefficient lies in $(0,\,2\cdot2^{-K}]$, so $R_n$ is positive and at most a geometric tail, $R_n\le 8\,(x_n/2)^{2n+1}$ for $n\ge2$; the stated bound is then an inequality between powers of $2$ and $3$. The cases $n=0$ and $n=1$ are computed directly, using $0<T\le 2/5$.

## The four identities for products of $2^a-2^b$ {steps: id1_test lower_test upper_test rhs_test id1_full}

These five steps have the same statements and proofs as the first five steps of the proof of `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3`, where they are explained in more detail. Write $P_m=\prod_{s<m}(2^{s+1}-1)$.

- `id1_test`: for $j\le n$, $\big(\prod_{i<j}(2^{n-i}-1)\big)P_{n-j}=P_n$ (induction on $j$).
- `lower_test`: $\prod_{i<j}(2^j-2^i)=2^{j(j-1)/2}P_j$.
- `upper_test`: for $j\le n$, $\prod_{j<i\le n}(2^j-2^i)=(-1)^{n-j}2^{j(n-j)}P_{n-j}$.
- `rhs_test`: for $j\le n$, $\big(\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K)\big)P_{n-j}=(-1)^n2^{jn}P_{2n-j}$.
- `id1_full` combines them: for $j\le n$,
$$Q_c(n,j)\,2^j\prod_{i\le n,\ i\ne j}(2^j-2^i)=\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j-2^K),$$
with $Q_c(n,j)$ written out as in the statement.

In other words, the numbers $2^jQ_c(n,j)$ are the Lagrange interpolation weights of the polynomial $N(X)=\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(X-2^L)$ at the nodes $2^0,2^1,\dots,2^n$.

## Interpolation, and the coefficients beyond $n$ {steps: lagr_eval tail_coeff}

`lagr_eval` is Lagrange interpolation over $\mathbb Q$: if $x_0,\dots,x_n$ are distinct, $\deg N\le n$, and $r_j\prod_{i\ne j}(x_j-x_i)=N(x_j)$ for every $j\le n$, then $N=\sum_{j\le n}r_j\prod_{i\le n,\,i\ne j}(X-x_i)$. It is proved by showing the difference has degree at most $n$ and vanishes at the $n+1$ nodes.

`tail_coeff` is the identity the rest of the proof is built on. For $K>n$ and any $Q_c$ satisfying the conclusion of `id1_full` for all $j\le n$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{K-j}-1}=\frac{\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K-2^L)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(2^K-2^i)}.$$
The proof takes the nodes $x_i=2^i$ and the polynomial $N$ above, checks that $\deg N\le n$ (`tail_coeff.hNdeg`) and the weight condition (`tail_coeff.hr`), applies `lagr_eval`, and evaluates both sides at $X=2^K$ (`tail_coeff.hev`). Dividing by $\prod_{i\le n}(2^K-2^i)$, which is nonzero because $K>n$, the $j$-th term becomes $Q_c(n,j)\,2^j/(2^K-2^j)=Q_c(n,j)/(2^{K-j}-1)$.

## Splitting the constant $3T$ {steps: tail3 split3T}

Let $T=\sum_{m\ge 0}1/(2^{m+3}-3)$ as in the statement.

`split3T` states that for every $n$
$$3T=\sum_{j<n}\frac{3}{2^{j+1}-3}+\sum_{m\ge 0}\frac{3}{2^{m+n+1}-3}.$$
With $g(m)=3/(2^{m+1}-3)$, $3T=\sum_{m\ge0}g(m+2)$, and $g(0)+g(1)=-3+3=0$ (`split3T.hfirst`), so $3T=\sum_{m\ge 0}g(m)$; this sum is then split after its first $n$ terms.

`tail3` states that for $n\ge 1$
$$\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{3}{2^{m+n+1}-3}=\sum_{s\ge0}\frac{(3/2^n)^{s+1}}{2^{s+1}-1}.$$
Both sides are the double sum of the nonnegative numbers $f(m,s)=\big(3/2^{m+n+1}\big)^{s+1}$: summing over $s$ first gives the geometric series $3/(2^{m+n+1}-3)$ (`tail3.inner1`), and summing over $m$ first gives $(3/2^n)^{s+1}/(2^{s+1}-1)$ (`tail3.inner2`). The order of summation is exchanged because all terms are nonnegative and the double series converges (`tail3.hU`, `tail3.hcomm`).

## The remainder as a power series {steps: cauchy_fin h4_series}

`cauchy_fin` is the product of a polynomial with a power series: if $\sum_{s\ge0}a_s x^{s+1}=A$, then the series over $K$ of $\sum_{j\le n,\ j<K}q_j\,a_{K-j-1}\,x^K$ has sum $\big(\sum_{j\le n}q_jx^j\big)A$.

`h4_series` states that for $n\ge 1$, with $x=3/2^n$, the remainder $R_n=3T\,Q_x(n)-A_q(n)$ is the sum of the series
$$R_n=\sum_{K>2n}\frac{\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K-2^L)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(2^K-2^i)}\,x^K .$$
The steps are these.

1. Put $a_s=1/(2^{s+1}-1)$ and $A=\sum_{s\ge0}a_sx^{s+1}$, which converges because $x/2<1$ (`h4_series.hx2`, `h4_series.hAsum`).
2. By `split3T` and `tail3`, $3T=c_n+A$ with $c_n=\sum_{j<n}3/(2^{j+1}-3)$ (`h4_series.h3T`).
3. The term $Q_x(n)\,c_n$ in $A_q(n)$ then cancels, leaving $R_n=Q_x(n)\,A-P$, where $P=\sum_{k\le n}\big(\sum_{j\le k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{k-j}-1}\big)x^k$ (`h4_series.hval`).
4. `cauchy_fin` with $q_j=Q_c(n,j)$ expands $Q_x(n)\,A$ as a series in $K$, and $P$ is a finite series (`h4_series.hP`); their difference is computed coefficient by coefficient (`h4_series.hfun`):
   - for $K\le n$ the two coefficients are equal, because the term $j=K$ of $P$'s inner sum is $Q_c(n,K)/0=0$;
   - for $K>n$ the coefficient is $\sum_{j\le n}Q_c(n,j)/(2^{K-j}-1)$, which `tail_coeff` turns into the quotient of products, using `id1_full` for the concrete $Q_c$ (`h4_series.hid1`);
   - for $n<K\le 2n$ that quotient is $0$, because the numerator contains the factor $2^K-2^K$.

## Bounding each term {steps: prod_one_sub_ge nd_bound}

`prod_one_sub_ge` is the inequality $1-\sum_{i\in s}a_i\le\prod_{i\in s}(1-a_i)$ for $0\le a_i\le 1$, by induction on the finite set $s$.

`nd_bound` states that for $n\ge 1$ and $K\ge 2n+1$ the coefficient in `h4_series` satisfies
$$0<\frac{\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K-2^L)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(2^K-2^i)}\le 2\cdot 2^{-K}.$$
Every factor is positive since $L,i<K$. The numerator is at most $(2^K)^n$ (`nd_bound.hN`). The denominator is at least $(2^K)^{n+1}/2$ (`nd_bound.hD`): each factor is $2^K(1-2^{-(K-i)})$, the numbers $2^{-(K-i)}$ for $i\le n$ sum to at most $1/2$ because $K-n\ge 2$, and `prod_one_sub_ge` applies.

## The bound for $n\ge 2$ {steps: ineq_pow h4_big}

`ineq_pow` states $9\cdot 81^n\le 256^n$ for $n\ge 2$ (induction on $n$).

`h4_big` is the conclusion of the statement for $n\ge 2$. Write $x=3/2^n$, so $x/2\le 3/8$ (`h4_big.hx2`), and let $G_K$ be the $K$-th term of the series in `h4_series`.

- **Positivity.** Every $G_K$ is $\ge 0$ and $G_{2n+1}>0$ (`h4_big.hGnn`, `h4_big.hGpos`, both from `nd_bound`), so the sum $R_n$ is positive.
- **Upper bound.** By `nd_bound`, $G_K\le 2(x/2)^K$ for $K>2n$. Summing the geometric series, $R_n\le 2(x/2)^{2n+1}/(1-x/2)\le 8(x/2)^{2n+1}$, using $1/(1-x/2)\le 4$. Squaring, $R_n^2\le 64\,(x/2)^{4n+2}$, and $x/2=3/2^{n+1}$.
- **Arithmetic.** It remains that $64\cdot 3^{4n+2}\cdot 2^{4n^2}\le 2^{2n+4}\,(2^{n+1})^{4n+2}$. The right side is $2^{4n^2}\cdot 64\cdot 256^n$ and the left side is $2^{4n^2}\cdot 64\cdot 9\cdot 81^n$, so this is `ineq_pow`. It is proved in $\mathbb N$ and cast to $\mathbb R$.

## The constant $T$, and the cases $n=0$ and $n=1$ {steps: T_facts s17 hQx0 hAq0 e hQx1 hAq1}

`T_facts` states that the series defining $T$ converges, $T>0$ and $T\le 2/5$, from $2^{m+3}-3\ge 5\cdot 2^m$, which bounds the $m$-th term by $\frac15 2^{-m}$. The step `s17` takes $T>0$ and $T\le 2/5$ out of it.

The proof then introduces the hypotheses of the statement and splits on $n$.

- $n=0$: $Q_x(0)=1$ (`hQx0`) and $A_q(0)=0$ (`hAq0`), so $R_0=3T>0$, and $R_0^2\cdot 2^0=9T^2$ (`e`), which is at most $2^4$ since $T\le 2/5$.
- $n=1$: $Q_x(1)=3/2$ (`hQx1`) and $A_q(1)=0$ (`hAq1`), so $R_1=\frac92T>0$, and $R_1^2\cdot 2^4\le 2^6$ again follows from $T\le 2/5$.
- $n\ge 2$: `h4_big`.
