## Overview

The statement asks for integer sequences $a_n, b_n$ with $b_n T - a_n$ never zero and tending to $0$, where $T = \sum_{m \ge 0} 1/(2^{m+3}-3)$. The proof builds them from explicit Padé-type rational approximations in the style of Borwein's $q$-series argument (the file's comment cites annex `f593a93a8960` on this node for the informal account). With $x_n = 3/2^n$ it defines three explicit families of rationals, $Q_c(n,k)$, $Q_x(n)$ and $A_q(n)$, and considers the remainder
$r_n = Q_x(n)\cdot 3T - A_q(n)$. Two estimates do the work:

- a denominator bound: some positive integer $d_n$ with $d_n^2 \le 2^{3n^2}$ makes $d_n Q_x(n)$ and $d_n A_q(n)$ integers. This is taken from the hole node `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3`, not proved here;
- a remainder bound: $r_n > 0$ and $r_n^2 \, 2^{4n^2} \le 2^{2n+4}$. This is the statement of the node `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h4`, but the file proves it inline (step `hrem_all`), so that the proof uses only the hole `--h3`.

Then $b_n = 3 d_n Q_x(n)$ and $a_n = d_n A_q(n)$ are integers, $b_n T - a_n = d_n r_n > 0$, and $(b_n T - a_n)^2 \le 64 \cdot 2^{-n}$, which tends to $0$. The four holes `--h1` to `--h4` are declared dependencies; the proof term uses `--h3` only.

## Product identities for the Gaussian coefficients {steps: hrem_all.id1_test, hrem_all.lower_test, hrem_all.upper_test, hrem_all.rhs_test, hrem_all.id1_full}

These are identities between finite products of numbers $2^a - 1$ and $2^j - 2^i$ in $\mathbb{Q}$. `id1_test` splits $\prod_{s<n}(2^{s+1}-1)$ at $j \le n$. `lower_test` and `upper_test` factor $\prod_{i<j}(2^j - 2^i)$ and $\prod_{j<i\le n}(2^j - 2^i)$ into a power of $2$, a sign and a product of $2^{s+1}-1$; `rhs_test` does the same for $\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(2^j - 2^K)$. Combined, `id1_full` says that for $j \le n$,
$Q_c(n,j)\cdot 2^j \cdot \prod_{i \le n,\, i \ne j} (2^j - 2^i) = \prod_{n < K \le 2n} (2^j - 2^K)$,
where $Q_c(n,j)$ is written out by its defining formula. Read with `lagr_eval` below, this says that the numbers $Q_c(n,j)\,2^j$ are the Lagrange-interpolation weights, at the nodes $2^0, \dots, 2^n$, of the degree-$n$ polynomial $\prod_{n<K\le 2n}(X - 2^K)$.

## Interpolation and the vanishing coefficients {steps: hrem_all.lagr_eval, hrem_all.tail_coeff}

`lagr_eval` is Lagrange interpolation over $\mathbb{Q}$: a polynomial $N$ of degree at most $n$, whose values at $n+1$ distinct points $x_0, \dots, x_n$ are $r_j \prod_{i \ne j}(x_j - x_i)$, equals $\sum_j r_j \prod_{i \ne j}(X - x_i)$. `tail_coeff` uses it: for every $K > n$, if $Q_c$ satisfies the identity of `id1_full`, then
$\sum_{j \le n} Q_c(n,j)/(2^{K-j}-1) = \prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K - 2^L) \big/ \prod_{i \le n}(2^K - 2^i)$.
For $n < K \le 2n$ the numerator has the factor $L = K$, so this is zero: these are the coefficients that cancel in the remainder.

## T as a constant plus a power series {steps: hrem_all.tail3, hrem_all.split3T, hrem_all.cauchy_fin, hrem_all.h4_series}

`split3T` splits, for every $n$, $3T = \sum_{j<n} 3/(2^{j+1}-3) + \sum_{m \ge 0} 3/(2^{m+n+1}-3)$; the first part is the rational constant that appears at the end of $A_q(n)$. `tail3` expands the tail, for $n \ge 1$, as a power series in $x_n = 3/2^n$: $\sum_m 3/(2^{m+n+1}-3) = \sum_{s \ge 0} x_n^{s+1}/(2^{s+1}-1)$. `cauchy_fin` multiplies a polynomial $\sum_{j\le n} q_j x^j$ by a convergent series $\sum_s a_s x^{s+1}$ term by term. `h4_series` puts these together: for $n \ge 1$, the remainder $r_n = Q_x(n)\cdot 3T - A_q(n)$ is the sum over $K > 2n$ only of
$\dfrac{\prod_{n<L\le 2n}(2^K - 2^L)}{\prod_{i \le n}(2^K - 2^i)}\, x_n^K$ (a `HasSum`, with every term for $K \le 2n$ equal to zero).

## Size estimates {steps: hrem_all.prod_one_sub_ge, hrem_all.nd_bound, hrem_all.ineq_pow, hrem_all.h4_big, hrem_all.T_facts}

`prod_one_sub_ge` is the inequality $1 - \sum_i a_i \le \prod_i (1 - a_i)$ for $0 \le a_i \le 1$. `nd_bound` uses it to show that, for $n \ge 1$ and $K \ge 2n+1$, the coefficient above lies in $(0,\ 2 \cdot 2^{-K}]$. `ineq_pow` is the numerical fact $9 \cdot 81^n \le 256^n$ for $n \ge 2$. From these, `h4_big` proves the remainder bound for $n \ge 2$: $r_n > 0$ and $r_n^2\, 2^{4n^2} \le 2^{2n+4}$. `T_facts` shows the series for $T$ is summable and $0 < T \le 2/5$, by comparison with $\tfrac15 (\tfrac12)^m$.

## The remainder bound for every n {steps: hrem_all, hrem_all.s17, hrem_all.hQx0, hrem_all.hAq0, hrem_all.e, hrem_all.hQx1, hrem_all.hAq1}

`hrem_all` is the statement of node `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h4`: for $Q_c$, $Q_x$, $A_q$ given by their formulas, and under the denominator hypothesis of `--h3`, for every $n$, $r_n > 0$ and $r_n^2\, 2^{4n^2} \le 2^{2n+4}$. The proof introduces the denominator hypothesis without a name and does not use it. It takes $0 < T \le 2/5$ from `T_facts` (`s17`) and splits on $n$. For $n = 0$: $Q_x(0) = 1$ and $A_q(0) = 0$ (`hQx0`, `hAq0`), so $r_0 = 3T > 0$ and the bound reads $9T^2 \le 16$ (`e`). For $n = 1$: $Q_x(1) = 3/2$ and $A_q(1) = 0$ (`hQx1`, `hAq1`), so $r_1 = \tfrac92 T > 0$ and the bound reads $\tfrac{81}{4} T^2 \cdot 16 \le 64$. Both follow from $T \le 2/5$. For $n \ge 2$ it applies `h4_big`.

## The approximants {steps: s2, s3, s4}

The main proof names the three families by their formulas (each `obtain` introduces a function and the equation defining it):
$Q_c(n,k) = (-1)^k\, 2^{k(k-1)/2} \prod_{i<k} \frac{2^{n-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1} \prod_{i<n} \frac{2^{2n-k-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}$ (`s2`), the product of a sign, a power of $2$ and two Gaussian binomial coefficients at $q = 2$;
$Q_x(n) = \sum_{k \le n} Q_c(n,k)\, x_n^k$ (`s3`);
$A_q(n) = \sum_{k \le n} \big(\sum_{j \le k} Q_c(n,j)/(2^{k-j}-1)\big)\, x_n^k + Q_x(n) \sum_{j<n} 3/(2^{j+1}-3)$ (`s4`).
All are rationals. Exponents are natural numbers, so $2n-k-i$ and $k(k-1)/2$ use truncated subtraction and natural-number division. In $A_q$ the term with $j = k$ divides by $2^0 - 1 = 0$, which Lean defines to be $0$.

## Denominators and remainder {steps: hden, hrem}

`hden` applies the hole `erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3` (the theorem `erdos_1050__h1_v2__h3` from this node's `Context.lean`) to these three families: for every $n$ there is a natural number $d_n > 0$ with $d_n^2 \le 2^{3n^2}$ such that $d_n Q_x(n)$ and $d_n A_q(n)$ are integers. `hrem` applies `hrem_all` to the same families and to `hden`: $r_n > 0$ and $r_n^2\, 2^{4n^2} \le 2^{2n+4}$ for every $n$.

## The integer sequences {steps: key, pos}

From `hden` the proof chooses, for every $n$, the $d_n$ and integers $\beta_n = d_n Q_x(n)$ and $a_n = d_n A_q(n)$ (Lean's `choose`, not an outline step), and offers $a_n$ and $b_n = 3\beta_n$ as the sequences the statement asks for. Step `key` shows $b_n T - a_n = d_n\, r_n$, using $\beta_n = d_n Q_x(n)$ (`key.hb'`) and $a_n = d_n A_q(n)$ (`key.ha'`) in $\mathbb{R}$. Step `pos` concludes $b_n T - a_n > 0$, since $d_n > 0$ and $r_n > 0$; in particular it is never zero, which is the first half of the statement.

## The limit {steps: bound, hsq, this}

Write $e_n = b_n T - a_n$. Step `bound` shows $e_n^2 \le 64 \cdot (1/2)^n$: squaring `key` and multiplying the bounds of `hden` and `hrem` gives $e_n^2\, 2^{4n^2} \le 2^{3n^2}\, 2^{2n+4}$ (`bound.h1`); writing $2^{4n^2} = 2^{n^2}\,2^{3n^2}$ (`bound.h2`) and cancelling $2^{3n^2}$ gives $e_n^2\, 2^{n^2} \le 2^{2n+4}$ (`bound.h3`); and $2^{2n+4}\, 2^n \le 64 \cdot 2^{n^2}$ because $3n + 4 \le n^2 + 6$ (`bound.h4`). Step `hsq` concludes $e_n^2 \to 0$ by squeezing between $0$ and $64 \cdot (1/2)^n$. Step `this` takes square roots, $\sqrt{e_n^2} \to \sqrt 0 = 0$; since $e_n > 0$ (`pos`), $\sqrt{e_n^2} = e_n$, so $e_n \to 0$, the second half of the statement.
