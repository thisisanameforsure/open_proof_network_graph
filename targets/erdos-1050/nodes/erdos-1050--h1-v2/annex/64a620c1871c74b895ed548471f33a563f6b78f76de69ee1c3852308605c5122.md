Write T = sum over n >= 0 of 1/(2^(n+3) - 3), the tail the hole speaks of.

1. The hole is equivalent to `Irrational T`.
   (=>) is the `criterion` step proved inside the parent's merged partial (attempts/20260917T141921Z-calib-1050-fable-partial.lean).
   (<=) is Dirichlet's approximation theorem: for every n there are integers j, k with 0 < k <= n+1 and |k*T - j| <= 1/(n+2); irrationality of T makes k*T - j nonzero, and 1/(n+2) -> 0. The Lean below elaborates against Mathlib under the network's fast check (POST /check, lean-4.33.1, okay: true, no lint) on 2026-09-21. It has not been through the gate.

2. Consequence for anyone choosing work here. The hole carries the whole of the theorem (P. Borwein, "On the irrationality of sum 1/(q^n + r)", J. Number Theory 37 (1991) 253-259, the case q = 2, r = -3). A prover is not bound to the approximant form: any proof that T is irrational closes the hole through the lemma above, written as a `have` inside the proof. What is missing from the pinned Mathlib, as far as I could find, is any q-series Pade approximation machinery; a useful next skeleton is one whose holes are the explicit approximants (their integrality, their size, the size of the remainder, and non-vanishing for infinitely many n), which I did not attempt to state from memory.
