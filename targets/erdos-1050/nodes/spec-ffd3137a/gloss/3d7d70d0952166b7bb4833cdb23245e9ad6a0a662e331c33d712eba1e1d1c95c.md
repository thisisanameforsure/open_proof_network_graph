For any $Q_c : \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Q}$ given by
$$Q_c(n,k) = (-1)^k\, 2^{\lfloor k(k-1)/2\rfloor} \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{\,n-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}\;\cdot\;\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{\,2n-k-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}$$
(the same definition as in the hole erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3), and for all natural numbers $k \le n$,
$$\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{\,k-j}-1} \;=\; Q_c(n,k)\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-2^{\,n+1+i-k}} \;-\; \sum_{b=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{b+1}}{2^{b+1}-1} \;-\; \sum_{b=0}^{n-k-1}\frac{1}{1-2^{b+1}}\Bigr) \;+\; \sum_{b=0}^{n-k-1}\frac{Q_c(n,k+b+1)\,2^{b+1}}{2^{b+1}-1},$$
in $\mathbb{Q}$.

Reading notes. Exponent subtractions are natural-number subtraction, which stops at $0$; under $k \le n$ none of $k-j$, $n+1+i-k$ or $n-k$ truncates. On the left, the term $j = k$ divides by $2^0-1=0$, and Lean's $x/0=0$ makes it $0$, so the left side is in effect $p_k = \sum_{j<k} Q_c(n,j)/(2^{k-j}-1)$. On the right every denominator is nonzero. The right side expresses $p_k$ through $Q_c(n,k)$ and the values $Q_c(n,j)$ for $k < j \le n$ only.

The doc comment explains the identity as the regular part at $z=2^k$ of $\sum_j Q_c(n,j)/(z/2^j-1)$ and says every term on the right has 2-adic valuation at least $k(k-1)/2$; that valuation claim is not part of the theorem.
