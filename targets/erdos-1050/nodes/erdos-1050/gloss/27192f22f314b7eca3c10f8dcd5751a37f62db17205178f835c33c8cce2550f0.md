This file states the theorem `Opn.erdos_1050`: the real number
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{n+1}-3}$
is irrational.

In detail:

- The sum is Lean's `∑'` over all natural numbers $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ (Mathlib's unconditional sum, a `tsum`). Each term is the real number $1/(2^{n+1}-3)$, computed in $\mathbb{R}$. The terms are $-1, 1, \tfrac{1}{5}, \tfrac{1}{13}, \tfrac{1}{29}, \dots$; the first, at $n = 0$, is $1/(2-3) = -1$.
- Re-indexed with $m = n+1$, this is the sum $\sum_{m \ge 1} 1/(2^m - 3)$ of Erdős problem 1050. The file's own comment says the statement is imported, as stated, from google-deepmind/formal-conjectures (`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1050.lean`, Apache-2.0).
- `Irrational x` is Mathlib's predicate: $x$ is not equal to $p/q$, read as a real number, for any rational $p/q$.
- Two Lean conventions do not come into play here, but a reader should know the file leans on neither. Division by zero is defined to be $0$ in Lean; no denominator $2^{n+1}-3$ is zero (it is $-1$ at $n = 0$ and at least $1$ afterwards). And `∑'` of a family that is not summable is defined to be $0$; the statement itself does not assert that the series converges, so the claim is about the `tsum` as Lean defines it. (The series does converge absolutely, since its terms are eventually bounded by a multiple of $2^{-n}$, so the `tsum` is the ordinary sum; the merged proof checks summability in its step `hT`.)

The statement has no hypotheses and no free variables. The file imports all of Mathlib; its body is `sorry`, the placeholder a statement file carries in place of a proof.
