## What is proved, and the plan

Fix $c = 8/3$. For $n \ge 1$ and $1 \le k \le n$ write
$$D_k=\prod_{1\le l\le n,\ l\ne k}\bigl(1-2^{\,l-k}\bigr),\qquad A_k(x)=-\frac{\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-x\,2^{\,t+k}\bigr)}{D_k},\qquad h_i=\bigl(1-c\,2^{i}\bigr)^{-1},$$
$$P(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(1-c\,2^k\bigr),\qquad M_n=\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}\bigl(1-2^k\bigr),\qquad W(n)=(n-2)!\,P(n)\,M_n .$$
The statement asks for integers $a(n)$ with $a(n)=9^nW(n)\bigl(S_1(n)-S_2(n)\bigr)$ for $n\ge1$, where
$S_1=\sum_{k=1}^n A_k(c)\sum_{i=1}^k h_i$ and $S_2=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} B_i/(2^i-1)$ with $B_i=\sum_{k=1}^n A_k(c)\,(c\,2^k)^{-i}$.

The proof shows separately that $9^nW(n)S_1(n)$ is an integer (`head_integral`) and that $9^nW(n)S_2(n)$ is an integer (`hmer`), and takes $a(n)=a_1(n)-a_2(n)$; the last four lines of the proof check the identity by `ring`. The factor $(n-2)!$ is carried along as an integer and plays no other part. The proof uses no other node of the graph (the outline lists none), and its comments point to annex a03db62f… on this node for the informal argument.

Throughout, $H_e=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2^k)^e/D_k$ for $e \ge 0$ (a sum the proof shows to be an integer).

## Finite sums of integers {steps: hsumZ}

`hsumZ` is bookkeeping: if every term $g(i)$, $i\in s$, of a finite sum of reals is (the image of) an integer, so is the sum. It is proved by induction on the finite set $s$, and is used whenever a sum is shown to be an integer term by term.

## Lagrange sums at the nodes $2^k$ {steps: h0 hrec hW hV}

For a finite set $s$ of naturals and $k\in s$ write $D_s(k)=\prod_{l\in s,\ l\ne k}(1-2^{\,l-k})$; for $s=\{1,\dots,n\}$ this is $D_k$.

- `h0`: for nonempty $s$, $\sum_{k\in s}1/D_s(k)=1$. Mathlib's Lagrange basis polynomials for the nodes $2^{-k}$ sum to $1$; evaluating that identity at $0$ gives exactly these terms.
- `hrec`: for $a\in s$ and any weight $f$,
$$\sum_{k\in s}\frac{2^k f(k)}{D_s(k)}=\sum_{k\in s\setminus\{a\}}\frac{2^k f(k)}{D_{s\setminus\{a\}}(k)}+2^a\sum_{k\in s}\frac{f(k)}{D_s(k)},$$
by splitting off the factor $l=a$ from each $D_s(k)$.
- `hW`: for every $s$ and every $e\ge0$, $\sum_{k\in s}(2^k)^e/D_s(k)$ is an integer. Induction on $|s|+e$: the empty set gives $0$, $e=0$ gives $1$ by `h0`, and otherwise `hrec` with $f(k)=(2^k)^{e-1}$ writes the sum as the sum for $(s\setminus\{a\},e)$ plus $2^a$ times the sum for $(s,e-1)$. In particular every $H_e$ is an integer.
- `hV`: for $m+1<|s|$, $\sum_{k\in s}(2^k)^{-(m+1)}/D_s(k)=0$. Induction on $m$, again from `hrec`, with `h0` in the base case.

## Geometric sums, a divisibility, and an integer polynomial {steps: hdvd hgeo hpoly}

- `hgeo`: for $m\ge1$ and every $k$, $\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{-im}=\dfrac{1-2^{-km}}{2^m-1}$, by induction on $k$.
- `hdvd`: for $1\le i\le n-1$, the integer $2^i-1$ divides $\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}(1-2^k)$. The multiple $k_0=i\lfloor n/i\rfloor$ of $i$ lies in $[\lceil n/2\rceil,n]$, and $2^i-1$ divides $2^{k_0}-1$. This is what the factor $M_n$ in the statement is for.
- `hpoly`: for $n\ge1$ there are integers $\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{n-1}$ with $\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}(1-2^t y)=\sum_{j<n}\alpha_j y^j$ for every real $y$ (the coefficients of that integer polynomial, of degree less than $n$). Consequently $A_k(x)=-\frac1{D_k}\sum_{j<n}\alpha_j\,(x\,2^k)^j$.

## The residues are integers {steps: residue_integral}

`residue_integral`: for $1\le i\le n$, the number $b_i=\sum_{k=i}^{n}A_k(2^{-i})$ is an integer. In fact each term is. Write $k=i+d$ and $Q(m)=\prod_{s=1}^{m}(1-2^s)$, which is never $0$.

- At $x=2^{-i}$ the numerator of $A_k$ is the integer $\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}(1-2^{\,t+d})$.
- $D_k$ splits into the factors with $l<k$ and $l>k$; the first part times $\prod_{s=1}^{k-1}(-2^s)$ is $Q(k-1)$, and the second part is $Q(n-k)$.
- Two identities about $Q$: $Q(a+b)$ is an integer multiple of $Q(a)Q(b)$ (the Gaussian binomial coefficient at $q=2$ is an integer), and $Q(d)\prod_{t=1}^{m}(1-2^{\,t+d})=Q(d+m)$.
- Together these give numerator $=z_1z_2\,Q(k-1)Q(n-k)$ for integers $z_1,z_2$, so the term $A_k(2^{-i})$ equals the integer $-z_1z_2\prod_{s=1}^{k-1}(-2^s)$.

## The polynomial part {steps: polypart_integral}

`polypart_integral`: for $n\ge1$, with $R(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_i\,h_i$, the number $3^n M_n\bigl(S_1(n)-R(n)\bigr)$ is an integer. So $S_1$ differs from its "residue part" $R$ by something whose denominators are only powers of $3$ and divisors of $M_n$.

- Exchanging the order of summation, $R=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_k(2^{-i})\,h_i$, so $S_1-R=\sum_{k}\sum_{i\le k}\bigl(A_k(c)-A_k(2^{-i})\bigr)h_i$.
- For each $k$ and $i\ge1$, using `hpoly` and the factorisation of $y^j-c^j$ with $y=2^{-i}$:
$$\bigl(A_k(c)-A_k(2^{-i})\bigr)h_i=\frac1{D_k}\sum_{j<n}\sum_{s<j}\alpha_j\,2^{kj}\,c^{s}\,2^{-i(j-s)} .$$
- Summing over $i=1,\dots,k$ with `hgeo` turns $2^{kj}\sum_i 2^{-i(j-s)}$ into $\bigl((2^k)^j-(2^k)^s\bigr)/(2^{j-s}-1)$.
- Summing over $k$ then gives $\sum_{j<n}\sum_{s<j}\alpha_j\,c^s\,(H_j-H_s)/(2^{j-s}-1)$. Here $H_j,H_s$ are integers by `hW`, $2^{j-s}-1$ divides $M_n$ by `hdvd` (as $1\le j-s\le n-1$), and $3^n c^s=3^{n-s}8^s$. Each term times $3^nM_n$ is therefore an integer, and so is the sum.

## The first sum $S_1$ {steps: head_integral s12}

`head_integral` produces integers $a_1(n)$ with $a_1(n)=9^nW(n)S_1(n)$ for $n\ge1$. Writing $S_1=(S_1-R)+R$:
$$9^nW(n)S_1=(n-2)!\Bigl[\bigl(3^nP(n)\bigr)\cdot 3^nM_n(S_1-R)+3^nM_n\cdot\bigl(3^nP(n)R\bigr)\Bigr].$$
Here $3^nP(n)=\prod_{k=1}^n(3-8\cdot2^k)$ is an integer, $3^nM_n(S_1-R)$ is an integer by `polypart_integral`, and $3^nP(n)R$ is an integer because $3^nP(n)\,h_i=3\prod_{j\ne i}(3-8\cdot2^j)$ and each $b_i$ is an integer by `residue_integral`. The sequence $a_1$ is then chosen from these integers (with value $0$ at $n=0$); `s12` unpacks it.

## The second sum $S_2$ {steps: hmer s14}

`hmer` produces integers $a_2(n)$ with $a_2(n)=9^nW(n)S_2(n)$ for $n\ge1$. Its step `key` shows that for $1\le i\le n-1$ the number $3^{\,n-1-i}B_i$ is an integer: expanding $A_k(c)$ with `hpoly`,
$$B_i=-\sum_{j<n}\alpha_j\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(c\,2^k)^{\,j-i}}{D_k}.$$
For $j<i$ the inner sum is $c^{-(i-j)}$ times a sum that vanishes by `hV` (since $1\le i-j<n$); for $j\ge i$ it is $c^{\,j-i}H_{j-i}$, and $3^{\,n-1-i}c^{\,j-i}=3^{\,n-1-j}8^{\,j-i}$.

Then each term of $9^nW(n)S_2(n)$ is an integer: write $9^n=3^{i+1}\cdot3^{\,n-1-i}\cdot3^n$, use $3^nP(n)=\prod_k(3-8\cdot 2^k)$, and use `hdvd` to write $M_n=(2^i-1)q$, which cancels the denominator $2^i-1$. The term becomes $3^{i+1}(n-2)!\prod_k(3-8\cdot2^k)\,q$ times the integer from `key`. (Inside `hmer` the proof restates `hdvd` and `hsumZ` as local copies.) `s14` unpacks $a_2$, and the proof ends with $a=a_1-a_2$.
