There is a sequence of integers $a(n)$, indexed by the natural numbers, such that for every $n \ge 1$
$$a(n) \;=\; 9^n\,W(n)\,\bigl(S_1(n)-S_2(n)\bigr),$$
where, with $c = 8/3$ and all arithmetic in the real numbers:

$W(n)=\displaystyle (n-2)!\;\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(1-c\,2^{k}\bigr)\;\prod_{k=\lfloor (n+1)/2\rfloor}^{n}\bigl(1-2^{k}\bigr)$;

for $1\le k\le n$, $A_k=\dfrac{-\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,t+k}\bigr)}{\prod_{1\le l\le n,\ l\ne k}\bigl(1-2^{\,l-k}\bigr)}$;

$S_1(n)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} A_k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(1-c\,2^{i}\bigr)^{-1}$;

$S_2(n)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n} A_k\,\bigl(c\,2^{k}\bigr)^{-i}}{2^{i}-1}$.

In other words: for each $n\ge1$ the real number $9^n W(n)(S_1(n)-S_2(n))$ is an integer. All sums here are finite.

Details the Lean fixes:

$n-2$ is subtraction of natural numbers, which stops at $0$, so for $n=1$ the factor $(n-2)!$ is $0! = 1$. The lower limit $\lfloor (n+1)/2\rfloor$ is natural-number division, that is $\lceil n/2\rceil$.

In the denominator of $A_k$ the exponent $l-k$ is an integer and may be negative, so $2^{\,l-k}$ is a real power such as $1/2$.

For $n=1$ the product over $t$ in $A_k$ is empty (equal to $1$) and $S_2(1)=0$ (an empty sum).

Nothing is required of $a(0)$.
