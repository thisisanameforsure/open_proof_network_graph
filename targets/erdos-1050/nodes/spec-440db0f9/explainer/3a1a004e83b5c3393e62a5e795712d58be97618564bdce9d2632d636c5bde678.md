## Overview

Write $P(m,j) = \prod_{i<j} \frac{2^{m-i}-1}{2^{i+1}-1}$ (exponents in truncated natural-number subtraction). The proof shows that $P(m,j)$ is an integer for every $m$ and $j$, by induction on $m$ using a Pascal-type recurrence, and the last line applies this to $m=n$, $j=k$.

## No denominator is zero {steps: hD hD.h1 hDk}

`hD`: for every $j$, $2^{j+1}-1 \neq 0$, because $1 < 2^{j+1}$ (`hD.h1`). `hDk`: hence the product $\prod_{i<k}(2^{i+1}-1)$ of all the denominators is nonzero, for every $k$.

## The recurrence {steps: key key.hshift key.hDj key.hj key.s4 key.s4.e key.s5 key.s5.hz}

`key` states, for all $m, j$,
$$P(m+1, j+1) = P(m, j) + 2^{j+1}\, P(m, j+1),$$
the $q$-Pascal rule at $q=2$. The proof writes each $P$ as one product of numerators over one product of denominators, splits off the $i=0$ factor $2^{m+1}-1$ of the left numerator, and reindexes the rest with $m+1-(i+1) = m-i$ (`key.hshift`); it then splits off the last factor of the other products and records that the denominators are nonzero (`key.hDj`, `key.hj`). Two cases follow. If $j \le m$ (`key.s4`), it rewrites $2^{m+1} = 2^{m-j}\cdot 2^{j+1}$ (`key.s4.e`), clears denominators and checks the polynomial identity with `field_simp` and `ring`. If $j > m$ (`key.s5`), the numerator product $\prod_{i<j}(2^{m-i}-1)$ contains the factor $i=m$, which is $2^0-1=0$ (`key.s5.hz`), so every term is $0$ and `simp` closes it.

## Integrality by induction {steps: main main.s1 main.s1.s1 main.s1.s2 main.s2 main.s2.s1 main.s2.s2 main.s2.s2.s1 main.s2.s2.s2}

`main` states that for all $m, j$ some integer equals $P(m,j)$, proved by induction on $m$, with a case split on $j$ inside each case. For $m=0$ (`main.s1`): $P(0,0)$ is the empty product, the integer $1$ (`main.s1.s1`); for $j \ge 1$ the factor $i=0$ is $2^0-1 = 0$, so the integer is $0$ (`main.s1.s2`). For $m+1$ (`main.s2`): $j=0$ again gives $1$ (`main.s2.s1`); for $j+1$ (`main.s2.s2`) the induction hypothesis gives integers $a = P(m,j)$ (`main.s2.s2.s1`) and $b = P(m,j+1)$ (`main.s2.s2.s2`), and by `key` the integer $a + 2^{j+1} b$ equals $P(m+1,j+1)$. The theorem is `main n k`.
