For all natural numbers $n$ and $k$, there is an integer $z$ whose value in $\mathbb{Q}$ equals the product
$$\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} \frac{2^{\,n-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1},$$
computed in the rationals. The exponent $n-i$ is natural-number subtraction, which stops at $0$: when $i \ge n$ it is $0$ and that factor's numerator is $2^0-1=0$. Every denominator $2^{i+1}-1$ is at least $1$. So the theorem says this product is always an integer. For $k=0$ the product is empty and equals $1$; for $k>n$ the factor with $i=n$ is $0$, so the product is $0$. The file's doc comment calls the product the Gaussian binomial coefficient at $q=2$, written the same way as the products inside `Qc` in the hole erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3.
