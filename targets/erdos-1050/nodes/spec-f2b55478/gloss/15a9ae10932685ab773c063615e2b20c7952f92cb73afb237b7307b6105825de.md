For natural numbers $n < m$, the following identity holds in $\mathbb{Q}$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^j\, 2^{\lfloor j(j-1)/2\rfloor}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{2^{\,n-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{\,2n-j-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}\Bigr)}{2^{\,m-j}-1}
\;=\; \frac{2^{n\cdot n}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(2^{\,i+1}-1)\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{\,m-n-1-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}\Bigr)}{\prod_{j=0}^{n}(2^{\,m-j}-1)}.$$

Reading notes. All subtractions in exponents are natural-number subtraction, which stops at $0$. Because $j \le n < m$, the exponents $m-j$ are at least $1$, so no denominator on either side is $0$; likewise $n-i \ge 1$ and $2n-j-i \ge 1$ wherever they occur. The one place truncation matters is $m-n-1-i$ on the right: when $m \le 2n$ the factor with $i = m-n-1$ has exponent $0$ and equals $0$, so the right side, and hence the left, is $0$; when $m > 2n$ no exponent truncates. The numerator of the $j$-th term on the left is exactly $Q_c(n,j)$ as defined in the hole erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3 (a signed product of two Gaussian-binomial-shaped products at $q=2$), so the left side is $\sum_{j=0}^{n} Q_c(n,j)/(2^{m-j}-1)$.

The doc comment reads this as the coefficient of $x^m$ ($m>n$) in $Q_n(x) f(x)$, with $f(x)=\sum_{k\ge1} x^k/(2^k-1)$ and $Q_n$ the Padé denominator, vanishing for $n<m\le 2n$ and positive for $m>2n$; the theorem itself states only the identity above, and says nothing about power series or signs.
