The theorem fixes three functions by equations and then makes a claim about $n=5$ and $n=7$. For any $Q_c : \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}$, $Q_x : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}$ and $A_q : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}$ such that, for all $n, k$,
$$Q_c(n,k) = (-1)^k\, 2^{\lfloor k(k-1)/2\rfloor} \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{\,n-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1}\;\cdot\;\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{2^{\,2n-k-i}-1}{2^{\,i+1}-1},$$
$$Q_x(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} Q_c(n,k)\Bigl(\frac{3}{2^n}\Bigr)^{k},$$
$$A_q(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{Q_c(n,j)}{2^{\,k-j}-1}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{3}{2^n}\Bigr)^{k} + Q_x(n)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{3}{2^{\,j+1}-3},$$
and for $n = 5$ or $n = 7$, there is a natural number $d$ with $d > 0$, $d^2 \le 2^{3n\cdot n}$ (compared in the reals), and both $d\,Q_x(n)$ and $d\,A_q(n)$ integers (each equal in $\mathbb{Q}$ to some integer).

Reading notes. The exponents $n-i$, $2n-k-i$ and $k-1$ are natural-number subtraction, which stops at $0$; $k(k-1)/2$ is natural-number division (exact here, since $k(k-1)$ is even). In the inner sum of $A_q$ the term $j=k$ divides by $2^0-1=0$, and Lean's $x/0=0$ makes it $0$. These definitions are word for word the ones in the hole erdos-1050--h1-v2--h3, so this theorem is that hole's statement restricted to $n \in \{5, 7\}$.
