For natural numbers $n$ and $k$ with $k \le n$, and any sequence of integers $c_0, c_1, c_2, \dots$ (a function $c : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$), there is an integer $z$ whose value in $\mathbb{Q}$ equals
$$\Bigl(\prod_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor < m \le n} (2^m-1)\Bigr)\cdot \sum_{j=0}^{k} \frac{c_j}{2^{\,k-j}-1},$$
computed in the rationals. Here $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ is natural-number division (the Lean `n / 2`), so the product runs over the integers $m$ with $\lfloor n/2\rfloor < m \le n$. Since $j \le k$, $k-j$ is an ordinary difference. The last term, $j=k$, divides by $2^0-1=0$, and in Lean division by zero gives $0$, so that term contributes nothing: in effect the sum is over $0 \le j < k$, with denominators $2^{k-j}-1$ for $1 \le k-j \le k \le n$. In words: multiplying by $M_n = \prod_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor < m \le n}(2^m-1)$ clears every denominator of such a sum, whatever the integer coefficients.

The doc comment says why the authors expected it (each $d$ with $1 \le d \le n$ has a multiple in $(n/2, n]$, so $2^d-1$ divides some $2^m-1$ in the product) and says that the 2-adic half of its ingredient is not claimed here; the theorem itself makes no 2-adic statement.
