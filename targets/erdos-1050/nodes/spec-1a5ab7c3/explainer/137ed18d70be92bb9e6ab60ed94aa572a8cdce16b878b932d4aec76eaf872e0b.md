## Overview

Let $M = \prod_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor < i \le n}(2^i-1)$. The proof names the integer explicitly (lines 33–34): $z = \sum_{j=0}^{k} c_j \cdot \bigl(M \mathbin{/} (2^{k-j}-1)\bigr)$, where $M$ is computed in $\mathbb{Z}$ and $/$ is integer division. It then shows, term by term, that the $j$-th term of $z$ cast to $\mathbb{Q}$ equals $M \cdot c_j/(2^{k-j}-1)$ in $\mathbb{Q}$. The work is the divisibility fact `key`.

## Every 2^m - 1 with 1 ≤ m ≤ n divides M {steps: key key.ht1 key.hle key.hlt key.hlt.this key.hmem key.hmem.s1 key.hmem.s1.this key.hmem.s2 key.h1 key.h1.this}

`key`: for $1 \le m \le n$, $(2^m-1) \mid M$ in $\mathbb{Z}$. Put $t = \lfloor n/m \rfloor$. Then $t \ge 1$ (`key.ht1`), $mt \le n$ (`key.hle`) and $n < mt + m$ (`key.hlt`, from $n < m(t+1)$, `key.hlt.this`). So $mt$ lies in $(\lfloor n/2\rfloor, n]$ (`key.hmem`): the lower bound because $m \le mt$ (`key.hmem.s1.this`) together with $n < mt+m$ gives $n < 2mt$ (`key.hmem.s1`), the upper bound is `key.hle` (`key.hmem.s2`). Next, $2^m - 1$ divides $2^{mt}-1$ (`key.h1`), from $x-1 \mid x^t-1$ with $x = 2^m$ (`key.h1.this`, Mathlib's `sub_dvd_pow_sub_pow`). Since $mt$ is one of the indices of $M$, $2^{mt}-1$ is a factor of $M$, and divisibility follows.

## The term j = k {steps: s2}

When $k - j = 0$, the rational term divides by $2^0 - 1 = 0$ and is $0$; the integer term divides by $0$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, also $0$. `simp` closes it.

## The terms j < k {steps: s3 s3.s1 s3.hne s3.hneq s3.hP s3.hP.this}

Here $1 \le k-j \le k \le n$, so `key` gives $q$ with $M = (2^{k-j}-1)\,q$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ (`s3.s1`). The divisor is nonzero in $\mathbb{Z}$ (`s3.hne`) and in $\mathbb{Q}$ (`s3.hneq`), since $2^{k-j} \ge 2$. Casting the factorisation to $\mathbb{Q}$ gives $M = (2^{k-j}-1)q$ there too (`s3.hP`, via `s3.hP.this`). So the integer division is exact, the $j$-th integer term is $c_j q$, and `field_simp` checks $c_j q = M c_j/(2^{k-j}-1)$.
