## What is proved, and the plan

Fix $c = 8/3$ and write $z=\sum_{j\ge0}\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,j+1}\bigr)^{-1}$. For $n\ge1$ and $1\le k\le n$ write
$$D_k=\prod_{1\le l\le n,\ l\ne k}\bigl(1-2^{\,l-k}\bigr),\qquad A_k=-\frac{\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,t+k}\bigr)}{D_k},\qquad W(n)=(n-2)!\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(1-c\,2^k\bigr)\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}\bigl(1-2^k\bigr),$$
and $E(n)$ for the infinite sum on the right of the statement, so the statement asks for integers with $b(n)z-a(n)=9^nW(n)E(n)$ for $n\ge1$. Put
$$\beta_0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_k,\qquad S_1=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(1-c\,2^i\bigr)^{-1},\qquad S_2=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{B_i}{2^i-1},\quad B_i=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_k\,(c\,2^k)^{-i}.$$

The proof has four parts, then one line:

(1) `numerator_integral`: $9^nW(n)(S_1-S_2)$ is an integer $a(n)$.

(2) `hden`: $9^nW(n)\beta_0$ is an integer $b(n)$.

(3) `hpf`: a partial-fraction identity for the summand of $E(n)$.

(4) `hres`: summing it, $E(n)=\beta_0\,z-(S_1-S_2)$.

The final line takes these $a$ and $b$ and checks $b(n)z-a(n)=9^nW(n)E(n)$ by `linear_combination` from the three equations. The comments in the proof point to annexes 959e0d5b… and a03db62f… for the informal argument (Borwein's construction). The outline lists no graph node as used: this proof proves part 1 in full itself and does not call the node erdos-1050--h1-v2--h2--h1, although that node states the same fact and is listed as a dependency.

## Shared lemmas {steps: h0 hdvd hgeo hpoly hrec hsumZ hV hW}

These eight steps are stated once at the top and used by parts 1 and 2. With $D_s(k)=\prod_{l\in s,\,l\ne k}(1-2^{\,l-k})$ for a finite set $s$ of naturals:

`hsumZ`: a finite sum of terms that are each integers is an integer.

`h0`: $\sum_{k\in s}1/D_s(k)=1$ for nonempty $s$ (Mathlib's Lagrange basis for the nodes $2^{-k}$, evaluated at $0$).

`hrec`: for $a\in s$, $\sum_{k\in s}2^kf(k)/D_s(k)=\sum_{k\in s\setminus\{a\}}2^kf(k)/D_{s\setminus\{a\}}(k)+2^a\sum_{k\in s}f(k)/D_s(k)$.

`hW`: $\sum_{k\in s}(2^k)^e/D_s(k)$ is an integer for every $e\ge0$ (induction on $|s|+e$ using `h0` and `hrec`). Write $H_e$ for this sum with $s=\{1,\dots,n\}$.

`hV`: $\sum_{k\in s}(2^k)^{-(m+1)}/D_s(k)=0$ when $m+1<|s|$.

`hgeo`: $\sum_{i=1}^{k}2^{-im}=(1-2^{-km})/(2^m-1)$ for $m\ge1$.

`hdvd`: $2^i-1$ divides $\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}(1-2^k)$ for $1\le i\le n-1$ (it divides $2^{k_0}-1$ for the multiple $k_0=i\lfloor n/i\rfloor$ of $i$, which lies in that range).

`hpoly`: $\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}(1-2^ty)=\sum_{j<n}\alpha_jy^j$ with integers $\alpha_j$.

## Part 1: the numerator {steps: numerator_integral s10}

`numerator_integral` states exactly what the node erdos-1050--h1-v2--h2--h1 states, and is proved here by the same argument, whose own steps are `residue_integral`, `polypart_integral`, `head_integral` and `hmer` nested inside it:

$S_1$ is split by partial fractions in the parameter $c$ into a residue part $\sum_{i=1}^n b_i\,(1-c\,2^i)^{-1}$, with $b_i=\sum_{k=i}^nA_k|_{c\to2^{-i}}$, and a remainder. Each $b_i$ is an integer: every term of it is an integer, using that Gaussian binomial coefficients at $q=2$ are integers. The remainder times $3^n\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^n(1-2^k)$ is an integer, by `hW`, `hgeo` and `hdvd`.

$3^{\,n-1-i}B_i$ is an integer for $1\le i\le n-1$. Expanding $A_k$ with `hpoly`, the terms of negative degree vanish by `hV` and the others are multiples of the $H_e$. The factor $2^i-1$ is cancelled by `hdvd`.

`s10` unpacks the resulting sequence $a$.

## Part 2: the denominator {steps: hden s12}

`hden` produces integers $b(n)=9^nW(n)\beta_0$ for $n\ge1$. Its step `key` shows that $3^{\,n-1}\beta_0$ is an integer. Expanding the numerator of $A_k$ with `hpoly` gives
$$\beta_0=-\sum_{j<n}\alpha_j\,c^{\,j}H_j,$$
and $3^{\,n-1}c^{\,j}=3^{\,n-1-j}8^{\,j}$ for $j\le n-1$, so $3^{\,n-1}\beta_0=-\sum_j\alpha_j3^{\,n-1-j}8^jH_j$, an integer by `hW`. Then, writing $9^n=3\cdot3^{\,n-1}\cdot3^n$ and $3^n\prod_{k=1}^n(1-c\,2^k)=\prod_{k=1}^n(3-8\cdot2^k)$, the proof defines
$$b(n)=3\,(n-2)!\prod_{k=1}^n(3-8\cdot2^k)\prod_{k=\lceil n/2\rceil}^{n}(1-2^k)\cdot\bigl(3^{\,n-1}\beta_0\bigr)$$
for $n\ge1$ (and $0$ at $n=0$). `s12` unpacks it.

## Part 3: partial fractions at $x=2^m$ {steps: hpf}

`hpf`: for $n\ge1$ and $m\ge1$, the summand of $E(n)$ evaluated at $x=2^m$, that is
$$-\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,m+n}\bigr)^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigl(1-2^{\,k-m}\bigr)\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,k+m}\bigr)^{-1},$$
equals
$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_k\,\bigl(1-c\,2^{\,k+m}\bigr)^{-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}B_i\,(2^m)^{-i}.$$

The step `lag` is a general partial-fraction formula from Mathlib's Lagrange interpolation. For distinct nonzero $p_k$ ($k\in s$) a polynomial $Q$ of degree less than $|s|$, and $x$ with every $1-p_kx\ne0$,

$$Q(x)\prod_{k\in s}(1-p_kx)^{-1}=\sum_{k\in s}Q(1/p_k)\prod_{j\ne k}(1-p_j/p_k)^{-1}(1-p_kx)^{-1}.$$

It is applied with $p_k=c\,2^k$ for $k=1,\dots,n$ and $Q(X)=-\prod_{t=1}^{n-1}(X-2^t)$, which has degree $n-1$.

The left side is rewritten into this shape (the factor $(2^m)^{-(n-1)}$ comes from $1-2^{k-m}=2^{-m}(2^m-2^k)$).

The coefficients are matched with $A_k\,(c\,2^k)^{-(n-1)}$.

A finite geometric identity, $(1-u)^{-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}u^{-i}=u^{-N}(1-u)^{-1}$ with $u=c\,2^{k}2^{m}$ and $N=n-1$, splits each term into the two sums shown. The `calc` chain at the end of `hpf` strings these equalities together.

## Part 4: summing over $m$ {steps: hres}

`hres`: for $n\ge1$, $E(n)=\beta_0\,z-(S_1-S_2)$.

Re-indexing. The terms of $E(n)$ are the summand above at $m=n+j$, $j\ge0$. The terms at $m=1,\dots,n-1$ are $0$, because the factor $k=m$ of $\prod_k(1-2^{\,k-m})$ vanishes. So $E(n)$ is the sum of the summand over all $m\ge1$.

Summability. The proof establishes it for every piece: $(1-c\,2^{\,k+m+1})^{-1}$ is bounded in size by $(1/2)^m$, and $\sum_m (2^{m+1})^{-i}$ is geometric. The full summand is then summable because it equals, by `hpf`, a sum of summable pieces.

Summing term by term. The proof applies `hpf` for every $m\ge1$ and sums. On the first part it uses $\sum_{m\ge1}(1-c\,2^{\,k+m})^{-1}=z-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-c\,2^i)^{-1}$, the tail of $z$. On the second it uses $\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-mi}=1/(2^i-1)$ for $i\ge1$. The result is $\beta_0z-S_1+S_2$.

The proof then concludes with the single `linear_combination` described in the first section.
